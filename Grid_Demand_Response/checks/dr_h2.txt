CHOICES
  C1 TIER (drlib): during an offered event the job runs at throughput 1 - x for min(H, w) hours (a throttle, no checkpoint, own
     overhead 0); curtailed energy per event min(H, w) (1 - power(1 - x)) fleet-hours; wall delay x min(H, w); valued on the own
     clock with a wall-clock deadline. The 'average throughput reduction over a 3-6 hour period' is read as this throttle.
  C2 ETM (drlib): a full pause for the H-hour event plus R own hours without progress at full power; curtailed energy H
     fleet-hours per event; wall delay H + R; deadline counted in own steps (W + n R <= D).
  C3 job delay = the wall-clock completion delay (completion hour - W); 'matched job delay' = equal mean delay per job across a
     fleet of identical jobs, so a fleet can realise any delay between whole-event points by letting a fraction of its jobs
     take one more event: each arm's season frontier is the line from (0, 0) to its (n_use events) point.
  C4 n_use = the most events that keep the arm's contract deadline (at most K): the frontier is restricted to seasons in which
     the job meets its deadline under its own contract (ETM: own-step deadline; TIER: wall-clock deadline).
  C5 the best tier at delay Delta = the tier supplying the most energy at Delta among the tiers that can reach Delta (dmax >= Delta);
     ties go to the first tier in the order printed.
  C6 'within 1 %' = rel(E_ETM, E_best) <= 0.01 with the harness formula |a - b| / max(|a|, |b|), non-strict, exact rationals;
     Q holds iff this is true at every matched delay in (0, Delta_top] (Delta_top = the smaller of ETM's and the tiers' largest
     delay) in every cell; Q is not computable if some cell has no common delay range.
  C7 offers are live only while the job runs (drlib); every offer here precedes hour 1000, so every arm is running at every offer.
