# APP1 citation checks, family M3: privacy and security of continual learning, erasure, poisoning, and oversight regulation (2026-09-30; R10)

This is stage C of `Applied_Suite/APPLICATIONS_DECLARATION.md` (pushed at 4e78058), family **M3**. It covers:
- the risks of stored examples (replay buffers, membership inference, data retention);
- the right to erasure and machine unlearning (GDPR Art. 17; the 2025–26 unlearning and continual-unlearning literature);
- poisoning and backdoors in continual or online learning;
- oversight and interruptibility requirements in 2025–26 regulation and standards: the EU AI Act (Art. 12, 14, 15, 43(4),
  Annex XI) and its timeline after the 2026 Digital Omnibus, the NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, the FDA's predetermined change
  control plans, the EDPB's opinion on AI models, the EU Machinery Regulation and the Digital Services Act's Art. 38.

The dossier records sources and verified quotes. It is a note, not evidence (R8): it grades no application and quotes no
number from the ledger. Rows of the record are named by id only. The grading is `Applied_Suite/checks/applications.py`'s.
Readings of legal texts are information, not legal advice.

**How the sources were handled.** Every source was fetched on the day (2026-09-30) through the session proxy with curl
(TLS verified against the proxy CA bundle). There is one exception, the AI Act's Official Journal text (below).
- **arXiv:** the abstract page, for the current version and the version history; then the PDF of that version.
- **Regulators, standards bodies, vendors, NGOs:** the PDF or HTML as served.
- **The AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), Official Journal text:**
  - On 2026-09-30, EUR-Lex answered **HTTP 202 with an empty body** for every URL tried (see Not reached).
  - EPS3 fetched the OJ HTML from EUR-Lex on 2026-09-29 (`docs/citations/eps3_2026-09-29.md`, sha256 7b1622f3…). That
    file was copied into `m3/reused/`, re-hashed (unchanged) and re-extracted here.
  - The quotes of Art. 14 and Art. 15 were also checked against today's reproduction at artificialintelligenceact.eu (FLI).
    Two of the four match verbatim. The other two match fragment by fragment: the reproduction inserts glossary tooltip
    text ("Article 3(1)", "Article 3(29)") inside the sentences.
- **Where EUR-Lex was needed and no copy existed:** GDPR Art. 17, DSA Art. 38 and the Digital Omnibus are quoted from
  unofficial reproductions. The Machinery Regulation is quoted through a vendor's guide. Each claim names its source.

How the text was extracted:

**Cross-cutting (general):**
- **Timeline** (m3:35). The AI Act's high-risk requirements, including Art. 12, 14 and 15, apply from 2 December 2027
  (Annex III) and from 2 August 2028 (Annex I products) after Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, instead of 2 August 2026. The
  Omnibus does not amend Article 15, but its item 18 adds Article 42(3): a high-risk system that meets Regulation (EU)
  2024/2847's Article 12(1) conditions is deemed to comply with Article 15's cybersecurity requirements (m3:35, m3:36).
- **Learning after release** (m3:36, m3:37). The Act asks a system that keeps learning to control feedback loops and to
  resist data and model poisoning. It exempts pre-determined learning changes from "substantial modification".
- **ISO/IEC 42001** (m3:38) is a management-system standard. Its body was not read.
- **Attacks specific to continual learning** (m3:39, m3:40). Backdoors persist across continual learning algorithms, and the
  replay sampler alone is an attack surface.

None of K1–K7 in the record addresses feedback loops, poisoning, backdoors or erasure.
