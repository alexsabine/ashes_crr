- [G3 close] Pausing needs a checkpoint to keep progress, and the authors "treat checkpointing overhead as negligible"

- [G8 close] Accelerated interconnection is named first, then lower tariffs and flexibility payments. The authors also

- [G5 bears] The utility required a 15-minute ramp and no "snap back" above the pre-event baseline, so the rebound was
  constrained by contract.
- [G9 bears] Power capping (DVFS) is one of three knobs; its control overhead is "negligible".

## 6. Position by position (F1)

- **G1, AI training and data-centre load is already used for demand response in the field.**
  - States: Google 2025 and 2026 (1 GW in contracts with five utilities, by limiting or shifting ML workloads); I&M;
    TVA; the Phoenix trial (arXiv v1 and Nature Energy); the London trial (Williams et al.); DCFlex's nine sites.
  - Qualified: Li (2026) finds immediately eligible curtailment of only 6.35 % of median facility power on one
    production trace.
  - F1 supports the expected grade REDUNDANT.
- **G3, a lossless pause makes the job's curtailment cost about the restart overhead.**
  - Close: Colangelo (checkpoint overhead "negligible" for infrequent events on long jobs); Gao et al. (pause/resume
    overhead "seconds to minutes", small for long jobs); Williams et al. (resume "from saved states" "without
    compromising correctness"); Lechowicz (switching cost); Ren & Islam (switching cost as the payment floor).
  - Contradicts, as written for a revenue-bound fleet: Ji et al. price delay at lost job revenue ($600 to above
    $3000/MWh). Li assumes half a checkpoint interval of lost work per call and says checkpoint completion is not
    observed.
  - F1 supports PARTLY REDUNDANT, with a named qualifier: the job-level cost can be about R, but a capacity-bound fleet
    loses the curtailed GPU-hours whatever the deadline.
- **G4, a stop-the-clock contract (deadline extended by the curtailed time).**
  - **Not found in F1.** Every delay term found is fixed in advance and bounded:
    - Google's 24-hour completion window;
    - Emerald's Flex 0–3 throughput tiers and "customer-designated flexibility on completion time";

    - Lechowicz's fixed deadline T;

    - Li's contract terms, none of them a deadline.
  - The tiers are REDUNDANT: Colangelo, Williams, NVIDIA, and Utility Dive's pre-agreed parameters and "hard floors".
  - F1 supports the expected grade: NOT FOUND IN THE SWEEP (the extension); REDUNDANT (the tiers).
