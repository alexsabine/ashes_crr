rules, prices, notice times, durations, response times and non-delivery penalties of demand-response programmes in markets

source also bears on G4 (contract form), G5 (step changes, rebound), G6 (response time) or G8 (interconnection), that is

**Reading.**
- [G2 close] The price is the **provider's own bid** (a Utilisation Price in £/MWh per half-hour Settlement Period), ranked
  lowest first against NESO's requirement. A training job could bid its own cost of curtailment, which is what G2 says sets
  the payment it needs. The difference: the rules do not say what the bid should reflect, and acceptance is NESO's choice
  against its own alternatives (see the pricing pro-forma and the Market Guidance below).
- [G4 bears] The contract is per Settlement Period, all-or-nothing ("not Curtailable"), with no term that extends anything
  of the provider's (no deadline, no schedule). The Guaranteed Acceptance Price applies **only to DFS Tests**.
- [G6 bears] Timing: a requirement may be notified at any time in a day for windows within an 8-hour procurement period
  commencing from the submission deadline; results come 60 minutes after that deadline. These rules set an upper bound on
  the lead time and state no minimum notice between acceptance and the start of a window (tightened 2026-09-30: the earlier
  "of the order of hours, not minutes" is not in the quoted rules).
- [G5 bears] In DFS Tests (paragraph 10.4), NESO may allocate participants to different Settlement Periods of a window "to
  ensure that step changes in aggregate delivery ... do not exceed operational limits": the operator treats the aggregate
  step change of many units as subject to an operational limit (tightened 2026-09-30: "may", and tests only).
- [G3 bears, indirectly] Moving the curtailed demand to another meter point is forbidden ("deliberate displacement"): a job
  migrated to another site would not count as delivery; a reduction at the unit's own meters, such as a pause, would. This
  bears on the alternatives to a pause, not on G3's claim about the pause's cost (tightened 2026-09-30).
- Size limits: 0.1 MW to 100 MW per submission. Does not supply: prices (bids are private until published), penalties (in
  the Service Terms).

> 1.3 The submission by NESO of a DFS Acceptance shall create a legally binding obligation on the Service Provider to provide from the relevant DFS Unit, and for NESO to pay for, the applicable Service Volume upon the terms of these DFS Service Terms.

> A settlement value shall be established for each DFS Acceptance in accordance with the following formula:

(Re-typed: the formula that follows is S_ie = 0.5 · P_ie · Q_ie · K_ie, with S the settlement value in £, P the Utilisation
Price in £/MWh, Q the Service Volume in MW for a half-hour Settlement Period, K the performance factor; the extractor renders
it in mathematical-italic glyphs.)

> Kie = 1.2 if RVie ≥120 Kie = 0.01*RVie if 50 ≤ RVie < 120 Kie = 0.01*((2*RVie)-50) if 25 ≤ RVie < 50 Kie = 0 if 0 ≤ RVie < 25 Kie = 0.01*RVie if -100 ≤ RVie < 0 Kie = -1 if RVie < -100

> RVie = 100* (Vie / (Qie * 0.5))

> iii. persistent poor delivery of Service Volumes from a DFS Unit (which for these purposes shall be the calculation of a performance factor K which is less than 50 percent either for five (5) or more consecutive DFS Acceptances or for ten (10) or more DFS Acceptances taking place in any three (3) month period), then NESO may in its discretion:- (a) Deregister the relevant DFS Unit(s) or any of them; and/or (b) Deregister the Service Provider as a Registered DFS Participant or Registered Service Provider.

**Reading.**
- [G2 bears] Payment is energy-only: (bid price) × (contracted MWh) × K. There is no availability or reservation payment
  in DFS; a provider is paid only for delivered curtailment.
- **Non-delivery penalty (numbers for a model).** K = 1.2 at ≥ 120 % delivery (a cap), K = RV/100 between 50 and 120 %, a
  steeper linear reduction between 25 and 50 %, **K = 0 below 25 %**, and **K negative (down to −1) if the unit moves in
  the wrong direction** (RV < 0). Computed here from the re-typed formula: with K < 0 the settlement value is negative, a
  charge on the provider; the quoted text does not say so in words (tightened 2026-09-30). Persistent K < 50 % allows
  deregistration.
- [G7 bears] Per-event revenue is capped at 1.2 × bid × contracted MWh. Computed here: per delivered MWh a unit earns at
  most its own bid (K = RV/100 between 50 and 120 %, and K is capped at 1.2 above that) (tightened 2026-09-30: the earlier
  "at most its own bid" did not say per delivered MWh).

## 8. Quotes that contradict or qualify the declaration

- **§3's price field.** The declaration's first data source lists DFS "guaranteed acceptance prices". In current DFS rules a
  GAP exists **only for DFS Tests** (Procurement Rules 12.1 and the GAP definition), and the 2024/25 report says GAPs
  "neither were issued during the winter period". Live DFS events are pay-as-bid: the public price record is each bid's
  Utilisation Price with its accepted or rejected status. A rule "ETM participates whenever the price covers R/H" needs a
  price the arm faces; in DFS that is the arm's own bid, with acceptance decided by NESO.
- **Event lengths (H4: 0.5–4 h; H1–H8 event length H).** Some real events are longer (tightened 2026-09-30: was "Real
  events are longer"; ELRP events of 1–5 h and DFS windows of 30 minutes overlap the declared range): PJM 17 September 2026, 14:30–22:00 (7.5 h)
  for 30-minute resources; ERCOT ERS holds until recall, up to 24 cumulative hours per December–March contract period;
  CAISO RDRR (2014) at least 4 h sustained; ELRP up to 5 h. DFS windows are one or more half-hour Settlement Periods,
  minimum 30 minutes.
- **G6.** The response times found run from 15 cycles (ERCOT FFR, automatic mode) through 10 and 30 minutes (ERCOT ERS,
  FFR on instruction), 30 to 120 minutes (PJM) and 40 minutes (CAISO RDRR, 2014) to DFS's within-day procurement, for which
  no minimum notice is stated. If a lossless pause's save takes minutes (F4's number), it excludes only the fastest
  automatic products, and G6's bite is narrow; F2 alone cannot say so, since no F2 source gives a save time (tightened
  2026-09-30: was "The position holds as written, but its bite is narrow").
- **G7.** The capacity route (PJM Capacity Performance DR at $333.44/MW-day for 2027/2028) pays per MW far more than event
  payments, outside scarcity events, in exchange for unlimited interruptions and non-performance charges. The declaration's
  expected MIXED grade names scarcity events as the exception; F2 finds capacity payments as a second exception.
- **Non-delivery penalties.** DFS's performance factor turns negative when a unit moves the wrong way (K down to −1, the
  provider is charged, computed here from the settlement formula); PJM charges per interval of shortfall; ERCOT squares a low availability factor; ELRP has no penalty. The
  arms' payoffs in `dr_data.py` are declared without penalties; that is a model choice the sources qualify.
