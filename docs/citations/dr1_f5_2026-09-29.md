### FERC, order accepting SPP's Conditional High Impact Large Load Service (CHILLS), Docket ER26-1323

- **Identifier:** 195 FERC ¶ 61,196, "Order Accepting Tariff Revisions, Subject to Condition", issued June 5, 2026,
  Docket Nos. ER26-1323-000/-001 (Document Accession #20260605-3090), as posted by SPP at
  https://spp.org/documents/76880/20260605_order%20-%20revisions%20to%20add%20the%20conditional%20high%20impact%20large%20load%20service_er26-1323.pdf;
  fetched 2026-09-29.
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 f35a6491193b19c26df0382f27bd17c66668af392e21b8bd2fcbfa5c8df69ce7.
- **Local copy:** `spp_chills_order.pdf` (text `spp_chills_order.pdf.txt`)
- **Positions:** G8, G7.

> Southwest Power Pool, Inc. (SPP) submitted proposed revisions to its Open Access Transmission Tariff (Tariff) to add a new type of non-firm transmission service called Conditional High Impact Large Load Service (CHILLS).

> According to SPP, depending on the size, scope, and availability of materials, completing network upgrades often requires six years or more.

> SPP explains that it will provide CHILLS using the available transfer capability that is in excess of the capability necessary to serve other customers taking firm transmission service. SPP states that CHILLS will have a maximum term of seven years and a minimum term of one year.

> SPP states that CHILLS is subject to curtailment and interruption on a non-discriminatory basis when the transmission system is constrained or under emergency or other unforeseen conditions.

> (3) not register as a demand response resource; (4) not be a demand response load; (5) not be a critical load;

> For the third and fourth requirements, SPP states that transmission customers receiving CHILLS should not be eligible to participate in the market for demand response because they are receiving a reduced level of transmission service with a different rate structure and a different curtailment priority.

> SPP states that CHILLS Customers will be billed the zonal rate, which is the hourly rate applicable to non-firm point-to-point transmission service for the specific hour of the CHILLS usage, based upon the zone in which the CHILL is located.

**Reading.**
- [G8 states] A tariff accepted by FERC gives a large load transmission service now, on spare capacity, in exchange for
  curtailability, while network upgrades that "often" take "six years or more" are built. The currency is access and time.
- [G7 bears; qualifies G8's "not event payments"] A CHILLS customer may **not** register as a demand-response resource
  or load. Under this product the curtailment is the price of access and earns no event payment at all. The two values
  cannot be stacked here.
- The rate is the hourly non-firm point-to-point rate, charged on the curtailed limit when curtailed; the order does not
  state it as a discount.

## 7. Position by position (F5)

- **G7, demand-response payments are small against the compute cost of a training fleet.**
  - States: GridLab/Telos (market prices and traditional DR "unlikely to be sufficient ... due to high willingness to pay
    for electricity"); Relae ("the value of uninterrupted compute time far exceeds any available curtailment payment";
    "valuation asymmetry between energy and compute"); Duke (incentives "modest and insufficient"); Cottier et al. (energy
    2–6 % of development cost, the premise).
  - Numbers for the check: compute list prices ($55.04 per 8×H100 instance-hour on AWS; $49.24 on demand and $19.71 spot
    per HGX H100 node at CoreWeave; $3.49–6.16 per H100-hour at neo-clouds; index $2.500 neo-cloud and $7.430 hyperscaler
    per H100-hour on 2026-04-18); power (~10.2 kW max per DGX H100; 700 W TDP per H100 SXM); energy price (US industrial
    9.77 c/kWh, July 2026); DR revenue ($333.44/MW-day PJM capacity at the cap; a vendor's $60–80k per MW-year for
    flexible bitcoin load); scarcity price (ERCOT HCAP $5,000/MWh).
  - Contradicts, as the exception: Riot (August 2023, $31.7 million in credits in an ERCOT scarcity month, mostly resale
    of contracted power) and Duke's report of it. The load is bitcoin mining.
  - Qualifies: CHILLS forbids DR registration for the curtailable-access customer; PJM connect-and-manage and Texas SB6
    take curtailment unpaid as a condition of connection. Where flexibility buys access, it often earns no event payment.
  - F5 supports the expected grade MIXED (small, with exceptions in scarcity events), with the named qualifier that the
    exceptions reached are for bitcoin mining, not AI training.
- **G8, the main commercial value of flexible AI load is faster or larger interconnection, not event payments.**
  - States: FERC CHILLS order and EL26-67/EL26-71 ("this willingness to curtail would potentially allow them to obtain
    service more quickly"; "strong desire for faster interconnection"); Camus/encoord/Princeton (3–5 years faster); PG&E
    (18–24 months; T-Flex for data centres); GridLab/Telos (incentivise by avoided capital and accelerated
    interconnection); Duke.
  - Close: PJM connect and manage; RMI; Utility Dive (PG&E pilot, 2–10 MW); Lu & Xu; Khanal et al.
  - Qualifies: Ofgem's GB reform uses a commitment fee and milestones, not flexibility; Texas SB6 makes curtailability a
    condition, not a bargain. The value of speed is stated in time, never in dollars, in the sources reached.
  - F5 supports the expected grade REDUNDANT.
- **G2 (bearing only).** Opportunity cost is named as what a DR payment must cover (Duke; Relae), priced as GPU-hours
  lost times the cloud price for a regulation service (Jahanshahi et al.), and set aside in a planning model that prices a
  deferred compute MWh at $10 and an interruption at $0 (Khanal et al.). No source prices a contract at a training job's
  opportunity cost.
- **G3 (bearing only).** Compute is "non-storable" (Bandi & Su): the capacity-bound qualifier recorded in F1 is a property
  of the asset.
- **G6 (bearing only).** Texas SB6's procured service gives at least 24 hours' notice; PJM DR must now be available in
  all hours; Camus/encoord events last 4–16 h.
