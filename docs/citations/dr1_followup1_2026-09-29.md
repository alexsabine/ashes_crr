## 1. What the H7 point measures (scope)

### McDonald et al., "Great Power, Great Responsibility: Recommendations for Reducing Energy for Training Language Models"

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2205.09646 **v1** (the abstract page lists only [v1]); fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF (v1) sha256 2e800db94586dc976a6e3eafe2796c685411cbcce4fb27879ab767c8a44b0372 (identical to the F4
  copy); abstract page sha256 6e9de4aeae8473bfe436c24e4f0bfc17498ab6f6558aab218d921531c9f50dc3 (identical to the F4 copy).
- **Local copy:** `pdf_2205.09646v1.pdf` (text `pdf_2205.09646v1.pdf.txt`); `abs_2205.09646.html`.

> Currently, there are two vendor-provided utilities to monitor resource consumption on NVIDIA GPUs.

> Broadly, these tools enable monitoring of GPU usage on a node and the collection of metrics on Streaming Multi-processor (SM) utilization, GPU memory footprint, power draw, GPU temperatures, PCI Express (PCIe) bandwidth, and several other hardware settings.

> On our system, this data is collected on every node and every GPU assigned to a job.

> A series of experiments is presented here that use energy tracking tools to measure the reduction in energy consumption provided by power-capping GPUs.

> Each model was trained on 16 V100 GPUs using four different power caps: 100 watts (W), 150W, 200W and 250W (the default power limit for an NVIDIA V100 GPU on our system).

> Averaging across each choice of conﬁguration, a 150W bound on power utilization led to an average 13.7% decrease in energy usage and 6.8% increase in training time compared to the default maximum.

**Reading.** The monitoring tools the paper names are the two NVIDIA GPU utilities (DCGM and nvidia-smi; the sentence
between the first two quotes names them), and the metrics they collect are GPU metrics, power draw among them, per GPU
assigned to a job. The energy figures (the last quote, which H7 uses as MCD-150) are therefore read as **GPU energy**: the
text states no CPU, node, network or facility energy in these experiments. The paper does not say in so many words that
the energy is GPU-only; that is the investigator's reading of the measurement it describes. The cap is on V100s (default
250 W), so the point is a GPU-level V100 point.

maxima, and a node's non-GPU draw at full GPU load is not quoted.)

## 3. Context only: facility overhead (PUE), from the same McDonald paper

> A highly efﬁcient datacenter will have a PUE close to 1, such that the facility energy overhead is minimal, while the global average for PUE is 1.59 (Ascierto and Lawrence, 2020).

> A PUE of 1.59 indicates that nearly 40% of a datacenter’s energy usage is consumed by facility energy.

> For instance the average PUE in January is 1.05 while in July it is 1.49, a 42% difference.

**Reading.** A facility draws more than its IT load (PUE above 1). Whether that overhead falls when the GPUs are capped
is not stated: cooling that tracks the heat load would fall with it (the GPU share of facility power is then unchanged),
a fixed overhead would not (the share is then the node share divided by PUE). The follow-up prints both readings as
context and scores neither. The paper's 1.59 is its citation of Ascierto and Lawrence (2020), not a measurement of its own;
1.05 and 1.49 are its own datacenter's monthly averages for 2020.
