## 1. The 2022/23 review: what a live event is, and its prices

### NESO (as ESO), DFS winter 2022/23 review (published 30 August 2023)

- **Identifier:** https://www.neso.energy/document/287006/download (as in the F2 dossier); fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 7b4892d5440740260bb1d92478b2d41a4bfcce7fd4f85909742e0bb19d205b3f (identical to the F2 copy).
- **Local copy:** `neso_dfs_winter2223_review.pdf` (text `neso_dfs_winter2223_review.pdf.txt`).

> A live event could be triggered when insufficient upwards flexibility was foreseen at the day ahead stage and we believed the inadequacy could not be solved by our existing services and market incentives.

> There were two live activations of DFS over winter 2022/23. These took place on 23 and 24 January 2023

> Prices higher than the GAP were rejected for test events

> For live events, the average price for demand reduction was £4,559/MWh and the highest accepted price was £6,500/MWh, although a sizeable portion of participants were accepted at £3,000/MWh (see Figure 8).

**Reading.**
- A live event is the scarcity (margin) activation; a test event is not. The two live events were on 23 and 24 January 2023
  (in the DFS records: the two events typed Live in W2022/23 start on 2023-01-23 and 2023-01-24 (data)).
- Test prices were capped at the GAP (£3,000/MWh); live prices averaged £4,559/MWh with a maximum of £6,500/MWh, and "a
  sizeable portion" of live acceptances were at £3,000/MWh. So a live event's volume-weighted price can sit below £4,559
  (in the DFS records the 23 January live event's mean accepted price is below it (data)).

## 2. The 2023/24 review: two more live (margin) events

### NESO (as ESO), DFS Winter 23/24 End of Year Report (PDF created 12 June 2024)

- **Identifier:** https://www.neso.energy/document/319911/download (as in the F2 dossier); fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 43fe1c242fec2cef5ca4c75e7141a8582991fc6775e7b466fca524522fe3030e (identical to the F2 copy).
- **Local copy:** `neso_dfs_winter2324_review.pdf` (text `neso_dfs_winter2324_review.pdf.txt`).

> A total of 14 test events along with 2 live events were run over the winter.

> This team conducted assessments at the day ahead stage to identify whether available supply was able to meet the forecasted demand and positive margin required, which on two occasions led to live events being run.

> As detailed within winter order of actions, market-based solutions alone would not be able to meet reserve requirements and so enhanced actions were deemed necessary.

> Cold weather led to high demand (top 5% Winter Outlook range).

> Low wind output (lowest 2-5% of Winter Outlook range).

> 29/11/2023 17:00 day-ahead 449 332.4 -26.0 £776,213

> 01/12/2023 16:30 day-ahead 452 298.8 -33.9 £746,458

**Reading.**
- W2023/24 had two live events, run because the day-ahead margin could not be met by market-based solutions, in cold,
  low-wind conditions: the same kind of activation as 2022/23's live events. By the sources' own description they are
  scarcity events, not ordinary ones.
- The first rows of Table 6 ("Live Events Data") date them 29 November 2023 17:00 and 1 December 2023 16:30 (in the DFS
  records: the two events typed Live in W2023/24 start at those times (data)).
- Nothing in either review calls the whole 2022/23 season, or its test events, a scarcity exception. DR1's C5 attribution
  ("the season the F2 sources mark as the scarcity exception") is therefore not supported by these sources or by the F2
  dossier's tightened reading.
