- **The H0 direction is stated by two developers.** OpenAI's Model Spec forbids pursuing "time-on-site or click-through

  - OpenAI's clause bars only time-on-site "that is not user beneficial", and allows such factors "only insofar as doing so
    is strictly instrumental to following the chain of command".
  - Anthropic's standard is the person's "genuine interest", reliance "a person would endorse on reflection", and
    "long-term flourishing".
  - Both state an objective of user benefit, which is the declaration's TRUE (welfare), not H0 (task completion only).
    In V1's model the two pick the same policy, because a retention turn is worth nothing to the task and costs a. The row

  - De Freitas et al. find retention tactics at the moment of exit in 37 % of farewells across the most-downloaded

  - So the no-engagement objective is the recommendation of two developers' published specs and of the safety
    researchers. It is not the only practice in the domain. WALL-like engagement objectives are documented in deployment.

- **Scope: the measured retention behaviour is in companion and social chatbots, not task assistants (flagged).**

  - No fetched source measures retention turns by a task assistant with project memory. The declared a = 0.2 and the

- **Whether retention turns bring the user back sooner is contested (qualifies the model).**

  - The EU AI Act prohibits manipulative or deceptive techniques only where they cause or are reasonably likely to cause

> The objective is to minimize the costs of eventually misclassified jobs. While classical learning to defer frameworks assume fixed-cost and immediate human feedback, our model introduces congestion in the human review system.

> To capture the impact of limited human capacity, we assume that jobs that are admitted (deferred) by the AI wait in a review queue.

> On the other hand, admitting a job leads to delay loss, which is the increase in congestion for future jobs due to the limited human capacity.

> We then admit a job if (a weighted version of) its idiosyncrasy loss (which one can avoid by admission) exceeds the estimated delay loss (which occurs due to the admission).

> Remark 1. Our model assumes that all jobs eventually reviewed by humans incur zero loss. **Reading (V2).**
- **The H0 rule is the domain's Bayes rule.** Mozannar & Sontag's Proposition 2 defers exactly when the classifier's best
  accuracy on x is at most the expert's probability of being right on x. In the declaration's terms that is "defer iff
  h ≥ a", with a the AI's per-case accuracy and h the human's.
  - The paper's sentence says "higher" (strict), while equation (6) uses "≤", so ties go to deferral in the equation.
  - Under a ~ U[0.5, 1] the tie a = h has probability zero, so "defer iff h > a" is the same rule almost surely.
  - Madras et al. frame the same point: the decision "should depend not only on the model’s conﬁdence, but also on the
    decision-maker’s expertise".
- **"With no time term" is the special case of zero deferral cost; the domain's own rule has a cost term (qualifies H0 and
  the WALL reading).**
  - Mozannar & Sontag allow "a constant additive cost function c(x) for querying the expert", "easily integrated". They
    note that rejection learning with a constant cost c predicts "only if one is 1 −c conﬁdent".
  - Madras et al.'s loss carries a per-deferral penalty γ_defer.
  - Mozannar & Sontag's clinical experiment constrains coverage because "there might be a cost associated to querying a
    radiologist".
  - Applying Proposition 2 to l_exp = I[m ≠ y] + c gives "defer iff h − c ≥ a". This line is our derivation, not a sentence
    in the paper.
  - WALL, which charges a deferral the L steps it costs, is that cost-sensitive rule with c set by the lost throughput. It
    defers less as L grows, which is what the domain prescribes when deferral costs something.
  - So the domain does not call WALL's behaviour an error. It is an error only under the declaration's TRUE (errors per
    case, no charge for time). The row should say that WALL is the domain's cost-of-deferral rule under a throughput
    objective, not a mistake the domain would recognise. Both arms reduce to the domain, which supports REDUNDANT-DOMAIN.
- **The domain's handoff does not stop the AI (flagged; the declared model differs).**
  - Lykouris & Weng state that "classical learning to defer frameworks assume fixed-cost and immediate human feedback".
    Their model adds congestion instead: deferred jobs "wait in a review queue" while new jobs keep arriving and being
    classified.
  - The cost of a deferral there is a "delay loss", "the increase in congestion for future jobs". A job is admitted only
    if its avoidable loss "exceeds the estimated delay loss".
  - Waiting also harms the principal directly: "a policy-violating post may get user views before a reviewer removes this
    post" (a holding cost).
  - The declaration's handoff, during which "the AI processes nothing" for L steps, has no counterpart in the fetched
    sources.
  - In the queueing literature the time a deferred case waits is a real loss to the principal, not only an artefact of
    the agent's valuation. That qualifies the declaration's expectation that the handoff "take[s] something from [WALL's]
    valuation" as a defect of WALL: when review is congested, the domain adds a time term on purpose.
- **Human capacity is scarce in practice (context; qualifies the unlimited human in the model).** The Meta figures cited by
  Lykouris & Weng imply "a review ratio of around 0.2% to 1%". Raghu et al. treat human effort, including "time spent
  collectively by doctors", as a budget B. V2's human (h = 0.9 on every case, always available after L steps) has no
  capacity limit, so the H0 rule's deferral share is not constrained as it would be in the settings the sources describe.
- **What the sources do not supply.** No value for h, no distribution of a, no handoff length L, no discount γ. The
  G-NEG world (h = 0.5) is the declaration's own.
