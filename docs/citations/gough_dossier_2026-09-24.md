## 1. What Gough proposes

### 1a. The base theory (Smolin's claims, not Gough's)

- Wheeler and DeWitt had suggested that a black-hole singularity "bounces" into a new universe. Smolin added small, heritable variation in the parameters of physics between parent and child universes. Universes that make more black holes then leave more offspring. Smolin published this in 1992 ("Did the Universe Evolve?") and in the book *The Life of the Cosmos* (1997).
- **Smolin's "master prediction"** (hep-th/0612185, v1 dated 2006-12-18): "almost no change in the parameters of the standard model from the present values will increase the numbers of black holes produced."
- The same paper lists three published predictions:
  1. the maximum neutron-star mass is about 1.6 M☉ (kaon-condensate stars);
  2. if inflation happened, it was single-parameter inflation;
  3. "little early star formation", meaning few supernovae at high redshift. Smolin says CNS could be wrong if massive stars can form without carbon chemistry at high z.
- In a footnote Smolin says the fine-tuning at issue "is the existence of carbon chemistry and stars, and not intelligent life, per se." **Smolin's CNS does not select for life.**

### 1b. Gough's extensions (his own claims)

**"Three-stage cosmological natural selection"**, which he also calls "Eggiverse theory" or "Evolutionary Cosmology". It is set out most fully in *The Blowtorch Theory* (2025-03-19). Gough gives this timeline of the idea:

> 1990s: Smolin's … single-stage model … 2000s: Vidal, Smart, Price and Kane expanded this to a two-stage model, adding technologically-produced small black holes … 2020s: I develop the three-stage model, incorporating direct-collapse supermassive black holes.

"Kane" is probably Louis Crane, whom Gough names elsewhere **[unverified that it is a typo]**. Smolin's 2006 paper cites earlier work in this direction: E. R. Harrison, "The natural selection of universes containing intelligent life", *QJRAS* 36 (1995). Smolin lists it among "attempted refutations". Crane's first paper on the idea is from 1994 **[date from memory, not re-fetched]**.

The three stages, as Gough describes them:

1. **Direct-collapse supermassive black holes (SMBHs).** "Right at the start, in a phase transition shortly after the big bang, a relatively small number of supermassive black holes quickly form by direct collapse. (Very roughly a trillion …)." He calls this "the original reproductive mechanism for universes, conserved by evolution."
2. **Stellar-mass black holes.** The SMBHs shape the remaining gas into galaxies, and the galaxies' stars yield "far more black holes – offspring – per unit mass."
3. **Technologically produced small black holes.** Life evolves, then technology, and intelligent species build "vast quantities of extremely small black holes for energy production." Gough puts the optimal mass at about that of Mount Everest (10¹⁴–10¹⁵ kg, citing Crane). Such life is therefore "intensely selected for, and strongly conserved."

His slogan for stage 3: "Biology is the first half of the periodic table coming to life. Technology is the second half."

**The "key implication"** (post of 2025-10-29; the idea dates to June–July 2022). The first universes must have reproduced in the simplest possible way: primitive gas collapsing directly into a few very large black holes. Evolution is conservative, so in our universe direct-collapse SMBHs should form first, "from smooth gas … before galaxy formation", and should not depend on stars.

Gough uses this as a filter he calls "is it evolutionarily plausible?". It rules out SMBH seeds from Population III stars, from mergers, and from primordial black holes. He credits Bromm, Loeb, Natarajan, Pacucci and Ferrara with the direct-collapse physics itself. What he claims as new is the evolutionary argument that picks it out.

**What differs from Smolin (Gough's framing):**
- Life and intelligence are selected for.
- SMBHs come first and are what build galaxies.
- Galaxy-scale features are read as *functional organs*: quasar jets make carbon and trigger star formation; spiral galaxies ration gas; elliptical galaxies are vestigial stage-2 forms.
- Very speculative, and flagged as such by Gough: the tau and muon are "fossil particles" from stages 1 and 2; much of life may be on free-floating planets ("stanets") and their moons ("ploons") (post of 2024-12-31).

**Blowtorch Theory (2025-03-19):** early SMBH jets "actively carved the large-scale structure" (voids and filaments). A follow-on spiral-galaxy model was posted for feedback on 2026-05-29.

**Evolution and development.** Gough keeps these apart:
- *Evolution* happens between universes: "our universe is descended from earlier, simpler universes."
- *Development* happens within one universe's lifetime, and is "far more like the development of an evolved organism … than … dead matter obeying eternal laws."
- "Our universe behaves like an egg, not a rock. Developing, not decaying." (2022-06-02)

**No carrying capacity.** A universe "is both organism AND environment." A billion baby universes can all thrive, so selection is "pure" reproductive competition with no competition for resources (the mouse analogy, 2022-06-02).

**Energy.** A new universe costs roughly nothing, because positive mass-energy and negative gravitational energy cancel in a flat universe. Gough gives this as the answer to "don't universes shrink each generation?"

**Mechanism of inheritance.** Gough says it is unknown and "nobody has looked". He compares this to Darwin, who worked without knowing about DNA.

## 2. Testable and quantitative claims, and who has responded

### 2a. Predictions made before the data (all dates checked against the post metadata)

| Date | Post | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| 2022-07-08 (JWST first images 2022-07-12) | "Predictions! What the James Webb Space Telescope will see (and not see)…" | SMBHs form by direct collapse just after recombination. That era is over "at the absolute latest … within 300 million years", and he suspects much earlier. Seeds are narrowly sized, ≥ ~10⁵ M☉, "not a random distribution". Quasars switch on before stars and make the first carbon. There is no era dominated by Population III stars. "At least an order of magnitude fewer" small early galaxies than ΛCDM predicts. "Loads of" galaxies within the first 100 Myr. **HD-1, CR7 and GN-z11 will prove to be AGN.** Ellipticals and spirals form by different mechanisms. |
| 2022-07-11 | "Prediction: Life will turn out to be everywhere (after a certain point)" | "Trillions of exoplanets" will host life, and JWST "will soon start to find some, nearby". Photosynthesis-like and ATP-like chemistry will be common. Life will not appear in the first few billion years. |
| 2023-06-28 (the eve of the pulsar-timing-array announcements) | "Last-minute gravitational wave predictions" | A gravitational-wave background "stronger than expected", with mergers "much earlier than was anticipated", from "the direct collapse of a couple of hundred million supermassive black holes" inside the first 50 Myr. |
| 2025-03-19 | Blowtorch Theory | About a trillion direct-collapse SMBHs within the first 100 Myr ("almost certainly inside the first fifty million"), born spinning near the Kerr limit. Further predictions: a "popcorn signature" in the gravitational-wave background; a CMB–SMBH statistical link; clustered quasar ignition; early ring starbursts around AGN; coherent, helical magnetic fields along filaments; void "skins" and "seams" visible in Faraday rotation; cosmic rays guided by filaments; near-step-function void edges; SMBH mass correlating with the size of the adjacent void. He says it "potentially solves" the satellite, cusp/core, rotation-curve and Hubble-tension problems, without working them out. |

**Other numbers he uses:**
- About 4×10¹⁹ stellar-mass black holes in the observable universe (citing Sicilia, Lapi et al. 2022) and about 10¹² SMBHs.
- Black holes convert "up to 42%" of infalling mass to energy, against about 0.1% for fission and about 0.7% for fusion.
- The 27 (or 25) "basic parameters of matter" are tuned.

**Where the claims shift between posts:**
- The number of first-wave SMBHs goes from "a couple of hundred million" (2023) to "roughly a trillion" (2025).
- The formation window goes from "≤ 300 Myr" (2022) to "first 50–100 Myr" (2025).

**Fine-tuning** (2024-06-17, an email to the philosopher Philip Goff replying to Goff's *Scientific American* essay). Random multiverses fall to the gambler's fallacy. An evolving lineage of universes does not, because slight inherited variation makes complexity more likely with each generation.

**Fermi paradox** (2025-01-21). If civilisations run on Everest-mass black holes, their best power source is far too heavy to accelerate. That may "anchor" them to their home systems. Gough offers this "not … as an answer" but as a contribution.

Dossier note, not from any source: the posts do not discuss how you would feed a black hole with a Schwarzschild radius of about 10⁻¹³ m.

### 2b. Gough's own assessment of these predictions

- He calls the 2022 predictions "successful" and says they were confirmed. He cites:
  - the Little Red Dots, via Kocevski et al. and Matthee, Naidu et al. 2024 ("a resounding triumph", 2025-01-25);
  - UHZ1 (Natarajan, Pacucci et al. 2023);
  - a dormant overmassive black hole (Juodžbalis, Maiolino et al. 2024);
  - QSO1, a "naked" black hole (*Quanta*, 2025-09-12).
- On the gravitational-wave prediction (2023-08-18) he says the main prediction came out right, and concedes it "was not particularly brave".
- Wikipedia states that the JWST predictions were "subsequently borne out by the data". That sentence rests on Gough's own post and an *Irish Times* piece.

**[Not independently verified]:**
- whether HD-1 and CR7 have been shown to be AGN;
- the GN-z11 AGN result, which I recall as Maiolino et al. 2024 but did not re-fetch;
- whether "life everywhere" has been tested at all.

The reading of the Little Red Dots as AGN is still debated. *Smithsonian* links a separate article on "black hole stars" as a possible new kind of object. I found no peer-reviewed paper that assesses Gough's predictions as predictions.

**Open question for the reader.** Smolin's third prediction (2006) is that early star formation is limited. Gough predicts very early, very rapid galaxy formation, with quasar-made carbon enabling star formation. Neither author addresses the other on this point in any text I read.

## 3. Metaphysical and philosophical framing

**Against reductionism.** Gough's introduction (2023-01-11) starts with the burning of Giordano Bruno. He argues that Galileo's purely quantitative language "deliberately left out qualities … consciousness", so science "cannot extract meaning" from its data. His proposed remedy is reasoning by analogy (Hofstadter), synthesis across disciplines, and "the techniques of art". He cites Laughlin, Anderson's "More is Different", Koestler on Kepler, and Goff's *Galileo's Error*. Later he calls this approach "jazz science" (2026-05-29).

**Egg versus rock.** "The movement of energy through the system seems to organise the system, rather than disorder it" (About page). He accepts the second law of thermodynamics overall and argues that complexity rises locally (2022-06-02).

**Function without a designer.** He insists the theory needs "nothing metaphysical, nothing woo woo … no God standing outside the universe … just evolution" (2022-06-02). Yet his prose is steadily functional in the way biologists use the word: jets and SMBHs are "vital organs"; he asks "what those helical jet fields … are FOR"; Population III stars are "spark plugs". The teleology is meant as selection-based, the "as-if" purpose of evolved traits. It is not intentional design.

**Nested evolutions.** Technological evolution sits inside cultural evolution, which sits inside DNA evolution, then chemical evolution, then universe-level evolution. That chain reaches "back into the infinitely distant past". "Evolution evolved." He suggests the hierarchy of the sciences reflects successive eras in the evolution of universes.

**Life and mind.** Humans are data about the universe's present state ("We are the only data point we have for the precise present state of the universe"). Our universe is "coming to know itself through us; act on itself through us."

**Time.** Two timescales: generations of universes (evolution) and a single universe's lifetime (development). He notes, somewhat jokingly, that there is "no universal 'now'" (2022-06-02). He treats observing the early universe as seeing an embryo's body plan being laid down "as early as possible" (2025-07-02).

**Sources he names:** Peirce ("the only possible way of accounting for the laws of nature … is to suppose them results of evolution"), process and systems biology (Dupré, Nicholson, Noble, Kauffman), and Whitehead.

**The End Poem.** Wikipedia reports that the line "The Universe loves you because you are love" prompted his December 2022 essay. In it he put the End Poem into the public domain (post "I wrote a story for a friend", 2022-12-07).
