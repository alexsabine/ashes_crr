## 1. Taheri-Araghi et al. 2015, "Cell-size control and homeostasis in bacteria", Curr Biol 25(3):385–391

- **Version.** PMC author manuscript PMC4323405, "available in PMC: 2016 Feb 2". Published in final edited form on
  2014 Dec 24.
- **DOI.** 10.1016/j.cub.2014.12.009 (PMID 25544609).
- **URL.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4323405/
- **Fetch status.** HTTP 200. The Europe PMC fullTextXML endpoint returned 500 for this ID.
- **Raw file.** `taheri_araghi_2015_PMC4323405.txt`

> Our combined experimental results and quantitative analysis demonstrate that cells add a constant volume each generation irrespective of their newborn sizes, conclusively supporting the so-called constant Δ model.

> The collapse of the conditional distributions in Fig. 2 establishes the constant Δ model, or “adder” (as opposed to timer or sizer).

> Ordering the variances of the rescaled distributions, the distribution of the septum position s1/2 is the smallest, and the added size Δ is the largest (Fig. 4A). Previously, sizer was supported because the coefficient of variance for division size (10%) was smaller than that for generation time (40%-60%) [19].

> E. coli growth experiments were performed in seven different nutrient conditions. The average generation time in these conditions evenly spans from 17.1 to 51.4 minutes at 37°C.

> The frequency of the time-lapse imaging was chosen such that about 20 or more images were taken per generation time.

- **Bears on CD-1.** The adder is the domain's own result: a constant added size, independent of birth size. The paper gives
  no CV of its own for added size against generation time. The 10 % against 40–60 % figures are quoted from its ref. [19].
  The paper states that Δ has the widest rescaled distribution of the six it shows.
- **Bears on CD-3.** Nothing. The imaging was phase contrast only, so there are no initiation times.

## 2. Si et al. 2019, "Mechanistic origin of cell-size control and homeostasis in bacteria", Curr Biol 29(11):1760–1770.e7

- **Version.** PMC author manuscript PMC6548602, "available in PMC: 2020 Jun 3".
- **DOI.** 10.1016/j.cub.2019.04.062 (PMID 31104932).
- **URL.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6548602/
- **Fetch status.** HTTP 200. Europe PMC XML returned 500.
- **Raw file.** `si_2019_PMC6548602.txt`

> Parallel to the “division adder” [2, 3], the “initiation adder” is characterized by the addition of a nearly constant size per origin between consecutive replication cycles.

> Observation of wild type cells growing at steady state indicated the presence of two types of adder in E. coli: one functioning at division (hereafter a “division adder”) and the other at replication initiation (an “initiation adder”) (Figure 1).

> We implemented the Helmstetter-Cooper model [35, 36] that is often interpreted to mean that initiation triggers division after a fixed elapsed time τcyc = C + D (Figures 1A and 2A) [17, 37].

> To our surprise, and counter to the co-regulation hypothesis [17], the division adder remained intact even when the initiation adder no longer held by periodic induction of DnaA expression (Figure 3B).

> We used a functional fluorescently-labeled replisome protein (DnaN-YPet) to image replication cycles, and a microfluidic mother machine to follow continuous lineages during steady-state growth [3, 26]

> Imaging frequencies were calibrated at about 20 frames per doubling time such that no physiological effects on the cells were discernible.

> We provide a dataset of single-cell growth and cell cycle as Data S1. We also would like to share all other data upon request.

> This dataset includes physiological parameters such as cell size, growth rate and timing of cell cycle measured from steady-state growth for different strains and nutrient conditions.

- **Bears on CD-3.** This is the domain rule named in CD-3's second baseline: a constant added size per origin between
  initiations (the initiation adder). The paper also finds the division adder and the initiation adder independent under
  perturbation.
- **Bears on CD-1.** It restates the division adder.

> Instead, the most parsimonious model consistent with our data is a double-adder model in which the cell cycle commences at initiation of replication and both the subsequent division and the next initiation of replication are controlled by the added volume.

> The double-adder model postulates that <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic> cell cycle is orchestrated by two independent adders, one for replication and one for division, reset at replication initiation.

> Just as a constant average cell size can be accomplished by adding a constant volume per division cycle rather than by dividing at a critical division volume, so a constant average volume per origin of replication can also be implemented by controlling the added volume between replication initiations rather than by a critical initiation volume.

> However, as can be seen in Figure 2A, we observe that the initiation length Li and birth length Lb are clearly correlated in all conditions, rejecting the initiation mass model.

This of course only offers a proxy for initiation timing, as origin splitting can be affected by a cohesion time (Reyes-Lamothe et al., 2008).

> After loading, bacteria were incubated during 2 hr before starting image acquisition. Every 3 min, phase contrast and fluorescence images were acquired for several well-separated positions in parallel.

- **About the table rows.** They are from Appendix 1—table 1. The columns are Discarded %, # cell cycles, 1/λ [min],
  Adder r, and two more. "Glycerol auto" is the automatically tracked analysis of the glycerol experiment. "Glycerol" is the
  manually tracked one.

- **Bears on CD-3.** It gives the double adder, and the initiation adder per origin as the replication-cycle rule (CD-3's

  span single and overlapping replication rounds.
- **Bears on CD-1.** It restates the division-level adder.

- **DOI.** 10.1038/2191077a0 (PMID 4876941).

> ROUNDS of DNA replication are initiated in Escherichia coli at different stages in the cell cycle of bacteria growing at different rates1. It is possible to calculate that the initiation of a round of DNA replication always takes place at a time when the cell mass/chromosome origin reaches a particular critical value.

- **Bears on CD-3.** In this abstract, initiation falls at different stages of the cell cycle at different growth rates,

### 5c. Jun et al. 2018, "Fundamental principles in bacterial physiology — history, recent progress, and the future with focus on cell size control: a review", Rep Prog Phys 81(5):056601

- **Version.** PMC author manuscript PMC5897229, "available in PMC: 2019 May 1". Published 2018 Jan 9.
- **DOI.** 10.1088/1361-6633/aaa628 (PMID 29313526).
- **URL.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5897229/
- **Fetch status.** HTTP 200. Europe PMC XML returned 500.
- **Raw file.** `jun_2018_PMC5897229.txt`

> During rapid growth (i.e., doubling times below 60 minutes), Helmstetter & Cooper observed regular, periodic changes in the DNA synthesis rate during the growth cycle; however, the age of the bacterium when the initiation event occurs exhibits puzzling discontinuities – jumping from 0 to 1 at doubling times of about 30 minutes

> They found that under conditions of rapid growth imposed by the quality of the nutrient environment, the C period is about 40 minutes and the D period is about 20 minutes, irrespective of growth rate.

> For example, if the inter-division time is 100 minutes and the cell cycle time is τcyc = 60 minutes, then the generations before division that DNA replication is initiated is −0.4, or 0.4 of a generation (i.e., 0.4 × 100 minutes = 40 minutes) after the cell is born in the current generation.

> Another recent study by Wallden et al. revealed the invariance of the initiation mass S0 at the single-cell level under nutrient-mediated slow growth [631]. Under these growth conditions, the cell cycle time τcyc changes in direct proportion to the doubling time τd at both the single-cell and population level.

> Based on the observation that the CV of division size is smaller than the CV of generation time, Koch further correctly predicted a negative correlation of the generation time between mothers and daughters, and a positive correlation between sisters.

- **Bears on CD-3.**
  - Under Cooper–Helmstetter, the age (cycle fraction) at initiation depends on τcyc/τd, and so moves with growth rate.
  - It has discontinuities where the number of overlapping rounds changes.
  - At slow growth the review reports τcyc ∝ τd (Wallden), which would make the initiation fraction nearly constant within
    that regime. That is relevant to CD-3's constant-fraction baseline.
- **Bears on CD-1.** The CV of division size is below the CV of generation time; this is the classical observation.

> To resolve this paradox, rapidly growing cells initiate new rounds of chromosome replication before completing the previous round, a situation that results in two, four or even eight rounds of replication proceeding simultaneously.

**Most useful for CD-3.** Witz et al. 2019 is the most useful data set located.
- **Why.** It has:
  - a 3 min frame interval;
  - three growth conditions, doubling times 89, 53 and 41 min, spanning single to overlapping replication rounds;
  - thousands of cell cycles;
  - an open licence;
  - MoMA per-frame segmentations in the Zenodo archive. These are needed for a Hilbert phase on the log-length trace. The
    brief's Colin and Si files are per-cell summaries.
- **Caveat.** Initiation is origin splitting, which the authors call a proxy.

**Second choice.** Colin et al. 2021 is second. Its per-cell initiation, birth and division times are published directly.
Its frame interval is 6–8 min and it has two slow media.
