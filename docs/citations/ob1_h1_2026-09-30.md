# OB1 citation checks, family H1: class-incremental, exemplar-free and online continual learning (2026-09-30; R10)

This is stage 1 of `Open_Bottlenecks/DECLARATION.md`, family **H1**: surveys, benchmarks, position papers and recent method
papers. The dossier records bottlenecks only. It makes no CRR reading; that is stage 2.

**How the sources were handled.** Every source was fetched on the day (2026-09-30) through the session proxy:
- **arXiv:** the abstract page, for the current version, the version history, the comment and the journal reference, and the
  PDF of the current version.
- **CVF open access:** the paper page and the camera-ready PDF.

Quotes are copied from the text that pymupdf 1.28.2 extracted from the fetched PDF. Only two things are changed: whitespace
is normalised, and hyphens at line breaks are rejoined. Table rows are quoted as the extractor emitted them, cell by cell in
reading order. Fetched files are held outside the repository, under `/tmp/claude-0/ob1_src/h1/` (`src/` the fetched files,
`txt/` the extracted text, `search/` the saved result pages). Their sha256 is recorded below and in
`/tmp/claude-0/ob1_src/h1/SHA256SUMS.txt`.

**Verification.** The claims are in `Open_Bottlenecks/checks/claims_h1.py`: 50 claims and 99 quotes. They were checked with a
scratch copy of `Relational_Reference_Memory/checks/verify.py`, pointed at `claims_h1.py` with root `/tmp/claude-0/ob1_src`:
- **Result:** "quotes found verbatim: 99 of 99 (claims 50; raw files 16, missing 0); not found 0; missing 0".
- **Negative control:** one table cell was altered ("Offline 49.7" changed to "Offline 59.7"). The check then read 98 of 99,
  with h1:26 quote 1 NOT FOUND, as it should.

The family's pinned check is `Open_Bottlenecks/checks/verify.py`, which reads claims_h1 to h4.

**Rules for the numbers.**
- Every number below is the paper's own, on its own protocol.
- Numbers from different papers are not comparable. The one exception used in a gap is h1-B5, where the cross-paper target
  is flagged.
- The gaps are the agent's arithmetic on quoted numbers.
- A description of a mechanism or setting is the agent's reading of the fetched text. The quotes are the evidence.
- Code links were **not checked**.

## Bottlenecks

The open test: B-a, B-b and B-c must all be present (`Open_Bottlenecks/checks/table.py` computes it from the roles). Six are
shown open. Two are listed as **not shown open**.

### h1-B1. The gap to joint training when the representation is learned (open)

- **B-a** (Momeni & Liu 2025; claim h1:1):
  > However, a significant gap remains between the accuracy achieved by state-of-the-art continual learning algorithms and the ideal or upper-bound accuracy achieved by training all tasks together jointly.
- **B-b** (Qiu et al., Sep 2025, h1:2):
  > Despite recent progress, current CIL methods still exhibit significant performance gaps compared to their oracle counterparts—models trained with full access to historical data.

  DRDN (2026, h1:3):
  > under-optimized shared representations in the backbone that cap long-term discriminability as tasks accumulate.
- **B-c** (DRDN Table II, h1:4). The benchmark is CIFAR100-B0 with a from-scratch ViT of about 11M parameters and a replay
  buffer of 2,000. The table caption reads "BOUND = JOINT-TRAINING UPPER". The rows:
  > Bound 10.72 – 81.49 – 10.72 – 81.49 – 10.72 – 81.49 –
  > DER [3] 56.13 76.80 68.32 -10.8 112.27 75.36 65.22 -12.1 224.55 74.09 62.48 -13.0
  > DRDN (ours) 10.75 77.91 69.19 -9.8 10.77 77.19 65.40 -11.2 10.80 76.03 59.86 -13.8

  Last accuracy against the bound of 81.49, from the same paper:

  | Steps | Best last accuracy | Gap to 81.49 |
  |---|---|---|
  | 5 | 69.19 | 12.30 |
  | 10 | 65.40 | 16.09 |
  | 20 | 62.48 (DER, at 224.55M parameters) | 19.01 |

  The gap grows with the number of steps.
- **B-d.** The method families, and the assumption each makes:
  - **Expansion** (h1:5) assumes given task boundaries and adds a component per step: "growing dedicated task-specific
    components — tokens or subnetworks — at each step". Parameters grow with tasks: DER goes from 56.13M to 224.55M.
  - **Parameter-space consolidation** (IVT, h1:6) is anchored to the previous task optimum and runs on a fixed step clock:
    "This is done every I steps". Importance is "diagonal Fisher Information Matrices".
  - **Replay** assumes a small stored sample suffices. Momeni & Liu say it does not (h1:7): "insufficient for fully adjusting
    all learned feature representations to achieve joint training accuracy".
  - **Frozen features** remove the problem by assumption: "It assumes that the foundation model provides strong and
    sufficient feature representations" (h1:7).
- **B-e.** Not CPU-testable on the named benchmark. It trains ViTs from scratch on GPU. A reduced proxy (a small CNN on Split
  CIFAR-100) would fit a CPU in hours, but it is a proxy.

### h1-B2. Cold-start exemplar-free class-IL: the gap to joint training (open)

- **B-a** (CIRCLE 2026, h1:8):
  > Cold-start exemplar-free class-incremental learning requires learning a growing set of classes without replay, external pretraining, or a large initial task.
- **B-b** (EFC++, IJCV 2026, arXiv v4 28 Sep 2026, h1:9):
  > As a final note, we report the performance under Joint Training, where the gap remains substantial. Closing this gap will likely require either a more advanced strategy than Gaussian prototypes or improved regularization techniques beyond those we proposed.

  APR (WACV 2026, h1:10): "the plasticity-stability dilemma […] remains unresolved".
- **B-c** (APR Table 1, h1:11). This is cold start: "Only 1/T of the total classes are in the initial task". The rows:
  > Joint Linear 83.25 78.69 84.18 78.69 69.84 65.71 70.54 65.71 87.48 85.39
  > APR Maha 74.99 65.55 69.96 57.94 60.74 50.77 56.39 44.35 73.86 62.88

  Final accuracy at T = 10 against Joint, from the same paper:

  | Dataset | APR (Maha) | Joint | Gap |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | CIFAR-100 | 57.94 | 78.69 | 20.75 |
  | TinyImageNet | 44.35 | 65.71 | 21.36 |
  | ImageNet-Subset | 62.88 | 85.39 | 22.51 |

  EFC++ Tables 7–8 (h1:12) give EFC++ (Ours) against Joint Training:

  | Setting | EFC++ | Joint Training | Gap |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | CIFAR-100, 10 steps | 47.52 | 71.00 | 23.48 |
  | CIFAR-100, 20 steps | 33.71 | 71.00 | 37.29 |
  | ImageNet-1K, 10 steps | 44.72 | 69.76 | 25.04 |
- **B-d.** The method families, and the assumption each makes:
  - **Trained backbone with drift compensation** remaps the stored statistics at each task transition, so the boundaries are
    given.
  - **Freeze after the first task** assumes the first task is representative. Both are named in h1:13: "either train the
    backbone throughout the stream and compensate for semantic drift, or freeze a backbone after the first task".
  - **Memory** is the stored class means and covariances only, and the covariances are held fixed.
  - **Tuning** is done on a validation split of each task (h1:14): "the shrinkage parameters γ1 and γ2 in eq. 9 are
    determined by splitting the validation dataset (N=50 per class)".
  - **CIRCLE** is the alternative. It works "without replay, task-boundary information, or backbone backpropagation", but it
    never learns features.
- **B-e.** No. APR alone reports "31.0 hours on Imagenet-Subset T = 10" on a GPU (h1:14). Only a reduced proxy fits a CPU.

### h1-B3. Estimating the drift of stored class statistics, against the oracle (open)

- **B-a** (CIRCLE, h1:15): "as ϕ changes, old-class features shift and stored head statistics become outdated."

  APR (h1:16):
  > The major cause of the catastrophic forgetting is the change in the feature extractor, also known as semantic drift [27] occurring when learning a new task.
- **B-b** (EFC++ 2025–26, h1:17):
  > From this analysis, we conclude that future work on Gaussian prototypes should prioritize more effective strategies for updating class means, beyond our current EFM approach.

  CIRCLE (2026, h1:18): "drift control is approximate and can accumulate error over many task transitions."
- **B-c** (EFC++ Table 8, ImageNet-1K cold start, h1:19). The oracle is "an idealized setting using real mean and real
  covariances, computed from old task data fed in the current task backbone":
  - 10 steps: EFM Fixed (Ours) 44.72 against Real Real 49.97, a gap of **5.25**.
  - 20 steps: 37.46 against 45.55, a gap of **8.09**.

  The real means alone recover most of the gap (49.95 and 44.92). The gap grows with steps.
- **B-d.** Drift is estimated from current-task samples only, at each task transition, so the boundary is given. Stored
  statistics are fixed between transitions. The families (h1:20) are prototype rehearsal, drift estimation (SDC, ADC, LDC,
  AdaGauss, EFC, EFC++) and distillation. EFC++ "does not update class covariances across incremental learning steps"
  (h1:21).
- **B-e.** Not on the named benchmark (ImageNet-1K). The estimate-versus-oracle gap itself could be measured on a small
  synthetic or reduced stream on a CPU; that would be a proxy.

### h1-B4. Online single-pass class-IL: the gap to offline training (open)

- **B-a** (NGD for OCL, NeurIPS 2025 per the PDF header, arXiv v1 21 Mar 2026, h1:22):
  > The model is thus required to achieve satisfactory performance from a single pass over the online data stream using very small batch sizes.
- **B-b** (h1:23):
  > There is still a big gap between OCL-trained models and Offline trained models.

  BISON (SAC 2026, h1:24): "it still remains a big challenge to achieve a well-balanced learner". The OCL systematic review
  (Jan 2025, h1:25): "many challenges remain unresolved".
- **B-c** (NGD Table 2, Split CIFAR-100 OCI, batch 10, h1:26). The offline reference is "Offline 49.7 ± 2.6", trained "for 70
  epochs with a batch size of 128". The best cell is the RV trick with NGD-KFAC, ER, M = 10k: **39.9 ± 0.5**.
  - The gap is **9.8** with 10k of the 50k training images stored.
  - At M = 1k the best is 20.3, a gap of **29.4**.
  - The agent's reading of the table layout: the check glyph that marks the NGD-KFAC rows was not extracted, and the rows
    alternate without and with NGD-KFAC.
  - Supporting: LOR (CVPR 2026, h1:27) reaches 39.02 on Seq-CIFAR100 at M = 5k with a reduced ResNet-18. LOR reports no
    offline row.
- **B-d.** The method families, and the assumption each makes:
  - **Fixed raw-sample buffer:** "maintain a small memory buffer of samples from past tasks and replay them alongside current
    data" (h1:30).
  - **Fixed learning rate and batch:** "a learning rate of 0.1, and a fixed batch size of 10" (h1:28).
  - **Retuning under shift:** "hyperparameters must be recalibrated whenever data distributions shift" (h1:29).
  - **Buffer review:** "an additional fine-tuning step using a balanced subset of the memory buffer" (h1:28).
  - **Gradient-conflict corrections**, such as hypergradients and lookahead (h1:30).
  - **Old/new classifier separation** (h1:31).
- **B-e.** Yes. One pass over Split CIFAR-100 with a reduced ResNet-18 at batch 10 is about an hour of CPU per run. The
  70-epoch offline reference takes several hours. The data are public.

### h1-B5. Long task sequences, 50 to 500 tasks (open, with a cross-paper target)

- **B-a** (CIRCLE, h1:32): "the behavior of these methods at much longer horizons remains underexplored", and "performance
  degrades sharply for trained-backbone drift-compensation methods in this regime."
- **B-b** (CaRE, ICML 2026, h1:33):
  > However, how to effectively learn both discriminative and comprehensive feature representations while maintaining stability and plasticity over very long task sequences remains an open problem.
- **B-c** (CIRCLE Table 1, CIFAR-100 cold start, h1:34). At T = 100:
  - the best final accuracy is CIRCLE, **45.34**, with fixed random features ("continual training for CIRCLE is equivalent to
    joint training");
  - the best trained backbone is EFC++, **16.40**, down from 52.68 at T = 10. That is a collapse of 36.28 in the same paper.

  The paper has no joint row for a learned backbone. The target used is EFC++'s own **Joint Training 71.00**, on CIFAR-100
  cold start (h1:12). **This target comes from a different paper; the protocols were not verified to be equal.** The gaps are
  25.66 for the best method and 54.60 for the best trained backbone. CaRE on OmniBenchmark-1K (h1:35) reaches a last accuracy
  of 68.51 at 301 tasks, but reports no joint or oracle row.
- **B-d.** The method families, and the assumption each makes:
  - **Adapters or routers per task** assume given boundaries and fixed epochs per task (h1:36): "construct a set of
    task-specific adapters", "train for 20 epochs per task".
  - **Drift compensation** has a cost that grows with the horizon: "per-task cost growing with the number of seen classes"
    (h1:37).
  - **Frozen features** are never learned.
- **B-e.** No. The frozen-feature arms run on a CPU. The trained-backbone arms at T = 100 and the ViT-B/16 OmniBenchmark
  runs do not fit in hours.

### h1-B6. The stability gap at task switches (open)

- **B-a** (Rodriguez-Garcia et al., arXiv v3 10 Aug 2026, h1:38):
  > Recent work in continual learning has highlighted the stability gap – a temporary performance drop on previously learned tasks when new ones are introduced.
- **B-b** (h1:39):
  > they still exhibit pronounced stability gaps. Importantly, these gaps persist even under ideal joint training

  The same source says its own fix gains "less pronounced than hypothesized in prior work".
- **B-c** (Table 1, class-incremental, under joint training, h1:40). The best is the proposed NGM-SGD. The target is no
  transient drop: avg-min-ACC equal to avg-ACC, or avg-SG = 0.

  | Benchmark | avg-min-ACC (best) | avg-ACC (target) | Dip | avg-SG |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Split CIFAR-10 | 79.485 | 90.630 | 11.145 | 0.134 |
  | Split mini-ImageNet | 33.165 | 48.312 | 15.147 | 0.300 |
- **B-d.** Optimiser timescales (momentum, adaptive rates) are fixed on the step clock. The fix adds a second, fast
  timescale (h1:41). The deployment workaround is to "be updated less often or are retrained entirely from scratch".
- **B-e.** Yes. Split MNIST or Split CIFAR-10 with small networks and per-step evaluation runs in minutes to hours on a CPU.
  MNIST is SEEN in this repository.

### h1-B7. Class and task order sensitivity (not shown open)

- **B-a** (Mitchell et al. 2025, h1:42): "most existing continual learning algorithms suffer drastic fluctuations in
  performance under different schedules". CLDyB (ICLR 2025, h1:43): "a 26% reduction in Final Acc […] across the evaluated
  methods".
- **B-b** (Lai et al., CVPR 2025, h1:44):
  > Thus, designing a model capable of maintaining stable performance across varying class orders remains a critical unsolved issue in CIL.
- **No B-c.** Order-robustness numbers (MOPD, AOPD) appear only in a bar chart (Fig. 4), and none is extractable. CLDyB's 26%
  is an aggregate across methods, not a best result against a target. CaRE's order standard deviations (0.16 to 1.50 in AB; h1:50)
  have no stated target.
- **B-d** (h1:45): structural changes (APD, HALRP), and class grouping over a frozen pre-trained extractor.
- **B-e.** Yes: order permutations on small benchmarks are cheap.

### h1-B8. The gap to joint training with a strong frozen pre-trained model (not shown open: the sources report it closed)

- **B-a** (AnaCP 2025, h1:46): "the inability to match joint training remains a key barrier to the practical adoption of
  CL."
- **No B-b.** The sources state the reverse: the target is reached.
- **B-c, for the record** (AnaCP Table 1 with DINO-v2, h1:47):
  - CIFAR100: 92.15 against joint fine-tuning 93.35, a gap of 1.20.
  - ImageNet-R: 86.60 against 88.79, a gap of 2.19.
  - CUB and Cars: ahead of joint fine-tuning.

  Momeni & Liu (h1:48), with frozen DINOv2-base: CIFAR100 88.81 against 90.30 (gap 1.49); T-ImageNet 83.18 against 86.43
  (gap 3.25), where "a small performance gap persists".
- **B-d** (h1:49): the closure depends on a strong frozen extractor. "With a weaker PTM such as MoCo-v3, AnaCP cannot match
  joint fine-tuning accuracy", and "the gap to joint fine-tuning increases to 6%".
- **B-e.** Yes: features are extracted once, and the analytic heads run in minutes.
