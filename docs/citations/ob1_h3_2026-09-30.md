# OB1 citation checks, family H3: loss of plasticity, long-horizon streams, continual reinforcement learning, non-stationary optimisation (2026-09-30; R10)

This is the Stage 1 harvest of `Open_Bottlenecks/DECLARATION.md` for family **H3**. For each bottleneck it records B-a (the
problem), B-b (a 2025–26 statement that it is open), B-c (the best reported number on a named benchmark against a target),
B-d (the method families and the assumption each makes) and B-e (whether it can be tested on a laptop-class CPU). All
sources were fetched on the day (2026-09-30) through the session proxy. For arXiv, the agent fetched the abstract page (for
the current version, the version history and the comment) and the PDF of the current version. For Nature, it fetched the
open-access published PDF. Quotes are copied from the text that pymupdf 1.28.2 extracted from the fetched PDF. Whitespace is
normalised and line-break hyphens are rejoined; nothing else is changed. Table rows and figure-panel labels are quoted cell by
cell, in the order the extractor emitted them. The fetched files are held outside the repository
(`/tmp/claude-0/ob1_src/h3/src/`). Their sha256 is recorded here and in `/tmp/claude-0/ob1_src/h3/SHA256SUMS.txt`, and the
search result pages are in `/tmp/claude-0/ob1_src/h3/search/` (sha256 in `SHA256SUMS_search.txt`). **Code links were not
checked.**

The machine-readable form is `Open_Bottlenecks/checks/claims_h3.py` (41 claims, 86 quotes; `CLAIMS` and `BOTTLENECKS`). Each
blockquote below is a quote from that file. Every number is the paper's own, on the paper's own protocol, and numbers from
different papers are not comparable. The gap arithmetic, the reading of each table and the assumption labels are the
harvesting agent's work, for the investigator's review.

**The rule for "open" (the declaration's, applied strictly).** A bottleneck is marked open only if three things hold:

- it has an **a** quote;
- it has a **b** quote from 2025 or 2026;
- it has **c** numbers that put the best reported result below a stated target.

A statement with no number, or numbers only in figures, gives **NOT SHOWN OPEN**. So does a benchmark on which the frontier
reports the target reached. Such bottlenecks are listed below, not dropped.

## Bottlenecks shown open (a, b and c all quoted)

### h3-B1. Plasticity loss in deep RL under task switches (OPEN, mixed)

**B-a (problem).** Klein et al., survey v3 (2026):
> Plasticity loss represents a fundamental challenge in deep RL, where networks progressively lose their ability to learn despite remaining capacity.

**B-b (open, 2025–26).** Klein et al. v3 (18 Apr 2026):
> This variability highlights a critical gap: We cannot yet predict which environment properties will cause severe plasticity loss and which interventions will prove effective for a given task.

> However, fundamental understanding remains limited despite empirical progress.

Plasticine v2 (10 Feb 2026):
> Despite its significance, this field lacks unified benchmarks and evaluation protocols.

> Accurately quantifying neural network plasticity remains an open research question

**B-c (best vs target).** C-CHAIN (Tang et al., ICML 2025), Table 1, continual Gym Control. Each environment is chained k times,
with a fixed Gaussian observation noise per instance. The target is an Oracle:
> we use a PPO agent that gets fully re-initialized every time the task is switched to be the oracle baseline.

> Oracle Vanilla TRAC Weight Clipping L2 Init C-CHAIN

> C-CartPole 299.925 ± 5.986 61.766 ± 6.927 217.376 ± 26.307 154.306 ± 16.047 266.114 ± 5.345 160.396 ± 14.643

> C-LunarLander 1.118 ± 2.002 -499.396 ± 19.571 -58.059 ± 17.474 -58.575 ± 2.896 −6.666 ± 5.903 -16.659 ± 8.369

> Agg. Score -147.302 -1156.958 -407.256 -290.99 -243.985 −221.22

**The gap (the agent's arithmetic).**
- **Aggregate.** The best single method (C-CHAIN) scores −221.22 against the Oracle's −147.302, **short by 73.92 return units**
  (summed over the four environments).
- **Per environment.** The best non-oracle method trails the Oracle on two environments:
  - CartPole: L2 Init 266.114 against 299.925 (−33.81);
  - LunarLander: L2 Init −6.666 against 1.118 (−7.78).
- **It is ahead on the other two.** On Acrobot, Weight Clipping scores −118.821 against the Oracle's −125.736. On MountainCar,
  C-CHAIN scores −245.746 against −322.608.
- **Mixed.** In the same paper, the ProcGen table puts methods above the Oracle:

  > One may note that TRAC, L2 Init and C-CHAIN outperform Oracle in ProcGen.

  So the per-task re-initialised network is not an upper bound everywhere.

**Context without a target.** On Continual MinAtar (Calibrated Partial Resets, 2026), every method's final return is below its
own average over training:
> Method Avg Final CPR 75.6 ± 14.3 52.3 ± 25.0 CBP 65.6 ± 3.2 5.8 ± 14.9

**B-d (method families and assumptions).**

*Resets.* They are scheduled on the clock, and their strength is tuned:
> Non-targeted weight resets periodically reinitialize network layers to restore plasticity and were initially proposed to mitigate early overfitting

> an optimizer that periodically pulls low-utility neurons toward their initialization, with pull strength scaled by each neuron’s utility.

> Ablations reveal a tunable trade-off between plasticity and peak performance

*Regularisers.* They are held fixed, because non-stationarity is not measured:
> Such quantification is necessary for principled regularization selection: stronger non-stationarity should require stronger regularization, but without measurement tools, this principle cannot be implemented.

> we recommend that practitioners encountering plasticity loss should start with general regularization techniques such as LayerNorm and SpectralNorm.

*Boundary-keyed methods.* The task switch is supplied from outside the learner:
> Adam with Relative Timesteps (AdamRel)

> The oracle baseline learns each task from random initialization, thus free of the influence of task change.

**B-e (CPU).** **Yes.** The Gym Control environments with small MLP PPO agents run on a laptop CPU, and a reduced chain (fewer
seeds or instances) fits in hours with public simulators (gymnasium). ProcGen and MinAtar variants are heavier.

### h3-B2. Streaming deep RL (no replay, batch size 1) against batch and replay methods (OPEN, continuous control only)

**B-a.** Stream-X, v3 (21 Sep 2026):
> However, reliable streaming learning has remained a persistent challenge in modern deep reinforcement learning (RL).

> We call this phenomenon stream barrier; overcoming it remains an open problem, which we address in this work.

**B-b (2026).** Nilaksh et al. (10 Feb 2026):
> As a result, streaming RL is severely sample-inefficient: each transition is expensive to obtain, and its informational content is only weakly exploited before being discarded.

> Not many works study representation learning in a streaming fashion (Han et al., 2025), and none do for streaming RL, making this an open challenge.

Intentional updates (21 Apr 2026):
> In several environments, Intentional AC closes most of the gap between streaming learning and replay-based training.

> There remain several open problems and natural next steps.

**B-c.** Stream-X v3, Figure 1b. The panel text is as extracted. Scores are median normalised over tasks, where 1 is the best
average return of any method or buffer setting:
> SAC1 PPO1 IAC Stream-AC Best(PPO, SAC): 1.00 0.04 0.07 0.00 0.73 Continuous control 50 tasks

> DQN1 Q-learning Stream-Q DQN: 0.67 0.00 0.15 0.99 Atari 58 games

> One denotes the best average return among all methods including buffer settings.

**The gap.** On 50 continuous-control tasks (MuJoCo Gym and DM Control, 5M steps each), the best streaming agent (Stream-AC)
scores **0.73**. The batch reference is 1.00: the per-task better of SAC with a 1e6 replay buffer or PPO with a 2048-step
rollout. The shortfall is **0.27 normalised**. On 58 Atari games, Stream-Q (0.99) is above batch DQN (0.67), so there is no
gap there. The 2026 successor (Intentional AC) reports closing "most of the gap" in several environments; its numbers are in
figures only.

**B-d.** Batch methods store the past. Stream-X fixes one configuration for the whole stream. The step size is set in
parameter units, and the successor moves it to output units but keeps a global scale:
> Instead, most deep RL algorithms learn from old experience by storing past interactions in a buffer—an approach that can be restrictive in resource-constrained and privacy-sensitive applications.

> Using one prescribed hyperparameter configuration per algorithm across tasks, Stream-X substantially improves aggregate performance, often on par with batch RL algorithms.

> In gradient-based learning, a step size chosen in parameter units does not produce a predictable per-step change in function output.

> We deliberately avoided learning-rate schedules and decay to respect the continual nature of RL.

> Intentional updates still use a global scale η

**B-e.** **Yes, for a subset.** Streaming updates use batch size 1 and run well on a CPU (the source runs Stream-Q on a
microcontroller). A subset of MuJoCo tasks at reduced step counts fits in hours. The full 50 tasks × 5M steps × 30 seeds does
not.

### h3-B4. Warm-started or data-incremental training ends below offline joint training (OPEN)

**B-a.** DASH (NeurIPS 2024):
> However, it often leads to loss of plasticity, where the network loses its ability to learn new information, resulting in worse generalization than training from scratch.

Hess et al. (CoLLAs 2026):
> we show that these two issues cannot fully explain the performance gap between naive sequential training and offline joint training.

> the gap between the incremental joint model and the (offline) joint target highlights the loss of plasticity.

**B-b (2026).**
> Efficient continual learning remains a fundamental challenge for deep neural networks.

> How to best facilitate such synergies, especially in compute-bounded settings, needs to be the subject of further research.

**B-c.** Hess et al., Table 2. ImageNet-100 is split into 4 i.i.d. chunks; a ResNet-18 is trained from scratch and scored by
linear-probe accuracy:
> Table 2: SL-IN-100 - LP-accuracy[%] IN-100

> Incremental Joint 73.24±0.48 79.58±0.12 81.85±0.10 82.75±0.15 Joint (offline) – – – 83.68±0.11

> Replay (mt =12500) 73.25±0.07 78.05±0.07 80.68±0.15 81.27±0.24 LwF 73.35±0.23 77.41±0.15 79.44±0.22 79.93±0.25

**The gap.** The target is Joint (offline), 83.68.
- **Warm-started incremental joint** (all data so far, continued from the previous chunk): 82.75, **0.93 pt short**. This is
  the paper's plasticity part.
- **Best continual method** (replay, 12500 per chunk, about 30%): 81.27, **2.41 pt short**.
- **LwF:** 79.93, 3.75 pt short.

**Counter-evidence (2024, its own protocol).** On Tiny-ImageNet, DASH is above cold start:
> Random Init 25.69 (0.13) 31.30 (0.09)

> DASH 46.11 (0.34) 49.57 (0.36)

DASH was not run in Hess et al.'s protocol.

**B-d.**
> For fair comparison, all training is constrained to an identical, fixed iteration budget per chunk.

> Storing approximately 15% of the data appears sufficient for the replay mechanism to effectively match the performance of the ensemble.

> the linear probing accuracy pushes past the ensemble baseline, closing half the gap to incremental joint training.

> a method aiming to mitigate plasticity loss by selectively forgetting memorized noise while preserving learned features.

These methods assume:
- the chunk boundaries are given;
- the step budget per chunk is fixed;
- replay stores 15–30% of the data;
- the data are stationary (DASH).

**B-e.** **No.** The named benchmark is ResNet-18 on ImageNet-100 or CIFAR-100 for about 100 epoch-equivalents, which takes
GPU-days. A small-MLP analogue would run on a CPU, but it is not the named benchmark.

## Listed as NOT SHOWN OPEN (not dropped)

### h3-B5. Hyperparameters without a natural horizon ("lifetime tuning")

**B-a.** Mesbahi et al. (ICML 2025 position):
> The standard practice in RL is to assume unfettered access to the deployment environment for the full lifetime of the agent.

> lifetime tuning does not allow us to identify algorithms that work well for continual learning—all algorithms equally succeed;

**B-b (2026).** AgarCL:
> Hyperparameter tuning is a central yet often overlooked challenge in continual RL.

> This pragmatic choice reflects a broader open question in continual RL: how should hyperparameters be selected when no natural training horizon exists?

Behdin et al. (ICML 2026 position):
> CRL research should treat hyperparameter tuning as part of online deployment, not a hidden offline phase

> Degris et al. (2024) showed that two commonly used optimizers, RMSProp (Hinton et al., 2012) and Adam (Kingma & Ba, 2015), were unsuitable for step-size adaptation in a simple continual learning scenario.

**B-c: none.** Mesbahi et al. report their results in figures only. The text gives qualitative statements:
> Tuning on one-percent of a lifetime leads to poor performance in Continuing Cartpole, whereas lifetime tuning allows DQN to achieve nearly optimal performance.

**B-d.** One hyperparameter vector is chosen before deployment, either on the whole lifetime or on k% of it, and then held
fixed:
> Instead, we suggest a limited tuning phase: a small percent of the total lifetime.

> The learning rate is particularity sensitive to lifetime.

("particularity" is the source's spelling.)

**B-e.** **Yes.** Continuing Cartpole and Non-stationary Catch with DQN run on a CPU in hours. **NOT SHOWN OPEN**, because
there is no number in any text.

### h3-B6. Supervised loss of plasticity on long task sequences

**B-a.** Nature (2024):
> Here we show that they do not—that standard deep-learning methods gradually lose plasticity in continual-learning settings until they learn no better than a shallow network.

> Although these networks learned up to 88% correct on the test set of the early tasks

**B-b (2026, about understanding rather than a benchmark gap).**
> Despite growing interest, loss of plasticity remains less understood and underexplored,

> Several questions remain open.

**B-c (target reached, as the frontier reports it).** Prakash et al., v3 2026:
> a RESET baseline that reinitializes parameters at each task change; algorithms outperforming RESET are considered to maintain plasticity.

> L2-ER maintains plasticity in all, and provides an early performance boost on Permuted MNIST before plasticity loss would typically occur.

The four benchmarks are Permuted MNIST, Continual ImageNet, Incremental CIFAR and Slippery Ant; the numbers are in figures.

**B-d.**
> A common thread across these studies is the use of benchmarks with abrupt task transitions;

> showing that the severity of plasticity loss is closely tied to the abruptness of task transitions, and can be substantially reduced when the environment changes gradually.

> a variation of backpropagation in which a small fraction of less-used units are continually and randomly reinitialized.

**B-e.** **Yes** (Permuted MNIST and slowly-changing regression). **NOT SHOWN OPEN.**

## Judgement calls (the agent's, for review)

- **h3-B1 is marked open on the continual Gym Control aggregate**, although the same paper's ProcGen table puts three methods
  above its Oracle. The declaration asks whether the best reported result falls short of a stated target; on Gym Control it
  does (aggregate, and 2 of 4 environments). A stricter reading ("short on every benchmark in the source") would make it NOT
  SHOWN OPEN.
- **h3-B2 relies on figure-panel text.** "0.73" and "Best(PPO, SAC): 1.00" are text in the PDF's figure. The extractor emitted
  them, and they are verified verbatim, but they are not in a table. The B-a source (Stream-X) says it addresses the stream
  barrier; the B-b statements come from two other 2026 papers. The gap exists only on continuous control.
- **h3-B3's target is the "Human" column of AgarCL Table 9.** The source does not describe it further in the quoted text. The
  column assignment of the extracted rows is the agent's reading.
- **h3-B4 overlaps H1** (data-incremental supervised learning). It is kept in H3 because its measured gap separates the
  warm-start (plasticity) part (0.93 pt) from the co-observation part. DASH (2024) closes a warm-start gap on a different
  protocol, and the two were not compared.
- **h3-B5 has strong 2026 open statements but is NOT SHOWN OPEN**, because the lifetime-tuning evidence is in figures only.
  This is the family's clearest case of an open problem that the strict rule excludes.
- **h3-B6 is NOT SHOWN OPEN** because the 2026 frontier reports the RESET target reached on the standard benchmarks. The quoted
  open statements concern mechanism and theory.
- **B-c numbers come from a 2025 paper for h3-B1 (C-CHAIN, ICML 2025) and a 2024 paper for the DASH counterpoint.** The
  declaration requires 2025–26 dating for B-b only. The B-b statements for every open bottleneck are dated 2026.
