# OB1 citation checks, family H4: deployment constraints (boundary-free streams, tuning without future data, compute budgets, on-device learning) (2026-09-30; R10)

This is stage 1 of `Open_Bottlenecks/DECLARATION.md`, family **H4**. The family covers task-free or boundary-free
streams, choosing hyperparameters without future data, compute-budgeted continual learning, and on-device learning.

**How the sources were fetched.**
- **Date and route.** All sources were fetched on the day (2026-09-30) through the session proxy.
- **arXiv.** For each source, the abstract page (for the current version, the version history, the comment and the
  journal reference) and the PDF of the current version.
- **Extraction.** Quotes are copied from the text that pymupdf 1.28.2 extracted from the fetched PDF. Whitespace is
  normalised and line-break hyphens are rejoined; nothing else is changed.
- **Tables.** Table rows are quoted as the extractor emitted them, cell by cell in reading order.
- **Storage.** Fetched files are held outside the repository (`/tmp/claude-0/ob1_src/h4/src/`), with the extracted texts
  in `/tmp/claude-0/ob1_src/h4/txt/` and the saved search pages in `/tmp/claude-0/ob1_src/h4/search/`. Their sha256 is
  recorded below and in `/tmp/claude-0/ob1_src/h4/SHA256SUMS.txt`.
- **Code links** were **not checked**.

**Verification.** The claims file is `Open_Bottlenecks/checks/claims_h4.py`: 41 claims, 76 quotes, 7 bottlenecks.
- **Result:** 76 of 76 quotes found verbatim in 17 extracted texts. None were missing or not found.
- **How it was checked:** a scratch copy of `Relational_Reference_Memory/checks/verify.py`, pointed at `claims_h4.py` and
  the root `/tmp/claude-0/ob1_src`.
- **Negative control:** a copy of the claims with two quotes altered (one table number, 62.62 → 62.26 in h4:3, and one
  word, "more" → "less" in h4:30). It read 74 of 76, and the two altered quotes were NOT FOUND, as required.
- **Not added to the repository:** the stage-1 verifier (`Open_Bottlenecks/checks/verify.py`) is to be run over this file
  by the caller.

**Reading rule for "open" (the agent's, for review).** A bottleneck counts as open only if two things hold:
1. it has a quote stating the problem (B-a), a 2025–26 quote stating it is open (B-b), and quoted numbers on a named
   benchmark against a target (B-c);
2. the best reported result falls short of the target by more than the reported spread.

A bottleneck that has a 2025–26 statement, but whose quoted numbers put the best result at the target or within about
one point of it, is recorded as **not shown open**. It is kept in the table, not dropped. Numbers from different papers
are not comparable. Every gap below is computed within one paper and one protocol.

## The bottlenecks

### h4-B1 · Boundary-free streams (task-free / general continual learning): **OPEN**

**B-a: the problem.** From Park, Lee & Lee (CVPR Findings 2026), claim h4:0:
> Task-free online continual learning has recently emerged as a realistic paradigm for addressing continual learning in
> dynamic, real-world environments, where data arrive in a non-stationary stream without clear task boundaries and can only
> be observed once.

**B-b: open in 2025–26.** From FlyPrompt (ICLR 2026), claim h4:1:
> We therefore identify two fundamental challenges that remain unresolved: (1) how to dynamically route inputs to
> appropriate experts without task labels or iterative training, and (2) how to ensure that each expert maintains strong
> and adaptive representations under sparse and imbalanced supervision. Both remain non-trivial

From MePo (ICML 2026), claim h4:2:
> these methods remain limited in reconciling the diverse and temporally mixed information along a single pass, resulting
> in sub-optimal GCL performance.

**B-c: best result against the target.** The source is Michel et al. 2025, claim h4:3. The paper runs the same one-pass
protocol twice:
- **Table 1:** the Si-Blurry stream, which has no boundaries;
- **Table 2:** the "clear setting", which has task boundaries.

The columns are CIFAR100, CUB and Imagenet-R, each at LR 5e-5, LR 5e-3 and Best HP (tuned on VTAB).

Best Si-Blurry row (Table 1):
> ConvPrompt 24.55±3.8 75.01±5.16 75.01±5.16 0.64±0.23 56.27±0.84 56.27±0.84 1.18±0.02 46.75±1.8 46.75±1.8 ,→+ ours
> 44.23±3.29 86.34±3.59 86.34±3.59 4.43±1.13 73.88±0.87 73.88±0.87 3.78±0.22 62.62±0.11 62.62±0.11

The same method with clear boundaries (Table 2):
> ConvPrompt 33.8±0.71 73.88±3.15 73.88±3.15 2.14±0.54 65.96±2.78 65.96±2.78 3.07±0.37 59.6±0.29 59.6±0.29 ,→+ ours
> 60.07±1.37 87.65±0.37 87.65±0.37 5.54±1.1 75.73±0.12 75.73±0.12 6.89±0.32 69.76±1.38 69.76±1.38

**Gap (agent).** The target is the same method given boundaries.

| Benchmark | No boundaries (Si-Blurry) | With boundaries (target) | Gap | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ImageNet-R | 62.62 | 69.76 | **7.14 points** | the gap |
| CUB | 73.88 | 75.73 | 1.85 points | |
| CIFAR100 | 86.34 | 87.65 | 1.31 points | inside the ±3.59 spread |

- **Caveat.** Si-Blurry also varies task sizes and class overlap. The gap is therefore an upper bound on the cost of the
  missing boundaries, not a clean measure of it.
- **The state of the art, for level only.** FlyPrompt reports Alast 55.27±0.91 on Si-Blurry ImageNet-R (Table 2, claim
  h4:4), with no joint or boundary-aware target.

**B-d: the families tried, and what each assumes** (claims h4:5 to h4:9).
- **Key-query prompt or expert selection.** It assumes the input identifies its expert. Its failure is quoted in h4:5:
  "such selection strategies often fail to select appropriate prompts".
- **Logit masking.** It masks the output to the classes present in the current mini-batch (h4:5), or uses a
  non-parametric logit mask (h4:9). The batch is taken as the unit of "what is present now".
- **Boundaries as consolidation triggers.** In offline methods, boundaries are used "solely to freeze certain prompts".
  The online adaptations remove the trigger rather than replace it (h4:6).
- **Self-detected shifts.** Detection runs over a fixed "batch window" (FiUni, h4:7) or a fixed "sample budget (window
  size) of each expert" (FlyPrompt, h4:8). Both are indexed by a count of samples or batches, not by an event in the
  learner.
- **A frozen pretrained backbone and pretraining data before the stream** (MISA, h4:9).

**B-e: CPU-testable?** **No**, as reported. Prompt tuning through a ViT-B/16 over 24k–60k images, with 5–10 seeds, is not
hours on a laptop CPU. A frozen-feature variant would be feasible: precomputed embeddings, then prototype or linear heads on
a Si-Blurry-ordered stream. That is not the reported benchmark.

### h4-B2 · Hyperparameters without future data: **not shown open** (as a performance gap)

**B-a: the problem.** From Lee et al. 2025, claim h4:10:
> The most common way to fit hyperparameters for CL is end-of-training hyperparameter optimisation (HPO)

> This is when the hyperparameters are fit by training over the whole data stream with each hyperparameter configuration
> and then selecting the configuration that has the best end-of-training performance

**B-b: open in 2025.** From Lee et al., claim h4:11:
> there is an open question: what HPO framework should a practitioner use for a CL problem in reality?

From Michel et al., claim h4:12:
> In general, LR selection remains a difficult topic in Continual Learning, as, in theory, future datasets are unknown and
> hyperparameter search is unavailable [10]. This problem is even more pronounced in the online setting, as not even a
> learning rate scheduler can be used, since the length and boundaries of tasks are considered unknown.

From Cha & Cho (TMLR 2025), claim h4:13:
> most state-of-the-art algorithms fail to replicate their reported performance, highlighting that their CL capacity has
> been significantly overestimated in the conventional evaluation protocol.

**B-c: best result against the target.** The target is the hindsight (end-of-training) choice.

Lee et al., Table 3 (split-task CORe50 and Tiny ImageNet; columns CORe50 Class-IL, CORe50 Task-IL, Tiny ImageNet
Class-IL, Tiny ImageNet Task-IL), claim h4:14:

> 75.72±0.24

The "Default HPs" row is from Table 6, which compares default hyperparameters with HPO on the same benchmarks.

- **The best pipelines are at their oracle.** The best realistic pipeline is within about one point of its hindsight
  oracle, or equal to it.
- **Some methods lose a lot.** The losses in the lower rows are specific to those methods.
- **Some cells favour the realistic choice.** In Table 2, ER-ACE on CIFAR-100 Class-IL reads 56.46 with current-task HPO
  against 55.58 end-of-training (quoted in h4:14).
- **The authors' own reading:** "all HPO frameworks perform similarly".

The sources show the choice is **unreliable** (method-dependent losses; rankings change under GTEP). They do not show that
the best attainable result falls short. By the reading rule, this is **not shown open**.

**B-d: the families tried, and what each assumes** (claims h4:16 to h4:18, and h4:12).
- **First-task HPO.** It assumes the first task is representative.
- **Current-task HPO.** It assumes task boundaries, and retraining at each one.
- **Seen-tasks HPO.** It uses stored memory or past validation data.
- **Tuning on a proxy dataset.** CIFAR-10 in Seo et al.; VTAB in Michel et al. It assumes the hyperparameters transfer.
- **GTEP** (Cha & Cho). It assumes the same scenario shape, including a known number of tasks.
- **No schedule online,** because the stream length is unknown (h4:12).
- **Online hypergradient reweighting** (Michel et al.): prior art for choosing step sizes online.

**B-e: CPU-testable?** **No** for the named benchmarks. There are up to 90 configurations × 3 runs of ResNet-18 on Tiny
ImageNet. A small synthetic analogue would be CPU-feasible.

### h4-B3 · Compute-budgeted continual learning: **not shown open** (on the quoted numbers)

**B-a: the problem.** From Prabhu et al. (CVPR 2023, foundational), claim h4:19:
> This is unreasonable for applications in-the-wild, where systems are primarily constrained by computational and time
> budgets, not storage.

> traditional CL approaches, with no exception, fail to outperform a simple minimal baseline that samples uniformly from
> memory.

**B-b: the state in 2025–26.** From Seo et al. (ICLR 2025), claim h4:20:
> several high-performing CL methods are not competitive under a fixed computational budget, falling behind a simple
> baseline of training on randomly retrieved batches from memory.

From Cho et al. (ICLR 2026), claim h4:21:
> arguing that in modern deployments, GPU cost is the true constraint.

> practical solutions to address this gap are still underexplored.

**B-c: best result against the target.** From Cho et al., Table 6 (CIFAR-100 class-IL, memory 4 / 20 / 40 / 60 / 80 % of
past data), claim h4:22:
> Full Retraining 46.15 64.73 69.96 76.02 78.94 Continual (Replay) 48.63 66.11 71.60 73.29 75.71 Ours 52.16 67.25 74.49
> 75.97 77.71

**Gap (agent).** The target is full retraining from scratch on the same memory: the compute-heavy option.
- At 80 % memory: 77.71 against 78.94, a gap of **1.23 points** (Table 2 gives ±0.8 for 77.71).
- At 60 % memory: 0.05 points.
- At 4–40 % memory: continual learning is ahead of full retraining.
- The paper reports its method at replay-level GPU cost.

On this benchmark the compute-budgeted learner is within about one point of the expensive target, so the bottleneck is
**not shown open**.

**The large-scale evidence is not quotable.** Prabhu et al. (ImageNet2K and CGLM, with an ERM-Naive oracle given the whole
budget in hindsight) show their numbers only in figure boxes, which the extractor did not recover. What the text does give
compares a method with the naive baseline, not with an oracle (claim h4:23):
> The final gap between MSE distillation and Naive is 11.41% for C = 40, this gap is reduced to 3.85% for C = 400.

**B-d: the families tried, and what each assumes** (claims h4:24 to h4:26).
- **The benchmark itself** fixes compute per time step: "per time step, CL methods are given a fixed computational
  budget", counted in iterations. That is a clock-indexed allocation, equal across steps.
- **Adaptive layer freezing** chooses layers "by maximizing the Fisher Information (FI) gained by the model for each batch,
  given a fixed computation budget". Compute is already allocated by the information a batch carries; **prior art to
  note** for any change-indexed compute proposal.
- **Weight-space consolidation** assumes "a representative subset of past data" and "relatively clean task boundaries".

**B-e: CPU-testable?** **No.** The sources used ImageNet2K/CGLM (1500 GPU-hours) and multi-epoch ResNet runs on CIFAR-100.

### h4-B4 · On-device, memory-constrained online continual learning (configuration without hindsight): **OPEN** (thin evidence)

**B-a: the problem.** From Verwimp et al. (TMLR 2024), claim h4:27:
> On such small devices, both memory and computational resources are typically constrained, and the primary goal is to
> maximize model efficacy under these constraints.

From Orion (2026), claim h4:28:
> However, its practical deployment is hindered by memory constraints in resource-limited systems, which affect key
> trade-offs in training latency, plasticity, and stability.

**B-b: open in 2026.** From Orion, claim h4:29:
> Yet none of these efforts jointly address runtime memory constraints, algorithmic efficacy, and system-level co-design
> for on-device OCL. This gap is critical

**B-c: best result against the target.** From Orion, claim h4:30:
> Oracle: We conduct an offline parameter search by brute force to ensure optimized plasticity and stability.

> Oracle shows a more significant advantage on medium and large benchmarks, achieving an average plasticity of 0.28 and
> stability of 0.33, compared to Orion’s 0.22 and 0.30

**Gap (agent).**
- **Plasticity:** 0.06 below the oracle (21 % of the oracle's value).
- **Stability:** 0.03 below (9 %).

**Caveats.**
- **The benchmarks.** The study uses ER on SplitCIFAR10/100 and CORe50-NI/-NC/-NIC. The paper calls CORe50-NIC "large";
  which benchmarks count as "medium" is the agent's reading.
- **The oracle uses the future.** It is 42 offline runs over batch size × buffer size.
- **No spread** is reported for these averages.
- **One source.** This is a single v1 preprint, so the evidence is thin.

**B-d: the families tried, and what each assumes** (claim h4:31).
- **Offline configuration search** over the whole run: the oracle, which uses future data.
- **Orion's online controller.** It decides at experience boundaries: "only accessible after the completion of training
  for one experience". Its threshold decays with an index: "a time-dependent threshold, Thrt, that decreases over time",
  with Thr_t = Thr_0·e^(−δt).
- **Static presets** (MAX-A, MAX-P) and **latent replay** (LR) are baselines in the paper, not quoted here.

**B-e: CPU-testable?** **No.** The constraint is GPU memory on embedded boards, and the oracle takes 42 runs. SplitCIFAR10
under Avalanche on a CPU is feasible, but the gap there is small (a 1.15 % plasticity gap per the paper; not quoted as a
claim).

### h4-B5 · Resource-constrained federated / edge continual learning: **OPEN**

**B-a: the problem.** From Li et al. 2025, claim h4:32:
> This is unreasonable for FCL applications in real-world scenarios, where edge devices are primarily constrained by
> resources such as storage, computational budget, and label rate.

**B-b: open in 2025.** From Li et al., claim h4:33:
> we find that, under limited resource-constrained settings, existing FCL approaches, with no exception, fail to achieve
> the expected performance.

From the survey of non-centralised continual learning, claim h4:34:
> Unfortunately, resource constraint issues remain under-investigated.

**B-c: best result against the target.** Columns are CIFAR-10 A(f), CIFAR-10 mean A, CIFAR-100 A(f), CIFAR-100 mean A;
claim h4:35.

With sufficient resources (Table 2):
> FOT [2] 43.74±1.02 58.24±0.78 28.43±1.14 38.57±0.95

With extremely limited resources (Table 3):
> FedWeIT [62] 9.37±1.05 20.52±0.67 5.95±0.95 12.32±0.87

**Gap (agent).** On CIFAR-100 final accuracy, the best result under extremely limited resources is 5.95, against the best
with sufficient resources, 28.43. The gap is **22.48 points**. FOT itself drops to 4.85.

**Caveats.**
- **Three constraints at once.** The limited setting restricts memory, compute and label rate together, so the gap is not
  attributable to compute alone.
- **The target** is the same methods with sufficient resources, not a joint model.

**B-d: the families tried, and what each assumes** (claim h4:36).
- **The compute budget** is counted as "the gradient step during the model update", per round: clock-indexed.
- **Replay** of cached exemplars or of generated samples.
- **Methods designed for sufficient resources, then truncated.**

**B-e: CPU-testable?** **No.** The source used 1000+ GPU-hours.

### h4-B6 · Real-time streams (the stream does not wait): **not shown open**

**B-a: the problem.** From Ghunaim et al. (CVPR 2023), claim h4:37:
> a practical real-time evaluation of continual learning, in which the stream does not wait for the model to complete
> training before revealing the next data for predictions.

> a simple baseline outperforms state-of-the-art CL methods under this evaluation

**Why it is not shown open.**
- **No 2025–26 statement** that this is open was found in the sweep.
- **No target.** The numbers compare methods with a simple baseline, not with a target.

**B-d: what the setting assumes** (claim h4:38). The delay is charged by a method's training complexity relative to the
stream rate, which is a wall-clock assumption.

**B-e: CPU-testable?** **No.** CLOC has 39 million images.

### h4-B7 · Lifetime tuning in continual RL (the RL form of B2): **not shown open**

**B-a: the problem.** From Mesbahi et al. (ICML 2025), claim h4:39:
> The standard practice in RL is to assume unfettered access to the deployment environment for the full lifetime of the
> agent.

**B-b: open in 2025.** Claim h4:40:
> This is a position paper with a call to action: stop lifetime tuning your continual RL agents. We hope and expect future
> work to improve upon and replace k-percent tuning.

**Why it is not shown open.** The results are plotted only, so there are no B-c numbers to quote.

**B-e: CPU-testable?** **Yes.** The environments are DQN on small continuing tasks (Catch, Cartpole). But without quoted
numbers there is no target to test against.

## Judgement calls (for review)

1. **B1's target.** The target for B1 is the same method run with clear boundaries in the same paper, not a joint model.
   The Si-Blurry stream also changes task sizes and class overlap, so 7.14 points is an upper bound on the boundary cost.
2. **B2 is not shown open.** It is recorded as not shown open even though the 2025 open-question statements and
   method-specific losses (3.25–21.15 points) exist. The reason: the best realistic pipeline reaches its hindsight oracle
   (a gap of 0.64 points, inside the spreads, and 0). A reviewer who reads "open" as "the choice is unreliable for most
   methods" would flip this to open.
3. **B3 is not shown open.** It rests on one CIFAR-100 table (Cho et al.), where full retraining leads by at most 1.23
   points. The large-scale evidence (Prabhu et al.) could not be quoted numerically.
4. **B4 is open on one v1 preprint.** No spread is reported, and part of the benchmark grouping is the agent's reading.
5. **B5's gap mixes three constraints.** It restricts memory, compute and label rate together, and the target is the same
   methods with sufficient resources.
6. **Prior art for stage 2.**
   - Compute is already allocated by per-batch Fisher information (Seo et al., h4:25).
   - Self-detected boundaries by Fisher-subspace matching exist (FiUni, h4:7).
   - Online step-size adaptation by hypergradients exists (Michel et al.).
   - These bear directly on the declaration's forecast points on self-detected boundaries and change-indexed schedules.
