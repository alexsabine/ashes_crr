# OB1 stage 3, candidate C1: prior art for arc-clocked optimiser timescales at the stability gap (2026-09-30; R10)

Stage 3 of `Open_Bottlenecks/DECLARATION.md`, candidate **C1** of `Open_Bottlenecks/CRR_READING.md` (bottleneck h1-B6, the
stability gap). The search was for the **specific mechanism**, before any code.

**The mechanism searched (CRR_READING.md, C1).**
- Momentum and Adam moment averages decay **per unit of the learner's own change**, not per step.
- The change is the step's arc: √(2·KL) between successive predictive distributions on a fixed probe set, or its function- or
  parameter-space equivalent. The rule is β_t = β^(a_t / ā), where a_t is the step's arc and ā a running mean.
- The purpose: when a distribution shift makes the per-step change jump, stale moments are forgotten faster, without being told
  of the switch.
- The comparators: step-clock moments, and NGM-SGD's fast timescale (arXiv:2507.14056, the h1-B6 source in
  `docs/citations/ob1_h1_2026-09-30.md`).

**Reading tags** (the caller's definitions; full text in `claims_s3_c1.py`).
- **states:** the source publishes the mechanism. That means an optimiser timescale, trigger or budget indexed by the learner's
  own accumulated change (KL, function-space, parameter-space or Fisher distance, or its per-step equivalent), used for the
  named purpose: forgetting stale optimiser memory at a shift, or the stability gap.
- **close:** a change-driven trigger or timescale, but on a different quantity (loss, likelihood, surprise, entropy, gradient
  norm or alignment) or for a different purpose. A reset of optimiser memory at a supplied or scheduled boundary is also
  **close**, because it is the clock the candidate replaces.
- **bears:** relevant evidence that neither publishes nor negates the mechanism.
- **contradicts:** the source shows the mechanism, or its limiting case, fails or is inferior.

## A. The frontier comparator (h1-B6)

### 1. NGM-SGD (Rodriguez-Garcia, Ghosh & Ramaswamy)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2507.14056, current version **v3** (10 Aug 2026; v1 18 Jul 2025, v2 27 Jan 2026). Comment: "22 pages,
  11 figures, 6 table, 1 pseudo-code".
- **Integrity:** PDF (v3) `pdf_2507.14056v3.pdf` sha256 b40635433214c28a4a5b4f9254513a0143a83892c50a464bb155ee96d5d683ed,
  identical to the stage-1 copy. Abstract page sha256 7080bdccf85a93d6f05683691225fa73530d2c170610f739981a9aecff06b301.

> Standard optimizers such as momentum-SGD (MSGD) (Rumelhart et al., 1986) and Adam (Kingma and Ba, 2015) introduce implicit multi-timescale regularization through momentum or adaptive learning rates, yet they still exhibit stability gaps.

> we introduce a dynamic gain scaling mechanism as a two-timescale optimization technique

> suggests that this form of uncertainty can be quantified as the Shannon entropy of the network’s readout:

- **Reading: close** (c1:0).
  - The fast timescale is driven by a change signal read from the learner. That signal is the **entropy of the current
    readout**, an uncertainty level, not the size of the step.
  - It modulates a forward-pass gain, not the decay of the optimiser moments.
  - `CRR_READING.md` calls this timescale "loss-driven"; the fetched text says entropy-driven.

> This was implemented by providing a task-change signal that forces the optimizer to clear its internal state, thereby removing all previously accumulated velocity

> momentum resets indeed reduce part of the instability in most of the cases (see highlighted colors), confirming that accumulated velocity can overshoot under abrupt loss-landscape shifts.

> The reset variants of MSGD and Adam exhibit mixed behaviour across benchmarks, sometimes reducing stability gaps and sometimes increasing them, indicating that resetting the optimizer state at task boundaries can lead to unstable optimization under replay.

- **Reading: bears** (c1:1). This is the limiting case of C1, run as an oracle:
  - a total reset of the momentum state at the supplied switch reduces part of the gap under joint training;
  - it stays behind NGM-SGD;
  - under replay it is mixed, and sometimes worse.

> Applying the entropy signal directly to the learning rate supports stronger acquisition of new tasks, but results in the largest average stability gap (Figure 4c,d).

> Entropy-dependent step-size adaptation alone is therefore insufficient to reproduce the transition-stabilizing effect of gain modulation.

- **Reading: bears** (c1:2), a warning for C1. A change-driven optimiser adaptation that raises the effective step at the
  transition gave the largest gap of the arms tested.

## B. Momentum decay indexed by the learner's own motion

### 2. iKFAD (Karoni, Rajpal, Leimkuhler & Stoltz)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2602.00334, current version **v2** (26 Jun 2026; v1 30 Jan 2026). Comment: "31 pages, 13 figures.
  Accepted at ICML 2026".
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 34a27bdefa18dd372b2827569e55bbed1ae95cb81e1bedd5f30c9558772d064c. Abstract page sha256
  7f9c4d8b799066d7b24bc0a8221541a811a2bd728370389cb0360733dba6a864.
- **Mechanism:** the discrete momentum coefficient is β_n = exp(−(γ + ξ_n)δt) (their eq. 10). Here ξ is an exponentially
  weighted average of the squared momenta, which is the squared rate of parameter change.

> we adopt a continuous-time formulation to introduce individual, adaptive momentum coefficients regulated by the kinetic energy of each model parameter.

> Having a friction or momentum coefficient that dynamically adapts based on the kinetic energy of the system would allow us to increase the damping when momenta are getting too high and decrease it when momenta are lower.

> We see that ξ is an exponentially weighted average of the squares of past momenta, i.e. kinetic energies.

- **Reading: close** (c1:3). This is the nearest source in **quantity**: the momentum memory shortens when the learner's own
  parameter motion has been large. It differs from C1 in three ways:
  - **quantity:** squared parameter velocity per coordinate, not the step's arc or KL relative to its running mean;
  - **form:** added friction, not β^(a/ā);
  - **purpose:** oscillation control in stationary training. No shift and no stability gap are studied.

## C. Moment decay shortened by a spike ratio

### 4. Kourkoutas-β (Kassinos)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2508.12996, current version **v2** (20 Aug 2025; v1 18 Aug 2025). Comment: "54 pages, 8 figures, 19
  tables".
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 91c37c02ff8a54896ad96de5c473e5fd18d2f77d1bf587b5dbff3bf9956881d6. Abstract page sha256
  9805127ea0a5da85687ebae99f081e58ac038aedfd5899700f2bb38fd3e669af.

> replaces the fixed second-moment discount β2 with a layer-wise dynamic value driven by a bounded “sunspike” ratio: the current pooled gradient norm divided by an EMA (with coefficient α) of past norms, squashed to [0, 1).

> Large spikes lower β2 toward β2,min to react quickly; calm phases keep it near β2,max to smooth updates.

- **Reading: close** (c1:5). This is the nearest source in **form**: an Adam discount lowered when a current magnitude jumps
  relative to its own running mean. It differs from C1 in three ways:
  - the magnitude is the gradient norm, not the learner's step;
  - only β2 is adapted;
  - the setting is bursty gradients in PDE surrogates and sequence tasks, not task switches.

## Is the mechanism published?

**Not as a whole, in this sweep.**
- No source was found in which an optimiser's moment averages decay per unit of the learner's own change (a per-step KL between
  successive predictive distributions, or a function-, parameter- or Fisher-space step), used to forget stale moments at a
  distribution shift or to reduce the stability gap.
- Every ingredient is published separately:
  - a moment timescale shortened by a jump relative to its own running mean (Kourkoutas-β, on the gradient norm);
  - momentum decay driven by the learner's own parameter velocity (iKFAD and FAD, for oscillation control);
  - an EMA memory that decays per unit of a per-step KL to drop stale statistics at a shift (MECTA, on BatchNorm feature
    statistics);
  - self-triggered momentum restarts after regime changes (Sahu et al., stated as a heuristic);
  - the same done at supplied or scheduled boundaries (Adam-Rel, Asadi et al., SPAM, and NGM-SGD's oracle reset).
- By the caller's rule, **PARTLY REDUNDANT** (no states; 19 close).

**The closest quotes:**
- MECTA (ICLR 2023): "where the parameter β ∈[0, 1] governs the memory length and therefore works as the forget gate." / "where
  D(·, ·) is a properly-defined distance function measuring distribution shifts."
- Kourkoutas-β (arXiv:2508.12996 v2): "Large spikes lower β2 toward β2,min to react quickly; calm phases keep it near β2,max to
  smooth updates."
- iKFAD (arXiv:2602.00334 v2): "we adopt a continuous-time formulation to introduce individual, adaptive momentum coefficients
  regulated by the kinetic energy of each model parameter."
- Sahu, Hogan, Wells (arXiv:2601.12238 v5): "suggests resetting the momentum buffer after regime changes. One practical
  heuristic monitors the alignment" / "Analyzing such restart rules rigorously, particularly whether they provably mitigate
  stale momentum under nonstationarity, is an interesting direction for future work."
- NGM-SGD (arXiv:2507.14056 v3), on the oracle limit: "momentum resets indeed reduce part of the instability in most of the
  cases (see highlighted colors), confirming that accumulated velocity can overshoot under abrupt loss-landscape shifts."

**What is missing, which is the part CRR predicts** (for the investigator's decision under the declaration's rule, "a PARTLY
REDUNDANT candidate goes on only if the missing part is the part CRR predicts"):
- The index is the **learner's own step** (arc or KL between successive predictive distributions on a probe set), not the
  input statistics (MECTA), the gradient norm (Kourkoutas-β), the parameter velocity (iKFAD) or the gradient–momentum alignment
  (Sahu et al., CAdam).
- The object is the **optimiser moments**, and the setting is the **stability gap** at task switches.

Whether that difference is the CRR-proper part, or a choice of signal inside a published design family, is the investigator's
call. A fair Phase A gate would need these arms:
- MECTA-form KL-gated decay applied to the moments;
- Kourkoutas-β-form ratio gating on the gradient norm;
- iKFAD;
- an alignment-triggered restart;
- the oracle reset.

"Not found" is never "novel".

## Judgement calls, for the investigator's review

- **MECTA read close, not states.** This is the call that could move the grade.
  - **For states:** MECTA's EMA forget gate is an exponential decay per unit of a per-step KL divergence, used to drop stale
    memory at a distribution shift without a boundary. That is C1's functional form and purpose.
  - **For close:**
    - the KL is between the running and the incoming batch **feature statistics** (the data seen at a layer), not the
      learner's own change;
    - the memory is BatchNorm statistics, not optimiser moments;
    - the setting is test-time adaptation, not the stability gap.
  - The caller's "states" names the learner's own accumulated change and the optimiser. A reviewer who weighs the form over the
    signal would grade REDUNDANT.
- **NGM-SGD's driver.** `CRR_READING.md` describes NGM-SGD's fast timescale as loss-driven. The fetched v3 text says it is
  driven by the **Shannon entropy of the readout**. The candidate's comparator should be restated accordingly.
- **The oracle reset and Entropy-LR are read bears, not contradicts.**
  - Both are the nearest evidence against C1's direction:
    - the total reset at a supplied switch is mixed, and sometimes worse, under replay;
    - an entropy-scaled learning rate gave the largest gap.
  - Neither is the arc-clocked decay itself, so neither shows that mechanism fails.
  - A stricter reader could grade c1:1 contradicts for the replay regime.
- **Obis (TU Delft) and Kang & Lee point against the premise.**
  - Obis finds that higher momentum deepens the gap but shortens it.
  - Kang & Lee find that keeping Adam's second moments across tasks reduces forgetting.
  - So "forget stale moments faster at a switch" is not an established improvement; the gate must be able to close on it.
- **The window.** O'Donoghue & Candès (arXiv v1 2012) and AMoC (2020) are older than the 2023–2026 priority window. They are
  kept because they are the classical form of trajectory-triggered momentum restart.
- **Not reached, not graded:**
  - MECTA via OpenReview (read from the NSF copy instead);
  - OpenReview PDFs generally;
  - "Learning Rate Scheduling via KL-Divergence" (ResearchGate only).
