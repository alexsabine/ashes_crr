# OB1 stage 3, candidate C2: arc-triggered refresh for plasticity loss under task switches, targeted prior art (2026-09-30; R10)

Stage 3 of `Open_Bottlenecks/DECLARATION.md` for candidate **C2** of `Open_Bottlenecks/CRR_READING.md` (bottleneck h3-B1,
`docs/citations/ob1_h3_2026-09-30.md`). The declaration's rule: REDUNDANT if the mechanism is published, PARTLY REDUNDANT if a

**The mechanism searched (CRR_READING.md, C2).** A refresh (full reset, shrink-and-perturb, soft reset toward the
initialisation, or re-initialisation) is **triggered when the learner's own accumulated change since the last refresh crosses
a unit**: the arc (Σ√(2·KL) between successive predictive distributions on a fixed probe set), or a function, parameter or
Fisher distance, or a divergence budget. It replaces a fixed clock schedule and a supplied task boundary (the Oracle). The
purpose is plasticity loss under task switches in continual RL (C-CHAIN's continual Gym Control chain) and in supervised
continual learning.

## A. Change-triggered refresh (graded close)

### 1. RDumb++ (Mishra, 2026)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2601.15544, current version **v1** (22 Jan 2026; the only version). OpenReview lists it at "TTU at
  ICLR 2026 (Main)" (forum lWu9V8z4cf; PDF NOT REACHED, 403). Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF `pdf_2601.15544v1.pdf` sha256 6fd2de41f7329e7194c674b5830bcf2dbf6e18e3870bb8f3bf92ecd103f0ecab;
  abstract page sha256 b2d311abea59f78b09053f369b7e93510c0b7d36d306adbb4b499a04a170c545.
- **Mechanism:** continual test-time adaptation (CCC benchmark). A full reset, or a soft reset θ ← λθ0 + (1−λ)θ, fires when a
  standardised drift score exceeds k. One score is the per-sample KL between the model's current predictive distribution and an
  EMA reference, z-scored against EMA mean and variance.

> RDumb depends on a fixed reset interval (e.g., every 1000 steps), which is fundamentally non-adaptive.

> Can we detect distribution drift during inference, and reset the model only when necessary?

> For each incoming sample, we compute the KL divergence between the current distribution pt and the reference qt:

> KL divergence quantifies how much the model’s predictive belief has shifted relative to its historical expectation.

> Upon detecting drift, RDumb++ applies one of two reset strategies depending on the model variant.

> RDumb++ triggers a reset exactly when a statistically significant deviation is detected.

- **Reading: close** (claim c2:0). The nearest in form: a KL-of-predictions trigger for a reset or shrink-to-init, replacing
  a clock. Not states: the trigger is a spike detector on the instantaneous KL against a running reference, not the
  accumulated own change since the last refresh crossing a unit; the purpose is collapse in test-time adaptation, not
  plasticity loss under task switches.

### 3. ABR: Adaptive and Balanced Re-initialization (Wang et al., ICASSP 2026)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2602.06328 **v1** (6 Feb 2026), comment "Accepted in ICASSP 2026". **Integrity:** PDF sha256
  bab274725bb31dd116e215e54f49923128777877b2d9c72265efc4dc0cbf2ed6; abstract page
  108378ea6e99d9292b2530b7251a3b53c64602611c7aa11a2f52bfbe06abb5df.
- **Mechanism:** the "label flip" is the confidence-weighted count of predicted-class changes between the model at step t and
  at step t−1, EMA-smoothed (α = 0.5). Re-initialisation fires when the slope from the running minimum of that curve is large.

> ABR performs weight re-initialization using adaptive intervals. The adaptive interval is determined based on the change in label flip.

> We refer to the predicted class differences between the two models as the label flip [9, 10] at the current time.

> If we perform re-initialization immediately after the sharp increase in the label flip, performance would be better preserved during later adaptation.

> resets the model by a fixed time interval tuned using the related validation dataset.

- **Reading: close, borderline** (c2:2). The trigger quantity **is** the learner's own per-step functional change (a
  discrete analogue of the step's arc on the test batch), and it replaces a tuned fixed interval. Graded close, not states,
  because the rule fires on a rise in the per-step change (a change point), not on the accumulated change since the last
  refresh crossing a unit, and because the purpose is long-run test-time adaptation, not plasticity loss under task switches
  in RL. The investigator may read this as nearer.

## Is the mechanism published?

**PARTLY REDUNDANT** (by the rule: no source states the mechanism; 13 are close).

**Published.** Three parts of C2 are in print:
- **Replacing a clock-scheduled or Oracle-timed refresh with a self-detected trigger.** This is published in test-time
  adaptation (RDumb++, ASR, ABR, Effective Restoration), in plasticity work (Soft Resets, SeRe, SNR), in continual RL (the
  boundary-free task-shift detector, workshop only; tabular adaptive restarts) and in Bayesian online learning (VBS, BONE).
- **Triggering on a KL of the learner's own predictive distribution.** RDumb++ triggers a full reset, or a shrink toward the
  initialisation, on a standardised KL of the model's predictions:
  > For each incoming sample, we compute the KL divergence between the current distribution pt and the reference qt:

  > RDumb++ triggers a reset exactly when a statistically significant deviation is detected.
- **Triggering on the learner's own per-step functional change.** ABR sets re-initialisation intervals by the label flip
  between the model at t and at t−1:
  > ABR performs weight re-initialization using adaptive intervals. The adaptive interval is determined based on the change in label flip.

  > We refer to the predicted class differences between the two models as the label flip [9, 10] at the current time.
- **Accumulating a statistic until it crosses a threshold, to drive re-initialisation for plasticity loss.** SeRe does this,
  though on prediction error:
  > This design accumulates the absolute prediction error, ensuring that we detect both underestimation and overestimation of rewards

**Not found in the sweep.** No source was found that does all of the following at once:
- triggers a refresh when the learner's **accumulated** own change since the **last refresh** (arc, KL path, function,
  parameter or Fisher distance, or a divergence budget) crosses a unit;
- does so for plasticity loss under task switches in continual RL or supervised continual learning;
- compares it with a clock-scheduled refresh at an equal number of refreshes, and with the Oracle.

**Is the missing part the part CRR predicts?** The missing part is the accumulation-to-a-unit of own change, applied to
plasticity in continual RL. That is the CRR-specific element (D6/H-L5: index by own accumulated change). The nearest
published triggers differ in two ways:
- **Spike or change-point detectors.** RDumb++ and ABR fire on a jump in own change, which is the "arc per step jumps at a
  switch" reading that CRR_READING.md itself gives.
- **Accumulators on another quantity.** SeRe accumulates prediction error.

So under the declaration's rule, the candidate can go on only if the investigator judges the accumulated-unit trigger
distinct from a change-point trigger on the same quantity. A gate would need both as baselines: RDumb++-style KL z-score
resets and ABR-style per-step-change resets, beside the clock and the Oracle.

**Risks found in the sweep (bears, not contradicts):**
- **False fires within a task.** Learned drift detectors fire within tasks and degrade learning (c2:8, c2:23).
- **Change triggers can lose to schedules.** Adaptive restarts can lose to scheduled ones in constructed environments (c2:9).
- **Resets can be the wrong lever.** Under gradual drift, resets lose to consolidation (c2:22).

No source was found that shows an own-change-triggered refresh failing.
