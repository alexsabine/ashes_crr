# OB1 stage 3, candidate C3: arc-triggered consolidation in boundary-free continual learning (2026-09-30; R10)

Stage 3 of `Open_Bottlenecks/DECLARATION.md`: the targeted prior-art search for candidate **C3** of
`Open_Bottlenecks/CRR_READING.md` (bottlenecks h4-B1 and h1-B1). Written by the stage-3 agent after the stage-2 reading was
fixed; no code for C3 exists.

**The mechanism searched for (C3, as CRR_READING.md words it).**
- **Frontier:** consolidation or freezing at supplied boundaries, "every I steps", or at shifts detected over a fixed batch
  window or sample budget.
- **CRR:** consolidate (freeze prompts or experts, snapshot, anchor a regulariser, add an expert) when the learner's **own
  accumulated change** since the last consolidation (the arc: KL between successive predictive distributions on a probe
  set, or a Fisher, function or parameter distance) crosses a unit.
- **Prediction (not tested here):** on a boundary-free (Si-Blurry-type) stream, arc-triggered consolidation is closer to the
  clear-boundary result than count-triggered consolidation, at an equal number of consolidations.

**Reading tags** (fixed by the stage-3 brief before the search):
- **states:** the source publishes the mechanism: a consolidation trigger, timescale or budget indexed by the learner's own
  accumulated change (KL, function, parameter or Fisher distance, or its per-step equivalent), used for consolidation.
- **close:** a change-driven trigger or timescale, but with a different quantity (the loss, a loss deviation, the data
  distribution, the Fisher of the incoming data) or for a different purpose (a reset, communication, merge gating, a
  regulariser's strength, compute allocation).
- **bears:** relevant evidence that neither publishes nor negates the mechanism.
- **contradicts:** the source shows the mechanism fails or is inferior.
- **Grade rule:** REDUNDANT if any source states; PARTLY REDUNDANT if any is close; else NOT FOUND IN THE SWEEP. "Not found" is
  never "novel".

## A. Task-free consolidation triggered by the loss

### 1. Task-Free Continual Learning (Aljundi, Kelchtermans, Tuytelaars, CVPR 2019)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:1812.03596, current version **v3** (19 Aug 2019), comment "Accepted as a conference paper in CVPR 2019".
- **Integrity:** PDF `pdf/1812.03596v3.pdf` sha256 8cffbbd6782a155c687fe6c50f142d3d66bde60a90b954e7bf8c669eff23c3c2; abstract
  page sha256 c03b7d91799c99b7bcff2720fb1474a285573195f1b071e10a446776483038f6.
- **Mechanism:** MAS importance weights (the consolidation anchor) are updated in a stream without boundaries whenever the
  loss window shows a plateau after a peak.

> In the online case, the data is streaming without knowledge of a task’s start or end (i.e. when distribution shifts occur). So we need a mechanism to determine when to update the importance weights.

> Whenever the model is in such a stable area, it’s a good time to consolidate the knowledge by updating the importance weights.

> We monitor the mean and the variance of the losses in this window and trigger an importance weight update whenever they are both lower than a given threshold.

- **Reading: close.** A self-triggered consolidation in a task-free stream, but the trigger is the loss (level and variance in a
  sliding window), not the change accumulated since the last consolidation. (Claim c3:0.)

### 2. Online-LoRA (Wei, Li, Marculescu, WACV 2025)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2411.05663, current version **v1** (8 Nov 2024), comment "WACV 2025".
- **Integrity:** PDF `pdf/2411.05663v1.pdf` sha256 66e0b74bd24886777628416255bdffa2d2e25a28b3e686fcac4317e5f284bbf2; abstract
  page sha256 3a8be207da1ac2b6fa50750beaf42c77644183082a88e524ba7cad6948dd5bcf.

> There brings the need for a mechanism to determine when to initialize the new LoRA parameters.

> At these plateaus, it is best to consolidate the learned knowledge by freezing the current LoRA weights and initializing a pair of new, trainable LoRA parameters.

- **Reading: close.** This is C3's action (freeze the current adapter, add a new one, re-estimate importance) in a task-free

- **Reading: close.** Expert switching or creation is triggered by a significant deviation of the smoothed loss. (Claim c3:2.)

### 9. FRCL (Titsias, Schwarz, Matthews, Pascanu, Teh, ICLR 2020)

## C. The learner's own change as the quantity, for another purpose

### 10. RDumb++ (Mishra, 2026)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2601.15544, current version **v1** (22 Jan 2026). The same abstract appears on OpenReview as TTU at
  ICLR 2026 (Main) and as a CAO poster (search JSON).
- **Integrity:** PDF `pdf/2601.15544v1.pdf` sha256 6fd2de41f7329e7194c674b5830bcf2dbf6e18e3870bb8f3bf92ecd103f0ecab; abstract
  page sha256 b2d311abea59f78b09053f369b7e93510c0b7d36d306adbb4b499a04a170c545.

> RDumb depends on a fixed reset interval (e.g., every 1000 steps), which is fundamentally non-adaptive.

> KL divergence quantifies how much the model’s predictive belief has shifted relative to its historical expectation.

> These mechanisms allow the model to detect when accumulated adaptation becomes harmful and to recover before prediction collapse occurs.

- **Reading: close.** Nearest in quantity and in the clock-versus-change contrast: a step clock ("every 1000 steps") is
  replaced by a KL on the model's own predictive distribution.
  - The score is a standardised per-sample KL spike against an unspecified "reference probability" (EMA z-score > k). It is
    not an arc accumulated since the last event.
  - The action is a reset (C2's purpose) in test-time adaptation, not consolidation.
  - (Claim c3:9.)

### 14. Dynamic model averaging (Kamp, Adilova, Sicking et al., 2018)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:1807.03210, current version **v2** (13 Nov 2018); venue not verified on the day.
- **Integrity:** PDF `pdf/1807.03210v2.pdf` sha256 ee06c5733624996d98da358e40e28ed9648ac2d335b436a5ad91f8b6f583136f; abstract

  - The distance is Euclidean in parameters, not a KL arc.

## Is the mechanism published?

**Grade under the brief's rule: PARTLY REDUNDANT.** No source was found that states it; 17 are close.

**Every part of C3 is published, in different places.**
- **Task-free consolidation with a self-triggered event** (the action):
  - Aljundi et al. 2019: "Whenever the model is in such a stable area, it’s a good time to consolidate the knowledge by
    updating the importance weights".
  - Online-LoRA: "At these plateaus, it is best to consolidate the learned knowledge by freezing the current LoRA weights
    and initializing a pair of new, trainable LoRA parameters".
  - Both trigger on the **loss**, not on accumulated change.
- **A trigger on the learner's own accumulated change since the last event** (the trigger structure):
  - Kamp et al. 2018: "This operator only communicates when the model divergence exceeds a divergence threshold ∆", with
    the reference "the average model from the last synchronization step".
  - Event-triggered FL: "communication at a device is triggered once the instantaneous local model is sufficiently
    different than the outdated local model".
  - Both are for **communication**, and both use a Euclidean parameter distance.
- **The learner's own predictive KL replacing a step clock** (the quantity and the contrast):
  - RDumb++: "RDumb depends on a fixed reset interval (e.g., every 1000 steps), which is fundamentally non-adaptive", and
    "KL divergence quantifies how much the model’s predictive belief has shifted relative to its historical expectation".
  - This is a per-sample z-scored spike, used for a **reset**.
- **A KL budget on own change** (STABLE: "KL divergence, quantifying distributional shift between the base and adapted
  models"), gating merges.
- **Own divergence indexing an anchor's strength and an update timescale** (PeTTA: "Sensing the Divergence of θt"; "a pair of
  (λt, αt) is adaptively chosen at each step").

**What was not found:** a consolidation event (freeze, snapshot or anchor, new expert) in a boundary-free stream, triggered
when the arc accumulated since the last consolidation crosses a unit. The arc here is a sum of per-step KL or Fisher lengths,
or a function or parameter distance from the last consolidation snapshot. This combination was not found in 117 arXiv
queries, 11 OpenReview searches and 7 web searches. Not found is not novel: the combination is one step from Kamp et al.'s
trigger, applied to Online-LoRA's action.

## Judgement calls, for the investigator's review

- **Kamp et al. and event-triggered FL are read close, not states.** Their trigger is C3's trigger almost exactly: the
  accumulated own parameter distance since the last event's snapshot, against a threshold, replacing a period. The brief
  requires "used for the named purpose", and their purpose is communication.
  - A reviewer who reads the mechanism as the trigger alone would grade C3 **REDUNDANT** on these two.
  - The stage-2 rule ("a PARTLY REDUNDANT candidate goes on only if the missing part is the part CRR predicts") then turns on
    this: what is missing is the **use** of that trigger for consolidation against forgetting, not the trigger itself.
- **RDumb++ is read close, not states.** It uses the learner's own predictive KL instead of a step clock, but as a standardised
  spike, not an accumulated arc, and for resets. It bears more directly on **C2** (arc-triggered refresh) than on C3. The
  investigator may want it in C2's dossier.
- **Loss-triggered task-free consolidation (Aljundi, Online-LoRA, TAME, OSAKA) is read close.**
  - Under the brief, the loss is a "different quantity".
  - But in these methods the loss is the learner's own signal, not the data's, and a loss peak is where the arc per step
    is largest.
  - So the fair baseline for any C3 gate is **Online-LoRA's loss-plateau trigger at an equal number of consolidations**, not
    only a count trigger. On the brief's rule, C3 must beat the loss-plateau trigger to add anything.
- **The FiUni lead reads close.** Its Fisher is that of the incoming data window under the frozen backbone (K-FAC factors),
  not the learner's own movement. The window is a count, which is the stage-1 note (h4:7) confirmed.
- **The Seo et al. lead reads close.** The per-batch Fisher information is a per-step proxy for own change, but it is used to
  allocate compute per batch, with no accumulation and no consolidation event.
- **Cautions, not contradictions.**
  - The RLC 2026 workshop abstract (c3:21) reports that a learner-internal drift detector "suffer[s] from frequent within-task
    fires", while a data-side statistical detector did not.
  - LargeMonitor (c3:19, withdrawn) argues that training-coupled cues "can inevitably drift or require per-dataset threshold
    tuning".
  - Both predict a failure mode for an arc threshold: the arc also accumulates within a task. A gate for C3 should include a
    stationary stream with a long single task, where arc-triggering must not fire more often than count-triggering.
- **TIDE is the open item.** Its abstract claims expansion "only at the moments they are needed", within tasks. The full text
  was NOT REACHED (OpenReview challenge; no arXiv version). If its trigger is accumulated parameter or Fisher change, the grade
  could move to REDUNDANT. The investigator should retrieve it by hand before stage 4.
- **Scope of "consolidation".** C3 includes "anchor a regulariser". ProRL re-anchors the KL reference at a snapshot of the
  online policy, which is consolidation in C3's sense, but on a schedule or on validation stagnation. It is read close, not
  bears.
