# ROB1 citation checks, family R2: robot learning (2026-09-30; R10)

This is stage 1 of `Robotics/DECLARATION.md`, family **R2**: generalist and foundation robot policies (vision-language-action
models, VLAs), lifelong and continual robot learning, forgetting on adaptation, generalisation under distribution shift and
across embodiments, sim-to-real transfer, data scarcity and cost, long-horizon tasks, and on-robot inference latency. The
dossier records bottlenecks only. It makes no CRR reading; that is stage 2, after the double check (stage 1b).

**How the sources were handled.** Every source was fetched on the day (2026-09-30) through the session proxy:
- **arXiv:** the abstract page, for the current version, the version history, the comment and the journal reference, and the
  PDF of the current version.
- **One web page:** UC Berkeley News, 27 Aug 2025 (an interview with Ken Goldberg about his Science Robotics papers). The
  Science Robotics paper itself was not reachable (see Not reached).

Quotes are copied from the text that pymupdf 1.28.2 extracted from the fetched PDF, or, for the web page, from a stdlib
HTML-to-text extraction (script and style removed, block tags as line breaks). Only two things are changed: whitespace is
normalised, and hyphens at line breaks are rejoined (or kept, for true compounds such as "real-world"). Table rows are quoted
as the extractor emitted them, cell by cell in reading order. Fetched files are held outside the repository, under
`/tmp/claude-0/rob_src/r2/` (`src/` the fetched files, `txt/` the extracted text, `search/` the saved result pages). Their
sha256 is recorded below and in `/tmp/claude-0/rob_src/r2/SHA256SUMS.txt` (132 files).

**Verification.** The claims are in `Robotics/checks/claims_r2.py`: 71 claims and 145 quotes, over 8 bottlenecks. They were
checked with a scratch copy of `Open_Bottlenecks/checks/verify.py`, pointed at `claims_r2.py` with root `/tmp/claude-0/rob_src`
(`Robotics/checks/verify.py` did not exist yet):
- **Result:** "quotes found verbatim: 145 of 145 (claims 71; raw files 30, missing 0); not found 0; missing 0".
- **Negative control:** one table cell was altered (Jetson-PI Table 4, "0.70" changed to "0.80"). The check then read 144 of

**Rules for the numbers.**
- Every number below is the paper's own, on its own protocol.
- Numbers from different papers are not comparable. Where a gap uses a target from another source, it is flagged
  CROSS-PAPER or CROSS-SOURCE (r2-B1 supporting, r2-B6, r2-B8).
- The gaps are the agent's arithmetic on quoted numbers.
- A description of a mechanism or setting is the agent's reading of the fetched text. The quotes are the evidence.
- Code links were **not checked**.
- **Fairness.** Where a source reports the target reached, the claim is recorded (role c) and the note says so: forgetting
  with a 20% replay buffer (r2:7), replay-free routing within 2.7 points of its oracle (r2:10), appearance perturbations
  (r2:25), sim-to-real for locomotion (r2:43), narrow long-horizon folding at 95% (r2:57), and desktop-GPU control rates
  (r2:65).

## Bottlenecks

The open test: B-a, B-b and B-c must all be present. All eight have them. Three carry contrary evidence that the double check
should weigh first: r2-B1 (forgetting is closed at a 20% replay budget), r2-B6 (possible misframing, weak target) and r2-B7
(narrow long-horizon tasks reach 95%).

### r2-B1. Forgetting in lifelong robot learning (open under a small replay budget; contested)

- **B-a** (LIBERO, 2023, r2:1):
  > the sequential nature of LLDM suggests that even minor forgetting over successive steps can potentially lead to a total failure in execution.

  LIBERO names sequential finetuning and multitask learning as the "lower bound and upper bound for lifelong learning
  algorithms".
- **B-b** (2026). LifeLong-RFT (r2:2): "directly applying SFT still results in severe catastrophic forgetting"; CRL-VLA
  (r2:3): "balancing stability (retaining old skills) and plasticity (learning new ones) remains a formidable challenge";
  PHASER (r2:4): "inevitably triggers severe catastrophic forgetting"; the self-evolving humanoids survey (r2:5): "The central
  challenge is therefore catastrophic forgetting"; DMPEL (TMLR 2026, r2:6): "we still observe a gradual decline in performance
  as the number of tasks increases"; RoboFolDeX (r2:15), on real robots: "continued training frequently causes the policy to
  forget previously acquired action behaviors".
- **B-c.** The best source is also the strongest contrary evidence. Liu, Kim, Liu, Liu & Zhu (Mar 2026, r2:7), LIBERO-10,
  experience replay, NBT (0 = no forgetting):

  | Replay per task | Pi0 NBT | GR00T NBT |
  |---|---|---|
  | 10 samples (0.2%) | 0.529 | 0.766 |
  | 100 samples (2%) | 0.229 | 0.525 |
  | 1000 samples (20%) | −0.068 | 0.059 |

  At 20% the target is reached. At 2% the best is 0.229 short of zero forgetting. The paper's text says ER "often achieving
  zero forgetting even with a small replay data size (e.g., 2% ...)"; on LIBERO-10 its own Table 5 does not show that.
  Supporting results:
  - PHASER Table 1 (r2:8): final success after 10 LIBERO-Long tasks 85.8 (NBT 10.0) with OpenVLA-OFT-7B; 48.6 and 51.6
    (NBT 11.1, 12.4) with 3B backbones whose action heads are largely trained from scratch. Against OpenVLA-OFT's multitask
    91.1 on LIBERO-Long (from LifeLong-RFT Table II; CROSS-PAPER) the gap is 5.3.
  - LifeLong-RFT Table IV (r2:9): LIBERO-Long continual AUC 64.5, NBT 12.8; the same model's multitask score is 89.0, but
    with 50 rather than 10 demonstrations per task, so 24.5 is an upper bound on the gap. Real world: NBT 6.1, AUC 75.9.
  - OrthoSkillVLA (r2:10), replay-free, three skills: final success 83.50 against oracle routing 86.2 (gap 2.7), NBT 0.13.
- **B-d.** The method families, and the assumption each makes:
  - **Replay** writes stored samples at the task boundary: "After completing policy learning for a task, ER stores a portion
    of that task's data" (r2:11). Budgets are fixed per task (5 demonstrations in LifeLong-RFT; 2% or 20% in Liu et al.).
  - **Regularisation** (EWC) computes importance per finished task (r2:11).
  - **Expert libraries** (DMPEL, r2:12) train "10 epochs on each arriving task", replay coefficients "after each task", and
    prune "every 10 tasks": a boundary and a task-count clock. Some baselines need "oracle task identifiers".
  - **Phase-aware replay** (PHASER, r2:13) names a time assumption: "standard ER relies on a uniform sampling strategy that
    fundamentally misaligns with the temporal characteristics of robotic manipulation". Its phase boundaries come from human
    annotation or from change points in the action signal.
  - **The deployed default** avoids the problem: controllers are frozen after deployment, so "neither further learning nor
    forgetting occurs" (r2:14).
- **B-e.** No, on the named results (3B-7B VLAs on GPUs). LIBERO's own small BC baselines with robosuite rollouts could run a
  reduced proxy on a CPU in hours.

### r2-B2. Erosion of pretrained VLM capabilities when fine-tuning into a VLA (open)

- **B-a** (VLM2VLA, Sep 2025, r2:16):
  > learning to produce actions often diminishes the VLM's foundational reasoning and multimodal understanding, hindering generalization to novel scenarios, instruction following, and semantic understanding.
- **B-b** (Anchor-Align, arXiv v2 28 Sep 2026, r2:17): "BC finetuning progressively overwrites the pretrained
  representations", and "BC finetuning corrupts the very prior that makes VLMs worth adapting, leading to two failure modes
  that persist even under good training practices". LifeLong-RFT (2026, r2:18): SFT "is prone to catastrophic forgetting".
- **B-c** (VLM2VLA Table 1, r2:19). The target is the base VLM, Gemma-3-12B-IT. The best retention method, VLM2VLA:

  | Benchmark | Base VLM | VLM2VLA | Gap |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | MMMU | 46.0 | 42.7 | 3.3 |
  | OCRBench | 75.0 | 63.9 | 11.1 |
  | MMB-en | 76.9 | 68.5 | 8.4 |
  | RealWorldQA | 50.6 | 43.3 | 7.3 |

  It is below the base on 9 of 12 benchmarks and ahead on 3 (MMStar, MME, ChartQA). A co-trained generalist VLA (π0.5, 3b)
  scores 0.3 to 27.0 on the text benchmarks.
- **B-d.** Low-rank adaptation of a frozen backbone, without co-training (r2:20); anchoring "from a frozen VLM copy", a frozen
  backbone, per-module proximity scheduling, and co-training with Knowledge Insulation (r2:21). All take the pretrained VLM as
  the fixed state to preserve; co-training adds a mixture ratio to tune.
- **B-e.** No. A 12B fine-tune and 12 VQA benchmarks need GPUs.

### r2-B3. Generalisation of VLA policies under distribution shift (open on spatial shifts)

- **B-a** (LIBERO-Plus, v3 Dec 2025, r2:22): "models exhibit extreme sensitivity to perturbation factors, including camera
  viewpoints and robot initial states, with performance dropping from 95% to below 30% under modest perturbations."
- **B-b.** LIBERO-PRO (v2 May 2026, r2:23): "although existing models achieve over 90% accuracy under the standard LIBERO
  evaluation, their performance collapses to 0.0% under our generalized setting." Colosseum V2 (RA-L 2026, r2:24): "their
  overall task performance often degrades under distribution shifts", and "Pose-Randomization remains a primary challenge for
  both models."
- **B-c** (Anchor-Align Tables 1 and 5, Sep 2026, r2:25). The target is the same model unperturbed on LIBERO-Spatial,
  **98.4**:

  | Perturbation | Best (Anchor-Align) | Gap |
  |---|---|---|
  | LIBERO-PRO position swap | 22.6 | 75.8 |
  | LIBERO-Plus robot initial state | 59.1 | 39.3 |
  | LIBERO-PRO mean | 71.9 | 26.5 |
  | LIBERO-Plus mean | 90.3 | 8.1 |

  Appearance axes are nearly closed (background 99.6, lighting 99.0). LIBERO-Plus's own Table 1 (r2:26): OpenVLA-OFT 97.1
  unperturbed, 37.2 under robot-initial-state change; the best robot-initial-state cell is 50.3 (UniVLA, unperturbed 95.2).
  On Colosseum V2's 28 new tasks (r2:27) even unperturbed success is low (29.2% and 44.5% for ACT, 21.2% and 12.6% for π0.5),
  and pose randomisation removes a further 12.9 to 51.4 points.
- **B-d.** Widen the training distribution ("over 20,000 successful trajectories", r2:28), which assumes the shifts can be
  enumerated in advance; preserve pretrained representations (r2:21). The evaluation assumption is named too: "the evaluation
  tasks are identical to the training tasks" in the standard protocol (r2:29).
- **B-e.** No. Evaluating 0.5B-7B VLAs over thousands of rollouts needs a GPU; the simulator itself runs on a CPU.

### r2-B4. Transfer to new robot embodiments (open)

- **B-a** (VLA review, IEEE Access, Oct 2025, r2:30): "transferring policies across embodiments remains a major challenge."
- **B-b.** The Embodiment Gap (TMLR, Aug 2026, r2:31): "Substantial work still remains in turning shared structure into
  stable execution on the target robot." RoboFolDeX (2026, r2:32): cross-embodiment differences "lead to severe action-space
  interference and catastrophic forgetting."
- **B-c** (ZETA Tables 3 and 4, Sep 2026, r2:33). Strict zero-shot transfer: the target robot is absent from all training.
  The metric is mean task progress (partial credit). The target is progress on the source embodiment.

  | Setting | Source embodiment | Full embodiment change | Gap |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Simulation | 91.5 | 60.4 | 31.1 |
  | Real world (140 rollouts per model) | 100.0 | 81.9 | 18.1 |
- **B-d** (r2:34, r2:35). A shared end-effector delta action interface (+about 15 points), source-embodiment diversity (+18),
  auxiliary co-training (+7), and exposure: "adding only 5% target-embodiment data during pretraining improves average
  target-embodiment progress by 13.4 percentage points". The Embodiment Gap names three directions: shared semantics and
  perception, shared data and interfaces, learned correspondence.
- **B-e.** No. The study pretrains a π0.5-class VLA on 640K trajectories from 512 embodiments.

### r2-B5. Sim-to-real transfer of manipulation policies (open for manipulation)

- **B-a** (Jin et al., v2 Jun 2026, r2:36): "the resulting synthetic data often exhibits a significant gap from real-world
  distributions."
- **B-b.** X2Real (company report, Sep 2026, r2:37): "reliable evaluation remains challenging due to fundamental flaws in
  existing simulation benchmarks: prominent sim-to-real gaps". RoboFolDeX (2026, r2:38): methods strong in simulation "can
  suffer substantial degradation when deployed on real robots."
- **B-c.** Jin et al. Table 3 (r2:39), OpenVLA-OFT on five bimanual tasks, zero-shot to a real Piper robot. The best recipe
  (SFT + RL + domain randomisation) reaches **42.8%** real against **70.8%** in held-out simulation: a transfer loss of 28.0
  points for the same policy. Per task the real rates are 50.8, 51.4, 16.2, 64.6 and 30.8. No real-data-trained reference
  is reported. For evaluation predictivity: X2Real reaches a sim-real correlation of **0.84** against its stated ideal of
  **1.0** (r2:40); Colosseum V2 reports R² = 0.49 over 35 task-perturbation pairs (r2:41).
- **B-d** (r2:42). Domain randomisation, rendering fidelity, physics fidelity and RL fine-tuning in simulation. A clock
  assumption is named: randomisation "episode-wise, where factors are sampled once per episode", or "frame-wise, where
  factors are resampled at every simulation step"; frame-wise transfers better. **Fairness** (Goldberg, r2:43): simulation
  "actually does work pretty well for robots running and performing acrobatics"; for dexterity "simulation doesn't seem to
  work".
- **B-e.** No. The real side needs a physical robot.

### r2-B6. Data scarcity and cost of robot interaction data (open; weak target; possibly misframed)

- **B-a** (VLA review, 2025, r2:44): "high-quality robot demonstrations, often collected via teleoperation are expensive and
  difficult to scale."
- **B-b.** Data Pyramid survey (Aug 2026, r2:45): "Embodied agents admit no such shortcut, since they require data that
  couple observations with physical states and actions." Goldberg (UC Berkeley News, Aug 2025, r2:46): "We don't have
  anywhere near that amount of data to train robots".
- **B-c.** The target is Goldberg's "about 100,000 years" (the reading time of the text used to train LLMs, r2:47), which he
  gives as a lower bound for robots. The best reported corpora (r2:48, r2:49): more than 100,000 hours of real-world UMI
  (hand-held gripper) trajectories for Xiaomi-Robotics-1; about 3,000 hours of robot data in AgiBot World Beta; over 10,000
  hours for π0. 100,000 hours is about 11.4 years, a factor of about 8,800 short; 3,000 hours is a factor of about 290,000.
  **This is CROSS-SOURCE, the units differ (reading time of text against recorded interaction time), and the target is an
  analogy, not a programme requirement.** It is an order-of-magnitude reading only.
- **Possible misframing, for the double check** (r2:71, TMLR survey, Apr 2026): "the central challenge of VLA is not merely
  data scarcity, but the lack of unified abstractions".
- **B-d.** Teleoperation accrues data at the operator's wall-clock rate: "every eight hours of work gives you just eight
  more hours of data" (r2:47). Substitutes: UMI, human egocentric video, simulation and web data (the five-level "pyramid",
  r2:50). Goldberg's alternative: deploy engineered robots and "collect data as it works" (r2:51).
- **B-e.** No. A corpus-size gap is not a computation.

### r2-B7. Long-horizon multi-step tasks with one generalist policy (open for breadth; narrow tasks near solved)

- **B-a** (Openpi Comet, v3 Jan 2026, r2:52): "compounding errors and shifting state distributions can degrade performance
  over time", and "achieving reliable long-horizon performance while preserving scalability and generality remains an open
  challenge."
- **B-b.** The 2025 BEHAVIOR Challenge winners (Dec 2025, r2:53): "Long-horizon household manipulation remains challenging.
  Even with extensive engineering, we achieve only 26% success." RoboSPA (EMNLP 2026, r2:54): "all models fail on the
  hardest Memory-Intensive Planning (MIP)".
- **B-c.** The 2025 BEHAVIOR Challenge (50 household tasks in simulation; Openpi Comet Table 1, r2:55). The best test result
  (1st place, Robot Learning Collective) is full task success **0.1240**, q-score (partial credit) 0.2599. The best reported
  post-challenge validation result is 0.1500 success, 0.3453 q-score. The target is full completion (1.0); no human success
  rate is reported. The gap in full success is 0.876 (test). RoboSPA Table 2 (r2:56): at the hardest level the best
  long-horizon success is **23.1%** (π0.5, from 71.2% at L1), and memory-intensive planning is 0.0 for every model.
  **Contrary evidence** (RoboFolDeX Table 3, r2:57): one real-robot π0 policy folds four garment categories at **95.00%**
  average success with recovery data (80.75% with demonstrations only). Narrow long-horizon tasks are close to solved;
  breadth is not.
- **B-d.** Decomposition into subtasks with separate local policies, which "does not resolve the skill chaining problem"
  (r2:58). Memory: "Without memory of past actions or explicit stage tracking, the policy cannot distinguish these states";
  the winners add stage tracking with voting (r2:59). The benchmark's clock: a time limit of "2× average human task
  completion time" (r2:59).
- **B-e.** No. BEHAVIOR needs Isaac Sim on RTX GPUs.

### r2-B8. On-robot inference latency and compute (open onboard; met off-board)

- **B-a** (Efficient VLA survey, TPAMI, v3 Sep 2026, r2:60): "current VLAs suffer from high inference latency and
  insufficient control frequency [1], making them incompatible with the sub-second control cycles required".
- **B-b.** The edge survey (Mar 2026, r2:61): quantisation and pruning "do not, on current evidence, resolve the worst-case
  failures associated with memory contention, timing variability, thermal instability, and limited safety assurance."
  Jetson-PI (CoRL 2026, r2:62): deployment on the Jetson Orin "remains challenging". RTC (NeurIPS 2025, r2:66): high latency
  "poses a significant challenge."
- **B-c** (Jetson-PI Table 4, r2:63). π0.5 evaluated on LIBERO:

  | Device | Naive | Best (Jetson-PI) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Jetson Orin | 0.70 Hz (1420.8 ms) | **6.06 Hz** (reaction 165.1 ms) |
  | Jetson Thor | 2.18 Hz | 7.59 Hz |

  The target is "at least 10–20 Hz" (Vishwanathan et al., Mar 2026, r2:64; CROSS-PAPER). On the Orin the best is 3.94 to
  13.94 Hz short (a factor of 1.65 to 3.3). The same workshop paper finds MolmoAct-7B on Orin and Thor "∼200 −300× higher"
  in latency than 10 Hz needs. Off-board the target is met: π0 runs at 20/50 Hz with 73 ms latency on an RTX 4090 (r2:65),
  which cuts battery life by 6.0× (r2:63). In Jetson-PI's real-robot test (XR-1 on an Orin) actions execute at 15 Hz; the
  frequency above is the closed-loop update rate.
- **B-d.** Action chunking, which "sacrifices the reactivity of a system to external stimuli" (r2:67); asynchronous
  inference that freezes "actions guaranteed to execute" given a known delay, and keeps success under delays "in excess of
  300 milliseconds" (r2:67; fairness: part of the gap is absorbed this way); confidence-triggered invocation of the VLM,
  "Its predicted confidence determines when to invoke the VLM" (r2:68); dual-rate hierarchies, "low-frequency VLM guidance
  for high-frequency control" (r2:69), with the fast system drawn at 50-500 Hz (r2:70); fixed nominal loops of "50–100 Hz"
  whose achieved rate drifts under thermal throttling (r2:70).
- **B-e.** No, on the named metric (it is hardware-specific). A synthetic scheduling model (clock-driven against
  event-driven replanning under delay) would run on a CPU, as a proxy.

### Harvester errors found (quotes all verbatim; these are readings and "best" choices)

1. **r2-B1, the named metric.** The harvester named NBT at 100 samples per task (2%). LIBERO's official ER budget is 1000
   transitions per task (about 15–20%). Under that budget, the same source reports Pi0 average NBT −0.016 over the four
   suites, which is the target. The harvester disclosed this and expected CONTESTED, so this is a framing choice.
2. **r2-B1, "20%".** This is the source's round figure. Its appendix says approximately 15–20% and counts transitions, not
   demonstrations. This is minor.
3. **r2-B1, PHASER's NBT.** The gaps given (10.0, 11.1, 12.4) are PHASER's, not the table's best NBT. Plain ER has NBT −5.6 on
   OpenVLA-OFT-7B LIBERO-Long, at only 54.6 success. iCaRL has 6.0 on QwenOFT-3B LIBERO-Long. This is minor.
4. **r2-B2, the best result.** VLM2VLA (2025, up to −11.1) is not the best retention. InstructVLA (ICLR 2026) and UAM
   (May 2026) are within about 3 and 5 points of their bases.
5. **r2-B3, the best result.** Position swap 22.6 and robot initial state 59.1 come from a paper that compares only 0.5B
   models. π0.5 reports 58.0 and 73.6 in other 2026 sources. These are cross-paper comparisons, and protocol identity was not
   verified.
6. **r2-B4, precision.** "140 rollouts per model" is ZETA's total over seven embodiments. The FULL cell rests on 10 trials per
   task-embodiment pair. This is minor.
7. **r2-B5, the best result.** The sim-real correlation of 0.84 (X2Real) is not the best reported. PolaRiS reports 0.9 on
   average and 0.98 against RoboArena, ReVeal 0.960, and SimFoundry 0.911–0.95.
8. **r2-B6, the best result.** Company statements report 270,000 hours (Nov 2025) and over 500,000 hours (Apr 2026), above
   the harvester's 100,000 hours. The shortfall factor is therefore about 1,750, not 8,800.
9. **r2-B8, the best result.** 7.59 Hz on Thor is not the best reported rate for π0.5 on Thor. A TensorRT FP8/NVFP4 build
   reaches about 49 ms per inference (NVIDIA, 2026).
