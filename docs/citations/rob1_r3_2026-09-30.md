## Bottlenecks

The open test: B-a, B-b and B-c must all be present (the stage-1b table computes it from the roles). Six are read as open
and one as **not shown open** (r3-B4). Two of the open ones carry an explicit note that the double check may find them
contested:
- r3-B1, by the hydrogen endurance record;
- r3-B3, because fog-inclusive training nearly closes the gap on 2 of 4 videos.

**Not formed as bottlenecks** (in scope, but no 2025-26 source with a best result against a stated target was found in this
sweep; "not found" is not "solved"):
- swarms and communication-denied coordination (programme-level comm-denied exploration is in family R1, r1-B5);
- wind-disturbance rejection (the one 2026 paper found is simulation-only, against a PD baseline);
- on-board compute as a bottleneck of its own. It appears instead as an assumption in r3-B2, r3-B4 and r3-B6.

### r3-B1. Endurance and energy of small battery-electric multirotors (open; may be contested)

- **B-a** (Williamson et al., arXiv 2503.16587 v1, 2025; r3:1): "One particularly vexing weakness of small drones
  (especially multicopters) happens to be their limited endurance", with a typical "10-30 minutes" aloft with a useful
  payload.
- **B-b** (the same paper, 2025; r3:2): "endurance augmentation remains a difficult technical challenge with no clear
  immediate remedy despite many expectant alternatives."
- **B-c.** There are two metrics, from two papers, and they are not combined.
  - **Mission range per battery** (SPRIN-D Funke 2024, the winner's paper, arXiv 2510.01348 v2; r3:3).
    - Target: the programme's 9 km course in a 2 h slot.
    - Best: the longest competition flight was 1371 m (977 s) and ended on "low battery". Three of the six competition flights
      in Table I ended on low battery, and the authors write: "Flights exceeding 1 km were consistently terminated due to
      battery constraints, rather than localization drift".
    - Gap: 1371/9000 = 15% of the course per flight, or about 6.6 battery-limited flights per course.
    - Caveat: the platform carried an autonomy payload (LiDAR, cameras, onboard computer; below 25 kg).
  - **Specific energy of the power source** (Williamson et al.; r3:4).
    - Target: parity with lithium-ion, 200 Wh/kg (lithium-polymer 150 Wh/kg).
    - Best: the thermoelectric prototype reached 30 Wh/kg at 1.8% efficiency, which is 15% of lithium-ion. The best module
      (12%) reaches parity only in the model, and "must increase by over two times" for endurance parity in VTOL multicopters.
  - **Contrary evidence** (Guinness World Records; r3:5). A liquid-hydrogen multirotor flew 12 hr 7 min 5 sec (3 Apr 2019;
    payload not stated). The bottleneck is therefore read for battery-electric small multirotors carrying a payload, not for
    multirotors as such.
- **B-d.** The method families, and the assumption each makes:
  - **Fixed wing:** it gives up hover.
  - **High-voltage tethering:** "practically endless endurance" but little mobility.
  - **Power beaming or powerline charging:** it assumes "peace-time-like infrastructure", i.e. an uninterrupted external
    supply (r3:6).
  - **Hydrocarbon or fuel-cell hybrids.**
  - **Online adaptation to battery depletion:** estimate the maximum motor RPM in real time and re-plan (SkyDreamer; r3:7).
    This is state-indexed rather than clock-indexed, and it adds no energy.

  Otherwise, the flight is planned to one battery's clock, and a longer mission is cut at battery boundaries.
- **B-e.** Not CPU-testable. Endurance is measured in flight. An energy-budget simulation would not be the benchmark.

### r3-B2. GNSS-denied long-range navigation at low altitude (open)

- **B-a** (Werner et al., SPRIN-D winning system, arXiv 2510.01348 v2, 2025-26; r3:8): odometry is "consistent locally, but
  accumulate[s] unbounded drift in previously unvisited areas, where loop closures cannot be used."
- **B-b.**
  - The same paper (r3:9): "Fully onboard systems that scale to kilometer ranges in diverse unseen environments remain rare,
    since outdoor geodata-based drift correction that is both lightweight and robust has not been solved."
  - The SPRIND Fully Autonomous Flight 2.0 page (r3:10): "The dream of fully autonomous flight is not yet a reality." The
    wording probably dates from the 2025 call. The page now reads "Funke completed" and names HERMES (University of
    Klagenfurt) as winner, with no metrics published.
  - The SPRIND article on the 2.0 final (2 Sep 2026; r3:11): "just how difficult it is to make a complex combination of
    hardware and software perform reliably on precisely the day it matters", with weather from "17°C and rain to 29°C and
    intense sunshine".
- **B-c.**
  - **The target.** The programme's own statement (SPRIND, 24 Sep 2024; r3:13): "a 9km track without using GPS or manual
    control", "27 waypoints", with "smoke, fog and rain". The paper adds: below 25 m AGL, no prior dense mapping (r3:12).
  - **The best** (the winner, Fly4Future/CTU; r3:12 and Table I):
    - the longest flight was 1371 m, with 4/4 waypoints detected;
    - localization RMSE was "below 11 m while the compass-aligned odometry had RMSE of up to 53 m" (flight 232: 7 m against
      53 m);
    - "only us and 2 other teams" of 9 flew more than 100 m autonomously.
  - **The gap.** No flight in Table I exceeds 1.371 km, which is 15% of the course, and the paper does not report the course
    completed. Competition ground truth is approximate (0-5 m). The 2025-26 edition published no scores.
- **B-d.** The method families, and the assumption each makes:
  - **Odometry with loop closure** (r3:14). It assumes revisits, i.e. a memory of places, which long unseen routes lack.
  - **SLAM** (r3:14). Its "memory and compute demands grow with the size of the environment".
  - **Learned geolocalization** (r3:14). It is too heavy for embedded compute.
  - **Satellite-image matching** (r3:14). It is "highly unreliable at such low altitudes".
  - **The winner's prior-geodata heightmap matching with a clustered particle filter** (r3:15). It assumes a current prior map
    with structure: in open fields the system relied on odometry. Its bounded local map "restricted flight speed, which in
    turn capped the effective mission range". It runs on CPU-only hardware.
  - **SPRIND's listed alternatives** (r3:16): visual-inertial fusion, ultra-wideband (which assumes installed beacons) and
    magnetic-field navigation.
- **B-e.** Not CPU-testable as a benchmark: it is a field course. The winning localization runs on CPU-only onboard hardware,
  so a replay on recorded flights could run on CPU if the data are public. The supplementary site named in the paper was not
  checked.

### r3-B3. Perception in fog and haze for airborne detection and tracking (open on the worst sequences; may be contested)

- **B-a** (Pouladi et al., arXiv 2607.05467 v1, 6 Jul 2026; r3:17): "Fog severely degrades the visibility of small unmanned
  aerial vehicles (UAVs) in sky-dominant, long-range imagery".
- **B-b.**
  - The same paper (2026; r3:18): "fog-robust airborne perception remains underdeveloped relative to road-scene perception,
    especially for safety-critical onboard obstacle or aircraft detection". It cites a review ([19]) that was not fetched.
  - HazyDet (arXiv 2409.19833 v2, May 2025; r3:19): "ensuring reliable perception in challenging real-world conditions remains
    a significant hurdle", and the effect of adverse weather on UAV detection "remains largely unresolved".
- **B-c** (r3:20; Table 3; YOLO11 + ByteTrack on four DUT Anti-UAV sample videos with depth-aware synthetic fog). At the
  heaviest fog (β = 3.6), the best MOTA over detector size, training regime and input (foggy or restored) is set against the
  best clean-condition MOTA on the same video:

  | Video | best at β = 3.6 | best clean (oracle) | gap |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 02 | 0.771 | 1.000 | 0.229 |
  | 03 | 0.940 | 0.980 | 0.040 |
  | 06 | 0.960 | 0.995 | 0.035 |
  | 10 | 0.179 (restored) | 0.321 | 0.142 (56% retained) |

  - Clean-trained detectors collapse at β = 3.6. For example, on Video 02 the clean-trained YOLO11n scores MOTA 0.000.
  - Fog-inclusive training nearly closes the gap on two of four videos. The reading is therefore "open on the worst
    sequences". It is synthetic fog, on four sample videos, with one tracker.
  - HazyDet's best real-haze detection is 38.7 mAP (DeCoDet), against 52.0 on synthetic haze (r3:21). The paper gives no
    clear-weather reference, so no gap is computed from it.
- **B-d.** The method families, and the assumption each makes:
  - **Test-time restoration (dehazing)** (r3:22, r3:23). It assumes a known degradation model.
  - **Fog-inclusive training** (r3:22, r3:23). It assumes the test fog distribution was sampled offline.
  - **Synthetic data from a scattering model** (r3:22, r3:23). It is used because foggy scenes are hard to collect.
  - **Staged fine-tuning, clear → synthetic haze → real haze** (r3:22, r3:23). The stage boundaries are fixed by the designer.
  - **Tracking-by-detection** (r3:22, r3:23). It cannot recover a target the detector has missed.
- **B-e.** Not CPU-testable as named. It retrains three YOLO11 sizes under three regimes for 100 epochs. Fog synthesis and
  pretrained-YOLO11n inference on the four public videos could run on CPU in hours as a proxy.

### r3-B4. Agile flight against human pilots (autonomous drone racing) (not shown open)

- **B-a** (TU Delft news, 15 Apr 2025; r3:24): the A2RL 2025 goal was physical AI "under extreme time pressure and with very
  limited computational and sensory resources", with "just one forward-looking camera".
- **B-b** (SkyDreamer, arXiv 2510.14783 v1, Oct 2025; r3:25): champion-level racing systems "remain highly specific to drone
  racing", and "no system to date simultaneously achieves full sim-to-real transfer, onboard execution, and champion-level
  performance". Humans handle "unseen tracks, different drones, and even unstructured environments".
- **B-c** (r3:26). The TU Delft AI "won the knockout tournament against human pilots, beating three former DCL world
  champions and reaching flight speeds up to 95.8 km/h". The race used organiser-designed hardware and track (unlike the 2023
  lab-setting win). **The target (human champions) is reached on this metric**, and the source says so; no lap times are
  published. The residual generality gap has no number against a target in the sources found.
- **B-d.** The method families, and the assumption each makes:
  - **End-to-end neural guidance and control sent "directly to the motors", trained by RL** for a known track and drone
    (r3:27). The task is given in advance and the tuning is done offline.
  - **Modular pipelines** that "cannot generalize beyond the highly structured settings they were designed for" (r3:28).
  - **World-model online estimation** whose "Parameter estimates tend to drift over time" (r3:28).
- **B-e.** Not CPU-testable. It is a physical race against humans.

### r3-B5. C2 link reliability and lost-link behaviour (open)

- **B-a.**
  - The FAA Part 108 NPRM (7 Aug 2025; r3:29): "a lost link or loss of control of the UA pose significant risks to aviation
    safety", and "BVLOS flights could at times experience intermittent lost link".
  - RUSI (Feb 2025; r3:30), in jammed spectrum: "Between 60 and 80% of Ukrainian FPVs fail to reach their target", and there
    are "long periods where either EW or the weather significantly degrades UAV operations". The 60-80% figure mixes causes
    and is not used as a C2 metric.
- **B-b.**
  - Chintareddy et al. (arXiv 2605.27755 v2, May 2026; r3:31): "the difficulty of meeting stringent reliability and latency
    requirements, such as 99.9% C2 reliability and sub-100 ms one-way delay".
  - RUSI (2025; r3:32): "navigational jamming is ubiquitous throughout the combat area. Jamming of command frequences is also
    widespread."
- **B-c** (r3:33; more than 10 flights, 4.5+ h, 18K samples; LTE and Starlink, rural Kansas).
  - Best: packet delivery "99.44%" (LTE) and "99.31%" (Starlink). Starlink RTT is under 50 ms for 95% of samples; LTE RTT is
    under 150 ms for 80%. The worst handover RTT degradation is 275 ms.
  - Target: 99.9% C2 reliability and sub-100 ms one-way delay, as stated in the same paper; the 3GPP study it relies on was not
    fetched.
  - Gap:
    - the loss rates, 0.56% and 0.69%, are 5.6× and 6.9× the 0.1% allowed;
    - at least 20% of LTE RTTs exceed 150 ms;
    - neither link's 99.9th-percentile latency is reported.
  - Caveats: the authors judge the delivery rates "sufficient reliability for data transfers", so the comparison with C2 is the
    agent's. The traffic was test packets, not a C2 protocol.
- **B-d.** The method families, and the assumption each makes:
  - **Timeout and predetermined action (FAA).** Link loss is declared by a **timeout** and answered by a **predetermined
    action** (return to home, loiter, continue), with "the link timeout metric" left to industry (r3:34).
  - **EASA's specification and pre-flight contingencies.** C2 performance is specified by "the transaction expiration time;
    ... the availability; ... the continuity; and ... the integrity", with "lost link contingency automatic procedures"
    uploaded before flight and return-to-home recovery (r3:35).
  - **Dual LTE + LEO links with failover.** This assumes the links do not fail together. Cellular handover thresholds, built
    for ground users, fire 3-4× more often at altitude (r3:36).
  - **Under jamming** (r3:37):
    - wire spools, with a range of about 10 km;
    - autonomous terminal guidance;
    - "planned pauses in friendly jamming", which make operations "periodic rather than continuous".
  - **Reliability shown on a clock.** It is demonstrated by "150 flight hours without experiencing any failure", and
    maintenance is scheduled by "time in service, calendar time, number of system operations" (r3:38).
- **B-e.** Not CPU-testable. It is an in-flight radio measurement. A timeout-policy simulation against a link model is
  CPU-cheap, but it would not be the measured link.

### r3-B6. BVLOS detect-and-avoid of non-cooperative aircraft by small UAS (open)

- **B-a.**
  - UK CAA CAP 3015 v2 (Sep 2026; r3:39): "Perhaps the most significant barrier to the growth of this sector is the mid-air
    collision risk associated with Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) operations".
  - The FAA NPRM (r3:40) cites MITRE: an unmitigated drone could collide with manned aircraft "between once every 10,000
    flight hours" and "once every 1 million flight hours" in Class G. It proposes non-cooperative DAA in Class B and C
    airspace.
- **B-b.**
  - DLR review (Aerospace 12:344, Apr 2025; r3:41): "none of these approaches have undergone the transition from prototype
    experiments to robust, safe, standardized, and secure subsystems", and there is "no standard available that applies to
    smaller UASs, which are not capable of hovering".
  - FAA reopening (28 Jan 2026; r3:42): commenters say the DAA provisions "would require prohibitively expensive technology
    that has not been adequately proven for safety value". The comment period was reopened.
  - ViSafe (arXiv 2505.03694 v2, May 2025; r3:43): small-UAS DAA "remains an ongoing challenge".
  - UK CAA page (2026; r3:44): DAA Policy Concept v2 is used "during the policy concept test phase".
- **B-c.**
  - **The target:**
    - CAP 3015 v2 DAA02 (r3:45): "Non-cooperative: NMAC RR ≤ 0.3, LWC RR ≤ 0.5", with NMAC "500 ft horizontally and +/-100
      ft vertically";
    - corroborated by ASSURE A65 (updated 16 Oct 2025; r3:46): the ASTM non-cooperative LoWC risk ratio of 0.5 and NMAC risk
      ratio of 0.3;
    - the human baseline, modelled general-aviation see-and-avoid (A65 Table 16, β=2859, standard turn rate, 3 s delay): NMAC
      RR 0.625 when only the ownship avoids, 0.331 when both avoid.
  - **The best** (ViSafe Table II, digital-twin hardware-in-the-loop, 4000-6000 encounters per row; r3:47): NMAC RR
    - 0.55 head-on;
    - 0.439 overtake;
    - 0.5248 crossing;
    - 0.1 above the horizon;
    - 0.91 below it;
    - 0.667 below the horizon in the real world (6 encounters).
  - **The gap:**
    - E1-E3 are 1.46-1.83× the allowed 0.3;
    - below the horizon, 3.0×;
    - above the horizon, the target is met;
    - E1-E3 beat the modelled human own-only baseline.

    This is **cross-document and protocol-different**. ViSafe's NMAC is its own "predefined unsafe distance" between small
    drones, not the regulatory volume around manned aircraft. The gap is therefore indicative only.
- **B-d.** The method families, and the assumption each makes:
  - **Qualification by fast-time simulation** (r3:48): "10,000–200,000 encounter-geometry simulations", sized by "convergence
    of the Risk Ratio ... to within 5% of a stable value", then enhanced in-service monitoring.
  - **Encounter models built "by long-term observations"** (r3:49). They assume stationary traffic statistics. The protection
    volumes and alerting times are fixed and tuned by "millions of fast-time simulations".
  - **Control-barrier-function avoidance** (r3:50). It assumes an intruder with "a constant velocity vector" that "doesn't
    react", planar manoeuvres, and hand-tuned hyperparameters. The reaction window is bounded by the detection range: 287 m and
    525 m give 14.35 s and 13.12 s.
  - **Strategic deconfliction of shared intent** (r3:51). It gives about 100× fewer UAS-UAS midair collisions in 450,000
    simulations, and assumes all UAS participate.
  - **Yield to ADS-B broadcasters** (r3:51).
  - **The reframing recorded by FAA** (r3:52): an electronic-conspicuity mandate as "the preferred, and only viable, collision
    risk mitigation strategy". It assumes universal equipage.
  - **EASA SORA 2.5** (r3:53): tactical mitigation scaled to air risk. At low risk the avoidance is a fixed "rapid descent";
    routine high-risk operations must use RTCA/EUROCAE standards.
- **B-e.** **CPU-testable.** The regulatory metric is itself a fast-time simulation: CAP 3015 allows "A deterministic
  simulation framework ... without requiring extensive computational resources" and gives an example non-cooperative EO sensor
  model. A logic risk ratio for a given avoidance logic over 10^4-10^5 encounters is minutes to hours of CPU. ViSafe's
  learned perception and Isaac Sim digital twin are not CPU-testable.

### r3-B7. Counter-UAS: tracking tiny drones in complex scenes, and non-destructive interception (open)

- **B-a** (SPRIND Anti-Drone Response 2.0 page, 2026; r3:54): small drones are "a significant security challenge", and
  "Existing countermeasures are overwhelmed by the speed, diversity, and agility of these systems."
- **B-b.**
  - The same page (the Funke started 1 Mar 2026; r3:55): "The core challenge therefore remains unresolved."
  - CST Anti-UAV (arXiv 2507.23473 v2, Nov 2025; r3:56): "tracking tiny UAVs in complex environments remains a challenge".
- **B-c.**
  - **CST Anti-UAV** (thermal infrared; 220 sequences; mean state accuracy; r3:57). The best is 35.92 (GlobalTrack, trained
    and tested on CST). The same trackers score 66.42-67.69 on Anti-UAV410. The target is the oracle (SA 100). The gap is 64.08
    points to the oracle. The best score falls 31.77 points from Anti-UAV410 to CST; per tracker, GlobalTrack loses 30.50 and
    SiamDT 31.85 (same paper, same metric).
  - **SPRIND** (r3:58). The programme target is "neutralizing small and micro UAVs ... of up to 25 kg and 200 km/h
    autonomously, precisely, and non-destructively". The first edition found that "no team was able to deliver" such a
    system. This is a count of zero, not a measured gap; the number of teams was not published.
- **B-d.** The method families, and the assumption each makes:
  - **Detect-classify-respond pipelines with reversible effectors** (r3:59): "guided interception, controlled redirection,
    blocking, or safe retrieval".
  - **Thermal-infrared sensing** (r3:60), because visible light is unreliable "in low-light or foggy conditions".
  - **Single-object trackers trained offline per dataset** (r3:60), which lose about half their accuracy on a harder
    distribution. On interruptions: targets reappearing where they vanished (occlusion) are easy, while out-of-view gaps need
    re-localization and score as low as 14.53%. Retraining used 8 A100 GPUs.
- **B-e.** Not CPU-testable as named: 20 trackers were retrained on GPUs. Inference of one pretrained tracker on the public
  CST data could run on CPU in hours as a proxy. Interception is a field trial.
