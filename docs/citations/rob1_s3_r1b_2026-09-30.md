# ROB1 stage 3, candidate C-R1, searcher B: prior art for a phase-keyed stop of a dynamically balancing legged robot (2026-09-30; R10)

This is stage 3 of `Robotics/DECLARATION.md` for candidate **C-R1** of `Robotics/CRR_READING.md` (bottleneck r4-B4, emergency
stop of dynamically balancing robots). It is the dossier of searcher B, who worked independently of searcher A
(`docs/citations/rob1_s3_r1a_2026-09-30.md`, `Robotics/checks/claims_s3_r1a.py`).

B read A's claims first. B then searched **differently**:

  and two AIST papers A did not find;

- regulation in other machine classes (cyclic presses, aircraft take-off), for the same structure outside robotics;
- a check of each of A's readings against its quote in context (section G).

**The mechanism searched (CRR_READING.md, C-R1).**
- A stop (emergency, protective, safe or fall-safe) of a dynamically balancing legged or humanoid robot that is **initiated,
  timed or shaped by the gait's own phase**. The caller's instances: gait-phase-dependent stopping, phase-aware stop
  trajectories, capture-point or stance-phase stopping, "stop at the next stance", phase-dependent safe-state selection.
- The frontier comparator: **one stopping time on the clock, sized by the worst gait phase**; the stop can arrive at any
  instant.
- **CRR's prediction:** over stop commands arriving uniformly in time, a stop keyed to the gait's own phase has a lower worst
  case and a lower mean of stopping distance and falls, at equal delay budget, than the best single clock-sized stop.
- **The must-fail case:** a statically stable system with no phase, where the two tie.

**Positions** (the caller's):
- **P1:** phase-dependent timing or shaping of a stop for a legged or humanoid robot is published.
- **P2:** it is shown to reduce worst-case or mean stopping distance, time or falls against a phase-independent stop.
- **P3:** it appears in a standard, a standards proposal or an industrial safety function (for example ISO 25785-1 drafts,
  IFR or industry white papers).

**Reading tags.** These are searcher A's, adopted unchanged so that the two sweeps grade alike (full text in
`Robotics/checks/claims_s3_r1b.py`). B added three clauses, all fixed before B's readings were written:
- **B1 (close, P2):** a qualitative or analytic claim about how the stop's outcome depends on the phase at which it starts,
  with no measured phase-independent baseline.
- **B2 (close, P3):** a phase-keyed stop in operational but uncertified, non-industrial deployment (a research robot operated
  in public); or a regulation or certification rule that keys a stop decision or a protective function to the machine's own
  cycle or progress in another machine class (cyclic presses, aircraft take-off), not legged robots.
- **B3 (contradicts, P3):** a deployed legged robot's safety function that applies the stop at the command instant
  irrespective of the gait phase. As with A's clause, this is evidence against P3 for that function only.

## A. AIST, HRP-2: the pause deferred to the gait's own phase (2006-07)

### 1. Motion Suspension System for Humanoids (Kaneko, Kanehiro, Morisawa, Kajita, Fujiwara, Harada & Hirukawa, AIST)

- **Identifier:** ヒューマノイドの動作一時停止システム — 実時間動作一時停止判断と実時間パターン修正 —, Journal of the Robotics
  Society of Japan 25(2):289-298, published 15 Mar 2007 (J-STAGE online 25 Aug 2010), DOI 10.7210/jrsj.25.289. An English
  version is listed in IEEE Xplore under IROS 2006 (not fetched).
- **Integrity:** `raw/jstage_kaneko2007.pdf` sha256 3e7487bd2b2d1ad0dd0636a52ba3c6e0b2c39eb8afdef8632ee053b2da349051.
- **Text:** `r1b/txt/jstage_kaneko2007.txt`

> We also propose a simple and effective method of real-time pattern generation to force humanoids to stop immediately by one step without falling.

> 本 稿で提案するシステムで は,両 脚支持期の中間点でこの信号をHighレ ベルにし,そ の 他の期間はLowレ ベルにしている.

"In the proposed system this signal [the suspension-start-enable signal] is set High at the midpoint of the double support
phase, and Low at all other times."

> 判 定信号:Jflag-ψ がHigh信 号になったタイミングで早急に一時 停止動作を開始する必要は必ずしもなく,両脚支持期の中間点 で開始しても十分に効果があると判断した.

"We judged that the suspension need not start at once when the judgment signal goes High; starting it at the midpoint of the
double support phase is sufficiently effective."

> 基本方針3:新 規に生成する一時停止動作パターンに切り替 えるタイミングは,両 脚支持期の中間点で行うこと.

"Basic policy 3: the switch to the newly generated suspension pattern is made at the midpoint of the double support phase."

> ε は,目 標胴体位置から見て, 目標とする左右足裏位置が同じ距離にあるか否かを判別するた めの微小な正の値である.

"Epsilon is a small positive value for judging whether the target left and right sole positions are at the same distance from
the target body position."

- **Reading: states P1** (r1b:1), in its **timing** form ("stop at the next stance"), on hardware.
  - The pause (一時停止, a temporary stop) of a walking HRP-2 may be requested by the operator or triggered by the robot's own
    posture and landing-force sensors. It is **deferred to an event defined by the gait's own geometry**: the two feet at equal
    distance from the body, the midpoint of double support. It is not deferred to a clock time.
  - A constant deceleration then brings the robot to rest within one step. The robot resumes on the operator's command.
  - A had no source for this paper.

> 両 脚支持期の初期や後期に,オ リジナルパターンから, 一定加速度で減速するような一時停止動作パターンに切り替え ると,切 り替えタイミング時のx軸 方向(Fig.6参 照)の 重心 速度が十分に減速しておらず,x'軸 方向の支持多角形の範囲も 狭いことから(x'軸 方向の安定余裕が少ないことから),一 時 停止動作を実現可能な歩行速度の上限値が低くなる問題が出て くる.

"If the switch is made early or late in double support, the centre-of-mass velocity along x' has not yet fallen enough and the
support polygon is narrow along x', so the highest walking speed at which the suspension can be realised becomes lower."

> 時 速2.5[km]ま では,提 案する一時停止動 作にて,HRP-2を1歩 以内に一時停止させることができるこ とを確認している.

"We confirmed that up to 2.5 km/h the proposed suspension stops HRP-2 within one step."

- **Reading: close P2** (r1b:2; clause B1).
  - Why the stop's feasibility depends on the phase is argued from the gait's dynamics, and the phase-keyed stop is shown on
    hardware. The same section names 2.8 km/h as the method's limit, where the rear foot lifts.
  - No immediate or clock-timed suspension is measured against it.

> 展 示会会場や研究室での運用を考え た場合には,実 験結果にも示す通り,提案する動作一時停止シ ステムは非常に有用である.

"Considering operation at exhibition venues and in the laboratory, the proposed motion suspension system is very useful, as the
experimental results show."

- **Reading: close P3** (r1b:3; clause B2). The same section lists the cases met in current operation: objects thrown into or
  dropped in the demonstration space, a person entering it, an operator's mistaken start command. A phase-keyed pause was in
  operational use by a national laboratory. It is not a standard and not a certified industrial safety function.

## B. AIST, HRP-2: the emergency stop begun at the next double support (2008)

### 2. Motion Generation of Emergency Stop at Double Support Phase for Humanoid Robot by Pole Assignment (Morisawa, Kajita, Kaneko, Kanehiro, Nakaoka, Harada, Fujiwara & Hirukawa, AIST)

- **Identifier:** 極配置法によるヒューマノイドロボットの両脚期における緊急停止動作生成, Journal of the Robotics Society of Japan

- **Text:** `r1b/txt/jstage_morisawa2008.txt`

"For reliability and real-time computation, the stop trajectory is generated in the double support phase, where the influence

- **Reading: states P1** (r1b:4), in its timing form, on hardware. This is exactly "stop at the next stance" for an emergency
  stop. The hardware runs are HRP-2 walking at 1.125 and 2.5 km/h, ten repetitions each.

> こ のように,両 脚期のどのタイミングで停止軌道を開 始するかによってZMPの 移動量が異なり,ZMPの 安定余裕が 異なってくることが分かる.

> これらの結果から2.5[km/h]ま での直進歩行においては,両 脚 期のどのタイミングで停止動作を開始しても,安 定な停止軌道 を生成することが可能であることが分かる.

"Up to 2.5 km/h, a stable stop trajectory can be generated whatever the timing within double support."

> この手法は収束計算を伴うため計算負荷が比較的 大きく,単脚期において着地軌道を新たに生成する際にモデル で考慮していない遊脚の慣性力の影響で着地時の床反力が大き くなり,低速の歩行にしか実用上適用できない問題があった.

Of the same authors' 2005 method (A's r1a:2), which also re-plans from single support: "the computational load is relatively
large; and when a new landing trajectory is generated in single support, the swing leg's inertia, which is not in the model,
raises the landing reaction force, so the method was practical only for slow walking."

> この手法は,動作パター ンの重心軌道とZMP軌 道が両脚期に交差するのを検出して停 止動作を開始するため,踊 りなどの複雑なステップを有する動 作パターンでは1歩以内に停止できない可能性がある.

Of Kaneko's phase-triggered suspension (section A): "because it starts the stop on detecting that the CoG and ZMP trajectories
cross in double support, it may fail to stop within one step for motion patterns with complex steps, such as dancing."

- **Reading: close P2** (r1b:5; clause B1).
  - The phase dependence is computed on the HRP-2 model, and the reason for deferring to double support is stated: a stop
    re-planned in single support was practical only at low speed.
  - **The caution cuts both ways.** The cost of deferral, the wait for the phase event, is named as a limitation of the
    phase-triggered variant. This bears directly on CRR's clause "at equal delay budget".
  - There is no measured comparison against an immediate or clock-timed stop.

> 2005年3月25日 から同年9月25日 にかけて開催 された愛・地球博で185日 間運用展示を行った.恐 竜ロボット の長期の運用に当たり,動 作中の観客への安全性の確保および ロボットの転倒防止を目的として,緊 急停止システムを開発し た[4][5].

"[AIST] operated and exhibited [a dinosaur-type biped robot] for 185 days at Expo 2005 (Aichi), from 25 March to 25 September
2005. For its long-term operation, an emergency stop system was developed to keep spectators safe during motion and to stop
the robot falling [4][5]." Here [4] is Kaneko et al. (section A) and [5] is Morisawa et al. IROS 2005 (A's r1a:2).

- **Reading: close P3** (r1b:6; clause B2). A phase-keyed stop was in public operation for six months, twenty years ago. It
  is not a standard, not certified and not industrial. The sentence cites both methods together, so it does not say which one
  ran on the dinosaur robot.

## C. Osaka University, HRP-2: the full text behind A's abstract-only records (2009)

## D. The learned-controller frontier: the phase attested, then removed (2024-26)

### 4. Revisiting Reward Design and Evaluation for Robust Humanoid Standing and Walking (van Marum, Shrestha, Duan, Dugar, Dao & Fern, Oregon State)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2404.19173, **v2** (30 Aug 2024; v1 30 Apr 2024); arXiv comment "8 pages, 5 figs". The venue (IROS
  2024, per a search-result summary) was not verified on the day. Digit.
- **Integrity:** `raw/2404.19173v2.pdf` sha256 c75e4eea5ef445a4fec39c689a38a271fa26aa831362261fec6fb146442fe2a9.
- **Text:** `r1b/txt/2404.19173v2.txt`

> 1) Stand. The robot should stop if moving and stand in place with two feet on the ground.

> For example, this may involve hand-engineered cycle-time constraints in transitioning from waking to standing [12] or blending the outputs of the controllers at transition points [13, 15].

> in this work, we choose to train a single SaW controller that does not require any form of reference or clock-based inputs, and natively learns to switch freely between commands throughout the entire learning process.

> Additionally, when transitioning from walking to standing, requiring double foot contact will cause a policy to opt for the closest stance position rather than the most stable one.

- **Reading: close P1** (r1b:10).
  - Reference [12] is Crowley et al., ICRA 2023 (A's source 6). The source attests that phase-keyed walk-to-stand transitions
    ("cycle-time constraints"; "waking" sic) are prior practice, and calls them hard to tune.
  - It replaces them with one learned policy, without clock inputs, that stops from any state. The stop is not phase-keyed.
  - It comes from the same laboratory as Crowley, so the attestation is not independent. The two ways of stopping are not
    compared.

### 5. Learning Safe-Stoppability Monitors for Humanoid Robots (PRISM; Sun, Pan, Li, Ding, Cui, Wang & Liu)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2603.22703, **v1** (24 Mar 2026; the only version). Unitree G1. A's sweep had the companion paper
  (Safe-Stop, r1a:13), not this one.
- **Integrity:** `raw/2603.22703v1.pdf` sha256 4b070a542090268b5650b932d718b1d2b7097d47e1d23c846614a5ccb38f53d4.
- **Text:** `r1b/txt/2603.22703v1.txt`

> On the other hand, E-stop triggers are asynchronous and unpredictable. An emergency stop may be triggered at any time due to human intrusion, system-level anomalies, communication failures, or direct operator intervention.

> must maintain its state in the safe-stoppable envelope (SSE): a set of states from which the fallback controller can reliably drive the robot to a dynamically stable terminal condition

> By continuously evaluating whether the current state remains within SSE, the monitor can autonomously trigger E-stop behavior before catastrophic failure occurs

> identifies elevated risks during manipulation phases (Pick, Place) while ensuring high confidence during steady-state locomotion (Transfer, Leave).

- **Reading: close P1** (r1b:11).
  - The 2026 frontier treats the stop command as arriving at any instant. It answers with a learned **state-level**
    stoppability monitor, which keeps the robot inside the stoppable set and may trigger the stop itself.
  - The phases it finds risky are task phases (pick, place); it judges steady walking highly stoppable.
  - There is no phase-keyed timing, and no comparison with a phase-independent stop.

### 8. US 29 CFR 1910.217, Mechanical power presses (OSHA)

- **Identifier:** Code of Federal Regulations, Title 29, vol. 5, revised as of 1 July 2021 (govinfo annual edition),
  paragraphs (c)(3)(iii)(d)-(e) and (h)(9)(v).
- **Integrity:** `raw/govinfo_cfr2021_1910_217.pdf` sha256 d0ef5f5ff9e63d72ff01fa19e1ca1e27e0456f1e02c36e86d7a7a477e3e3ef2e.

> Ts = stopping time of the press measured at approximately 90° position of crankshaft rotation (seconds).

> Ts = Longest press stopping time, in seconds, computed by taking averages of multiple measurements at each of three positions (45 degrees, 60 degrees, and 90 degrees) of crankshaft angular position; the longest of the three averages is the stopping time to use.

- **Reading: close P3** (r1b:14; clause B2, another machine class). US machine-safety regulation for a cyclic machine does
  three things:
  - it measures the stopping time per phase of the cycle (the crank angle);
  - it sizes the safety distance by the **worst phase's** stopping time, the structure of ISO 13855 sizing by the worst gait
    phase (r1a:12);
  - it keys the protective function itself to the cycle's phase (muted on the non-hazardous upstroke).
- **Both sides of C-R1 are therefore old regulatory practice for presses.** Neither concerns a legged robot, and the stop
  itself is not timed by phase.
