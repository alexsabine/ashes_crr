# ROB1 stage 3, candidate C-R2, searcher A: prior art for an own-progress budget on out-of-contact exploration (2026-09-30; R10)

Stage 3 of `Robotics/DECLARATION.md`, candidate **C-R2** of `Robotics/CRR_READING.md` (bottleneck r1-B5, search in
communication-denied spaces). The search was for the **specific mechanism**, before any code. This is searcher A's dossier.

**The mechanism searched (CRR_READING.md, C-R2).**
- An out-of-communication excursion (exploration beyond radio range, lost-link behaviour, return to comms) is bounded by the
  robot's **own progress**: the information it has gathered since the last contact (information gain, map growth, novelty,
  unreported data, artifact count), counted in its own units.
- The frontier comparator (stage 1, harvester r1:40 and r1:41): a **homing timer** set by a human supervisor, or homing on
  the **remaining mission time** (Team CERBERUS, DARPA SubT winner).
- CRR's prediction: more artifacts per mission at an equal rate of failures to return, against the best fixed timer tuned on
  other maps. The must-fail case: a uniform map, where information accrues at a constant rate, so the two clocks coincide.

**Positions** (the caller's):
- **P1:** a return or rendezvous decision triggered by information or progress accumulated since the last contact is published.
- **P2:** it is compared with a fixed time budget and shown better (more coverage or artifacts at equal loss or return
  failure).
- **P3:** it is used in a fielded system (SubT teams, mine-mapping drones).

**Reading tags** (fixed before the readings; full text in `Robotics/checks/claims_s3_r2a.py`).
- **states:** the source publishes the position as stated. For P1, a return, relay, reconnection or rendezvous rule whose
  trigger or bound is information or progress accumulated since the last contact. For P2, such a rule measured against a clock
  rule and reported better. For P3, such a rule configured on a system run in the field or in a competition.
- **close:** a nearby mechanism that differs in one named respect. Examples: the accumulated information is an input to a
  learned or optimised policy, not a stated trigger; the rule is known only second-hand or from an abstract; the quantity is
  monitored but the trigger is not stated; the comparison is in a neighbouring setting, on another metric, or mixed.
- **contradicts:** the source shows the mechanism, or its limiting case, fails or is inferior to a clock rule.
- Clock-only sources (the frontier's side) are listed as **context** (section F) and are not claims.

## A. Fielded SubT teams: a data-buffer trigger (Team CoSTAR, JPL)

### 1. ACHORD (Saboia, Clark, Thangavelu, Edlund, Otsu, ... Agha-mohammadi)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2206.02245, **v1** (5 Jun 2022; the only version). RA-L 2022 per the search listing; the venue was not
  verified on the day.
- **Integrity:** `raw/pdf_2206.02245v1.pdf` sha256 ad9df9ccc9be00543ae8e7e342b08e8e296de1db4a2719061752cdcb2f163e00.
- **Text:** `r2a/txt/pdf_2206.02245v1.txt`

> the robots also maintain statistics on the reliable data that needs to be transferred to the base or other robots: (1) buffer size: the amount of data (in bytes) that needs to be transferred;

> Return to Comms: When the buffer sizes exceeds an upper bound (T u B = 300KB), the robot will sacrifice nominal exploration and instead move towards an area with strong comms.

> If the buffer size drops below a desired threshold (T l B = 200KB) before reaching the target checkpoint, nominal exploration continues immediately.

- **Reading: states P1** (r2a:1).
  - The excursion ends when the data gathered and not yet delivered (the buffer, in bytes) reaches a fixed unit (300 KB). No
    timer is involved.
  - The unit is bytes of reliable data (maps and artifact reports), not information gain in resolvable steps. A hysteresis
    band (300 and 200 KB) is added.
  - The only clock in the rule is a 60 s congestion timeout at the checkpoint (context quote in section F).

> We evaluate our solution with respect to the comms performance in several challenging underground environments including the DARPA SubT Finals competition environment.

> If the network infrastructure covers enough of the environment, we found it was sufficient to return to comms sparingly. With sophisticated autonomy that can reliably determine when to return and transfer data, a small effective comms range and long outages are permissible.

- **Reading: states P3** (r2a:2).
  - The comms-aware coordination that contains the buffer trigger is the system Team CoSTAR ran in the SubT Finals environment
    and in a Kentucky Underground field test.
  - The paper does not count how often the trigger fired, and does not isolate its effect on artifacts. It reports comms
    metrics (maximum delay, up time, data rates).

### 6. Multirobot Exploration of Communication-Restricted Environments: A Survey (Amigoni, Banfi & Basilico)

- **Integrity:** `raw/amigoni_survey_ieeeis2017_polimi.pdf` sha256 fa995ed2a7de61f51a31f5b23e5f28549b5819bd041713912f532d736e5964e1.

> Event-based connectivity: robots must regain connection with some teammates according to a policy triggered by particular events, such as the discovery of new information about the environment or simply striking a given time.

### 7. Strategies for coordinated multirobot exploration with recurrent connectivity constraints (Banfi, Quattrini Li, Rekleitis, Amigoni & Basilico)

- **Identifier:** Autonomous Robots 42(4):875-894, 2018 (online 25 Jul 2017), doi 10.1007/s10514-017-9652-y (Crossref). Author
  manuscript (file AURO2016.pdf) at the PoliMi repository.
- **Integrity:** `raw/banfi_auro2018_polimi.pdf` sha256 5f76bbc2ee8e5c74d111fa6b28ffb41c6ab26bce0502390889e7a0bd65c8ecfd.
- **Text:** `r2a/txt/banfi_auro2018_polimi.txt`

> With recurrent connectivity, robots have to connect with each other and with the BS each time they gather new information. This entails an online constraint scheme, where robots can disconnect for arbitrarily long periods, but they must be able to coordinate in order to report to the base station as soon as new information is acquired.

> Hollinger and Singh (2012) consider a general mission scenario in which robots must synchronously regain global connectivity with the BS after a fixed time interval.

- **Reading: states P1** (r2a:9), with the clock contrast drawn explicitly.
  - The reconnection requirement is keyed to **new information** (each new observation), not to a fixed time interval
    (periodic connectivity).
  - The disconnection may be arbitrarily long in clock time.
  - The unit is one new observation at an assigned location.

> In (Spirin et al, 2013), the robots’ behavior is regulated by a utility function, which considers the amount of information not delivered yet by a robot to the BS and the predicted amount of information known by the BS.

> Utility defines a distributed strategy where each robot chooses autonomously which frontier to explore in a greedy fashion and returns to the BS as soon as the ratio between the area supposed to be known at the BS and that known by the robot goes below a predefined value

> In our experiments, we set r = 0.5 to obtain a balanced, yet more exploration-prone, behavior.

- **Reading: states P1** (r2a:10).
  - Return to base is triggered when the information the robot holds but has not delivered grows relative to what the base
    knows. The trigger is a ratio threshold r; the paper's runs set r = 0.5.
  - This is second-hand, but from users who re-ran the strategy in MRESim. The primary abstract is source 8.

> We compare against it since it has been shown to complete exploration faster than the role-based strategy of de Hoog et al (2009)

> Note that this strategy does not embed the recurrent connectivity constraint, so robots can remain disconnected from the BS for an unpredictably long amount of time and are thus expected to explore more freely.

- **Reading: close P2** (r2a:12).
  - This is a second-hand report: the undelivered-information return rule completed exploration faster than role-based
    exploration.
  - Role-based explorers return on a **timed rendezvous schedule** (de Hoog et al. 2010, section F).
  - The metric is exploration time, not artifacts at equal return failure. The comparator is a planned schedule, not a fixed
    homing timer.

## D. Relay decisions driven by unreported information, compared with fixed schedules

### 11. PRoID: Predicted Rate of Information Delivery in Multi-Robot Exploration and Relaying (Kim, Baek, Corah, Best, Moon & Scherer)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2604.10433, **v1** (12 Apr 2026; the only version). No venue is stated.
- **Integrity:** `raw/pdf_2604.10433v1.pdf` sha256 e0e6cddfb816001ac89f8bb974093d2edf72da347cda994e7227591f15ea9d26.
- **Text:** `r2a/txt/pdf_2604.10433v1.txt`

> The central challenge is deciding when each robot should stop exploring and relay: this depends on what the robot is likely to find ahead, what information it uniquely holds, and whether immediate or future delivery is more valuable.

> We define RoID as the ratio of novel, reportable information to the estimated travel time back to the base station.

> under which the robot terminates exploration and initiates relay to the base station.

> Others trigger relay based on fixed information thresholds [11].

- **Reading: states P1** (r2a:14).
  - The return (relay) trigger is built on the information the robot holds and has not reported since its last delivery
    (I_unreported over the travel time home).
  - That rate is compared with a predicted rate that includes a learned map prediction of what lies ahead. The rule is
    Γnow > α Γpred.
  - The paper also names the pure fixed-information-threshold relay trigger, C-R2's form, as prior work: Clark et al., RA-L
    2021 (source 13).

> Periodic Relay: Each robot relays to the base station at a fixed interval P, then resumes exploration. We evaluate P ∈{100,200,300} timesteps.

> PRoID outperforms the best periodic baseline (P = 300) by 11.8, 12.4, 10.5, and 7.8 percentage points at n = 2,3,4,5 respectively, and outperforms Final Relay Only by 1.3, 3.4, and 2.1 percentage points at n = 3,4,5. At n = 2, Final Relay Only is marginally competitive (58.4% vs. 58.2%)

> Against the best periodic baseline (P = 300), PRoID-Safe achieves gains of 9.8 and 12.2 percentage points at n = 3 and n = 5 under λ = 1100, and 8.6 and 10.6 percentage points under the harsher λ = 900 condition.

- **Reading: states P2** (r2a:15).
  - **The comparison:** an information-driven relay rule against the best fixed-interval relay and against exploring until
    the deadline (mission-time homing).
  - **The metric:** the coverage that reached the base station. Robot failures are modelled: a robot that fails loses its
    undelivered data.
  - **The result:** it wins against the best interval at every team size. Against final-relay-only it wins at n = 3 to 5 but
    not at n = 2.
  - **Two differences from C-R2:**
    - the rule adds a learned map prediction;
    - the fixed interval was swept on the same test maps, not tuned on other maps.

## Is the mechanism published?

**Yes, for P1 and P3. For P2, in its direction; not in the exact comparison C-R2 proposes.**

- **P1: published many times, since at least 2013–2017.**
  - Returning, relaying or reconnecting when the information gathered since the last contact reaches a unit appears in:
    - a data-buffer threshold in bytes (ACHORD; NeBula addendum);
    - an unreported-artifact count (MARBLE);
    - a cap on new data cells (Zijlstra et al.);
    - a ratio of undelivered to delivered information (Spirin et al.; surveyed by Amigoni et al.);
    - a buffer-overflow bound on meeting times (Guo & Zavlanos);
    - reconnection on each new observation (Banfi et al.'s recurrent connectivity).
  - The survey of the field names information-triggered and time-triggered reconnection as two members of one published
    class.
  - PRoID (2026) names the pure fixed-information-threshold relay as prior work (Clark et al. 2021).
- **P3: fielded in the DARPA SubT Challenge by two teams.**
  - Team MARBLE, in the Final Event Prize Run: 3 unreported artifacts, or 5 minutes after the first unreported one.
  - Team MARBLE, in all Phase I–II circuits: one artifact per excursion.
  - Team CoSTAR: the buffer threshold, in the Finals environment.
  - The fielded systems also used clock rules (MARBLE's 5-minute backstop, CoSTAR's 10-minute rule, CERBERUS's homing timer).
- **P2: an information-driven relay rule has been compared with fixed-interval relay and reported better.**
  - PRoID reports coverage delivered to the base with robot failures modelled: ahead of the best fixed interval at every team
    size, ahead of mission-time homing at n = 3 to 5 but not at n = 2.
  - Close comparisons point the same way on other metrics or settings (IR2 against a pre-set time budget; Spirin's rule
    against timed rendezvous, second-hand). CoCoPlan's is mixed.

## Judgement calls, for the investigator's review

- **Units.** CRR's own clock counts information in resolvable steps (A1′). The published triggers count it in:
  - bytes (ACHORD);
  - artifacts (MARBLE);
  - new data cells (Zijlstra);
  - map-area ratios (Spirin);
  - new observations (Banfi).

  C-R2's own statement lists "information gain, map growth, novelty, artifact count" as instances, so these are read **states**.
  A reviewer who holds that only a Fisher-information unit is CRR's would read them **close**. The grade would still be
  REDUNDANT on the mechanism's form, with the unit as the only difference.
- **The practitioners' own reading of the buffer.** The NeBula addendum reads buffer size as a proxy for time out of contact.
  That is the must-fail case in reverse: on a map where data accrue at a constant rate, the two clocks coincide. The fielded
  record contains no ablation of buffer or count against timer.
- **Hybrid rules.** Both fielded count or buffer triggers sit beside clocks: MARBLE's 5-minute backstop, CoSTAR's 10-minute
  rule in a field test, ACHORD's 60 s congestion timeout. None of the SubT papers compares the two. This is where C-R2's
  specific prediction is still untested in the sources found.
- **PRoID read states for P2, not close.** It is the strongest P2 source. Two differences could move it to close:
  - its rule is a rate comparison with a learned map prediction, not a pure accumulated-information threshold;
  - the periodic baseline was swept on the evaluation maps, where C-R2 asks for a timer tuned on other maps.

  It does model robot failures, and it scores delivered coverage, which is the equal-loss accounting that C-R2 asks for.
- **CoCoPlan read close, not contradicts.** Its N = 3 progress trigger is behind the fixed intervals in SubT. That is a
  warning that a badly set count threshold loses to a clock, as a badly set clock loses to a count. Its N = 10 trigger ties or
  beats the intervals. Neither arm is a return from an excursion.
- **Second-hand and abstract-only sources.**
  - r2a:10 and r2a:12 describe Spirin et al. through Banfi et al., who re-ran the strategy.
  - r2a:11 (Spirin) and r2a:19 (Clark) rest on abstracts; their full texts are paywalled.
