**The mechanism searched (CRR_READING.md, C-R2).**
- An out-of-communication excursion (exploration beyond radio range, lost-link behaviour, return to comms) is bounded by the
  robot's **own progress**: the information it has gathered since the last contact.
- The alternative is a wall-clock homing timer or the remaining mission time.
- **CRR's prediction:** more artifacts per mission at an equal rate of failures to return, against the best fixed timer tuned
  on other maps.
- **The must-fail case:** a uniform map, where information accrues at a constant rate and the two clocks coincide.

**Positions** (the caller's):
- **P1:** a return or rendezvous decision triggered by information or progress accumulated since the last contact is
  published.
- **P2:** such a decision is compared with a fixed time budget and shown better (more coverage or artifacts at equal loss or
  return failure).
- **P3:** it is used in a fielded system (SubT teams, mine-mapping drones).

**Reading tags.** These are searcher A's, copied unchanged so that the two searches grade alike (full text in
`Robotics/checks/claims_s3_r2b.py`).
- **states:** the source publishes the position as stated.
- **close:** a nearby mechanism that differs in one named respect:
  - the information is an input to a learned or optimised policy, not a stated trigger;
  - the source is second-hand or abstract-only;
  - the quantity is monitored but the trigger is not stated;
  - the comparison is in a neighbouring setting, on another metric, or mixed.
- **contradicts:** the source shows the mechanism, or its limiting case, fails or is inferior to a clock rule.
- Clock-only sources are **context** (section F), not claims.

## A. The full text A could not read: Clark, Galante, Krishnamachari & Psounis (RA-L 2021)

- **Identifier:** "A Queue-Stabilizing Framework for Networked Multi-Robot Exploration", IEEE RA-L 6(2):2091-2098, 2021,
  doi 10.1109/LRA.2021.3061304.
- **Version:** author copy on the first author's site (pdfTeX, created 15 Apr 2021); there is no arXiv version. A search
  listing shows a "Correction to" notice for the paper; it was not read.
- **Integrity:** `raw/clark_queue_stabilizing_lillyclark.pdf` sha256
  77de18dc1b3f83443512c6b00425c986381065f25307e7043cd3e4097cb6ae6a.
- **Text:** `r2b/txt/clark_queue_stabilizing_lillyclark.txt`

> The closest work to ours is the work by Spirin et al. [28], which introduces a constraint on the ratio of information at the data sink and a crude approximation of total information across all mobile robots. Based on this constraint, robots choose a role-based controller (explore or return to the data sink).

> Time Preference (TP): This controller presented by Spirin et al. uses a target ratio ρ comparing the queue to the map size [28].

> where |mr(t)| is the number of visited cells, robots choose αr(t) ∈Ar(t) to minimize Y (αr(t)). Otherwise robots choose αr(t) ∈Ar(t) to minimize ∥αr(t) −D∥mr(t).

- **Reading: states P1** (r2b:1).
  - The rule is given as a formula, and the authors implemented and ran it. The robot explores while 1 − q_r/|m_r| ≥ ρ,
    where q_r is its untransferred data queue and |m_r| its map. Otherwise it heads for the data sink D.
  - This settles what A could read only from abstracts (Spirin, r2a:11; Clark, r2a:19).

> The crux of our approach is that a backlog in the data queue puts pressure on the robot to transmit this data and therefore move to recover a communication path to the data sink.

> We now introduce a queue qr(t) of untransferred data stored at robot r with the following dynamics:

> We assume Pr(Xi = 0) = Pr(Xi = 1), so the number of cells visited is the reduction in map entropy.

> But this approach assigns a utility to connectivity which does not depend on whether or not there is new information to share.

- **Reading: close P1** (r2b:2). This is the nearest to CRR's units.
  - The return pressure is the untransferred **information**. The queue grows by the mutual information gained, which equals
    the reduction in map entropy.
  - But the pressure drives a Lyapunov drift-plus-penalty controller, not a stated threshold.
  - It also acts through a delay queue that adds the backlog at every step, which mixes information with the clock.
  - The paper draws C-R2's contrast itself: it criticises a connectivity utility that does not depend on new information.

> QS outperforms UN, SC, and MO and slightly outperforms TP with respect to map coverage.

> MO can achieve the highest localizability (when minimizing CRB is the sole or primary objective), but suffers with respect to coverage from not adapting to the amount of untransferred data.

> This finding emphasizes that the choice of kq, kQ, kZ, kY which maximizes coverage depends on characteristics of the environment (see Table I).

- **Reading: close P2** (r2b:3).
  - The two information-adaptive rules deliver more map to the sink than a fixed-weight connectivity objective.
  - No arm is a time budget.
  - The best weights depend on the environment: the tuning-transfer problem that C-R2 wants settled by tuning on other maps.

## E. Control theory: the own-change trigger against the clock at an equal rate (foundational; neighbouring setting)

### Åström & Bernhardsson, "Comparison of Periodic and Event Based Sampling for First-Order Stochastic Systems"

- **Identifier:** 14th IFAC World Congress, Beijing, 1999. The extended CDC 2002 version is "Comparison of Riemann and
  Lebesgue sampling for first order stochastic systems".
- **Version:** Lund University portal copy, file 8520116.pdf.
- **Integrity:** `raw/astrom_lund_8520116.pdf` sha256 ac36c80bf48ae5aa6666c1ce49f9dee74b1209486585a7d918a9412bce47e271.
- **Text:** `r2b/txt/astrom_lund_8520116.txt`

> One possibility is to sample the system when the output has changed with a specified amount.

> The analysis shows that event based sampling gives better performance than periodic sampling.

> Another way to say this is that one must sample 4.7 times faster with Riemann sampling to get the same mean error variance.

> The figure shows that Lebesgue sampling gives substantially smaller variances for the same average sampling rates.

- **Reading: close P2** (r2b:11), in a neighbouring setting.
  - This is the foundational form of C-R2's comparison. Contact fires when the system's own change since the last contact
    reaches a set amount.
  - It is compared with the clock at an **equal average rate**, and it wins. The 4.7 ratio is from the paper's section on an
    integrator.
  - The record already names event-triggered control as established (`Robotics/DECLARATION.md`).

### Antunes & Hespanha, "Event-triggered control cannot improve the ℓ2 gain of h∞ optimal periodic control and transmit at a smaller average rate"

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2310.17033, **v1** (25 Oct 2023; the only version). The authors' web copy is filed as "TAC23"; the
  venue was not verified.
- **Integrity:** `raw/pdf_2310.17033v1.pdf` sha256 f4b372be761fff92c6e2a16f6e7a1e0277e6b788e80a4a09b9f5259ec3898f9e.
- **Text:** `r2b/txt/pdf_2310.17033v1.txt`

> We show that, under mild assumptions, there does not exist a controller and scheduler pair that strictly improves the optimal attenuation bound of periodic control with a smaller average transmission rate.

> In the h2 control setting, closely related to Linear Quadratic Gaussian Control (LQG), performance is measured by an average quadratic cost. Considered in this setting, ETC can (strictly) improve the average quadratic cost of optimal periodic control for the same, and even smaller, transmission rate;

- **Reading: close P2** (r2b:12). It cuts the other way.
  - Against a worst-case metric, no event trigger beats the best periodic schedule with fewer transmissions. Against an
    average metric, event triggers can win.
  - C-R2 is scored on a mean (artifacts per mission) at an equal rate of failures, which is the favourable case.
  - A gate for C-R2 should report a worst case beside the mean.

## Is the mechanism published? (searcher B, with A)

**Yes. By the caller's grade rule (REDUNDANT if any claim reads states), C-R2 is REDUNDANT on B's claims alone** (r2b:1,
r2b:6), as it is on A's. B's search adds four things A did not have.

1. **P1 is published outside robotics research as well.**
   - A granted patent (Travelers, US 11,710,411 B2) lists a data-amount, map-portion or memory-level trigger for a drone's
     return to communication range, beside a time trigger.
   - The Clark et al. full text gives Spirin's undelivered-information return rule as a formula and runs it. It also makes
     its own controller's return pressure the untransferred information in bits of map entropy (the nearest to CRR's units
     found).
2. **The equal-rate comparison C-R2 proposes is foundational in control theory**, and its outcome depends on the metric.
   - Åström & Bernhardsson (1999): triggering contact on the system's own change beats the clock at an equal average rate,
     on mean variance.
   - Antunes & Hespanha (2023): on a worst-case metric, no own-change trigger beats the best periodic schedule with fewer
     transmissions.
   - Both are in a neighbouring setting (close). They show that C-R2's direction is established, and that its sign is not
     guaranteed off the mean.
3. **The fielded record for P3 is more mixed than A's sources alone suggest.**
   - MARBLE and CoSTAR fielded information or buffer triggers (A).
   - The other SubT teams fielded clocks:
     - CSIRO Data61's successful Urban explore-sync was a three-minute clock, although the team's Field Robotics paper
       describes the return in information terms;
     - Team Explorer used a return-home time and a return on loss of comms;
     - CERBERUS used a time limit;
     - CTU used remaining mission time.
   - The two commercial mine-mapping drones (Emesent Hovermap, Exyn) bound the beyond-comms excursion by goal completion
     (a waypoint, a volume mapped completely, no unexplored area left) or the battery. They use neither a timer nor an
     information unit per excursion.
4. **No contradicting source in C-R2's own setting.** The nearest negative evidence is:
   - neighbouring: Antunes & Hespanha, and the mixed AUV result;
   - A's CoCoPlan N = 3 row;
   - CSIRO's lesson that the return itself costs exploration.

**Not found as such, by either searcher:** a pure accumulated-information threshold for returning to comms against the best
fixed timer **tuned on other maps**, at an **equal rate of failures to return**, counted in **artifacts**. That is C-R2's
exact prediction.
- PRoID (A, r2a:15) is the closest. It adds a learned prediction and sweeps its intervals on the test maps.
- The control-theory comparison is exact in form (equal average rate), but it is in sampling, not exploration.
- "Not found" is never "novel". The mechanism is published, fielded and compared in direction. What remains is one
  benchmark configuration.

## Judgement calls, for the investigator's review

- **r2b:1 read states on a comparator.** Clark et al. publish and run Spirin's rule as a baseline; it is not their own method.
  A read Banfi et al.'s run of the same rule (r2a:10) as states, and B does the same. A reviewer who holds the policy's
  "second-hand" clause strictly would read both as close. The grade still stands on r2b:6 and on A's primary sources.
- **The patent's triggers are alternatives.** The patent lists "a passage of a predetermined amount of time" beside the data
  and map triggers. It shows the progress trigger is claimed prior art, not that it beats the clock.
- **Units.** The published units are:
  - map entropy (Clark);
  - a map-to-queue ratio (Spirin, via Clark);
  - data amount or memory level (patent);
  - voxel counts (r2b:17);
  - location confidence (r2b:13).

  Only Clark's is an information measure in the strict sense. If CRR's A1′ unit (resolvable steps) is required, every source
  is close on the unit alone. The form of the mechanism would still be published.
- **Mine-mapping drones read close, not contradicts.** Their "return when the volume is mapped" is progress-bounded but
  mission-level. They do not compare it with a timer.
- **CSIRO read close, not states, despite the explicit sentence.** The sentence gives no unit, and the same team's detailed
  paper reports a clock for the fielded Urban rule.
- **Second-hand sources:**
  - r2b:13, r2b:14 and r2b:17 rest on surveys;
  - r2b:10 is a journalist's description.

  Dropping all four changes no grade.
- **Window:** Åström & Bernhardsson (1999), Wang et al. (2013) and Klaesson et al. (2019) are older than 2023. They are kept
  because they state the mechanism in its foundational form, as the caller asked.
