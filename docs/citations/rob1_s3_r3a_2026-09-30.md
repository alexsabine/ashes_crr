# ROB1 stage 3, candidate C-R3, searcher A: prior art for progress-indexed runtime failure detection (2026-09-30; R10)

Stage 3 of `Robotics/DECLARATION.md`, candidate **C-R3** of `Robotics/CRR_READING.md` (bottleneck r4-B3, runtime failure
detection for generalist learned policies). The search was for the **specific mechanism**, before any code. This is
searcher A's dossier.

**The mechanism searched (CRR_READING.md, C-R3).**
- A runtime failure detector for a learned robot policy (anomaly or failure prediction, conformal monitoring) whose
  thresholds or statistics are indexed by **task progress, phase or the policy's own accumulated change**. Examples:
  progress-aware, phase-aligned, time-warped or DTW-aligned, or progress-estimation-based monitoring. The alternative is
  indexing by the raw step count.
- The frontier comparator (stage 1, harvester r4:23 and r4:24): conformal thresholds **per step index**. These are SAFE's
  functional conformal band and Hide-and-Seek's time-varying band.
- CRR's prediction: earlier detection at an equal false-alarm rate on rollouts whose speed varies. The must-fail case:
  rollouts that all run at the same speed, where the two clocks coincide.

**Positions** (the caller's):
- **P1:** progress- or phase-indexed failure detection, or conformal calibration, for robot policies is published.
- **P2:** it is shown to detect earlier or more accurately than step-indexed thresholds.
- **P3:** the step-index mismatch (rollouts of different speed) is named as a problem.

**Reading tags** (fixed before the readings; full text in `Robotics/checks/claims_s3_r3a.py`).
- **states:** the source publishes the position as stated, for a robot (a learned policy or a robot behaviour).
  - For P1: a runtime failure or anomaly detector whose threshold, calibration or detection statistic is indexed or
    conditioned on an estimate of progress, phase or the robot's own accumulated change.
  - For P2: such an index compared with a step-indexed threshold and reported earlier or more accurate.
  - For P3: the source says that per-timestep thresholds or calibration fail or are unreliable because rollouts differ in
    speed, length or timing.
- **close:** a nearby mechanism that differs in one named respect. Examples:
  - the index is an execution state or skill, not progress;
  - progress or accumulated change is the monitored signal, not the index of the threshold;
  - the comparator is a fixed or context-free threshold, not a step-indexed one, or the comparison is confounded;
  - the setting is a neighbouring domain (batch process monitoring, functional-data statistics, teleoperated surgery);
  - the step-index problem is named for another reason, or it is answered by adding the clock.
- **contradicts:** the source shows the mechanism, or its limiting case, fails or is inferior to a step-indexed threshold.
- Clock-only frontier sources are listed as **context** (section J) and are not claims.

## A. The foundational robot case: a progress-indexed likelihood threshold (Park et al., ICRA 2016)

### 1. Park, Erickson, Bhattacharjee, Kemp, "Multimodal Execution Monitoring for Anomaly Detection During Robot Manipulation"

- **Identifier:** IEEE ICRA 2016 (Stockholm, 16–21 May 2016; the PDF metadata name the conference; PDF created 15 Feb 2016);
  doi 10.1109/ICRA.2016.7487160 per the search listing.
- **Reachability:** open copy in the Georgia Tech repository,
  https://repository.gatech.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/815fb414-6f8a-4218-99fd-411af2be5bce/content (HTTP 200).
- **Integrity:** `raw/park_icra2016_gatech.pdf` sha256 584ec4c8b61731a96cd206f09ba66a28a01722cfee31d8edc2454b89fa67cb85.
- **Text:** `r3a/txt/park_icra2016_gatech.txt`

> In contrast to prior work, our system also uses a detection threshold that changes based on the execution progress.

> Our execution monitor performs anomaly detection by comparing the log-likelihood, log P(X|λ), of the observations, X, with a threshold, τ(γ), that depends on the estimated execution progress, γ.

> Our system represents execution progress, γ, using the probability mass function over hidden states given the current observations

> Each cluster is a time-based soft cluster that represents execution progress vectors that occurred at similar times during execution

- **Reading: states P1** (r3a:1).
  - The anomaly threshold is indexed by an estimate of execution progress: the HMM's hidden-state distribution inferred from
    the observations so far. It is not indexed by the step count.
  - The system is a robot behaviour (a PR2 pushing doors and switches, and feeding yogurt), not a learned visuomotor policy.
  - The progress variable is the monitor's own HMM state, not the policy's accumulated change in resolvable steps.
  - The clock enters the construction: the progress clusters are radial basis functions in time. At run time the matching
    cluster is chosen by the KL divergence between hidden-state distributions.

> Even during non-anomalous executions, likelihood tends to vary significantly with the number of observations, which reduces the effectiveness of a constant detection threshold.

> Compared to directly using time, this would have the advantage of handling variability in the timing of a behavior’s execution.

- **Reading: states P3** (r3a:2). The progress index is motivated by the variability of execution timing, against indexing
  by time directly. This is C-R3's own argument, published in 2016.

> We also compared the performance of our time-varying likelihood threshold to two baseline methods from the literature: likelihood change detection [23] and fixed-threshold likelihood detection [19].

> The ROC curves in Fig. 10(a) Right show that for any given false-positive rate, our method had a higher true-positive rate than the two baseline methods.

- **Reading: close for P2** (r3a:3).
  - The progress-indexed threshold beats a fixed threshold and a change detector at every false-positive rate.
  - No step-indexed (per-timestep) threshold is among the baselines, so the comparison C-R3 predicts is not made.
  - The authors call their own progress-indexed threshold "time-varying".
  - The journal follow-up (Park, Kim and Kemp, Autonomous Robots 2019), which compares eight detectors, could not be read on
    the day (section K).

> We introduce a varying threshold that changes over the estimated state of a task execution motivated by the dynamic threshold [3].

> In this paper, the state is the latent space representation of observations.

> HMM-GP: A likelihood-based classifier using an HMM introduced in [32]. We vary the likelihood threshold with respect to the distribution of hidden states.

## C. A learned policy whose OOD detector is conditioned on predicted task progress (RAFAIL)

### 3. Schneider, Welte, Rayyes, "RAFAIL: Relationship-Aware Failure Detection for Robotic Manipulation"

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2609.18324, **v1** (16 Sep 2026; the only version).
- **Integrity:** `raw/pdf_2609.18324v1.pdf` sha256 1eb5bdb623481a74f711c88053409a8fe11559713a0d48a1f5b612777aad249e.
- **Text:** `r3a/txt/pdf_2609.18324v1.txt`

> RAFAIL represents individual relationships from point-cloud observations and applies task-progress-conditioned and importance-gated OOD detection to each.

> Consequently, neither the VLM nor the importance/progress labelers are executed at deployment; the policy directly predicts relationship importance

> We use FiLM [20] to condition the RND OOD detector on the current task progress pt.

> A relationship-specific threshold θk ood is calibrated using CP [10].

- **Reading: states P1** (r3a:6).
  - The policy is learned: a ManiFlow flow-matching imitation policy on a UR10e arm.
  - The failure statistic (an RND OOD score per object relationship) is conditioned on task progress. The progress is
    predicted by an auxiliary head of the policy itself.
  - The threshold is calibrated by conformal prediction on successful demonstrations.
  - The progress label is distilled offline from VLM pairwise preferences. It is monotone along the demonstration and is not
    the clock.
  - Differences from C-R3:
    - the conformal threshold itself is one number per relationship; progress enters through the score;
    - no ablation removes the progress conditioning or replaces it by the step index, so P2 is not shown here.

## D. The policy's own progress, read from its activations (Bhardwaj et al.)

### 4. Bhardwaj, Duan, Dan, Ma, Culbertson, "Decoding Task Progress from VLA Representations"

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2608.13474, **v1** (13 Aug 2026; the only version).
- **Integrity:** `raw/pdf_2608.13474v1.pdf` sha256 7f3be0ad3f34e1a75e0be4b8baa7a6d6ac863f68d8b49a53dafc20fa8edffa27.
- **Text:** `r3a/txt/pdf_2608.13474v1.txt`

> we probe the residual stream of π0.5 and find that task progress, the normalized time remaining in a trajectory, is linearly readable from the activations.

> We use the probe as a simple label-free OOD detector, which detects stalled task progress, and find it competitive with state-of-the-art methods.

> which measures the deviation between the predicted and actual progress. The rollout is flagged as out-of-distribution the first time Vτ(t) > δ.

- **Reading: close for P1** (r3a:7).
  - The detector reads the policy's own internal progress: a linear probe on the VLA's activations.
  - It alarms when that progress falls behind the clock-expected progress (one minus elapsed steps over the expected task
    length) by a constant margin.
  - Progress is the monitored signal, not the index of the threshold.
  - The probe is trained to regress normalised time.
  - A slow but successful rollout would be flagged by construction. That is the opposite of C-R3's invariance to rollout
    speed.

> Under per-episode aggregation, Vτ achieves the best seen-task score (0.910) and is competitive on unseen tasks (0.851), trailing only SAFE-MLP (0.917).

> wins both unseen columns (per-replan 0.871, per-episode 0.960), beating both supervised SAFE detectors despite never observing any OOD examples.

- **Reading: close for P2** (r3a:8), and mixed.
  - A progress-based detector is set against SAFE, the step-indexed functional-CP frontier, scored by AUROC on injected image
    perturbations.
  - It wins on held-out perturbation types and trails SAFE-MLP on unseen tasks (0.851 against 0.917).
  - The rollouts are not varied in speed, and the metric is not detection time at an equal false-alarm rate.
  - Progress is the signal here, not the index.

## E. The policy's own accumulated change as the alarm statistic (Sentinel's STAC)

### 5. Agia, Sinha, Yang, Cao, Antonova, Pavone, Bohg, "Unpacking Failure Modes of Generative Policies: Runtime Monitoring of Consistency and Progress" (Sentinel)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2410.04640, **v2** (10 Oct 2024; v1 6 Oct 2024). CoRL 2024 per the arXiv comment.
- **Integrity:** `raw/pdf_2410.04640v2.pdf` sha256 92a6cf1cfdcf05323a0dd929a06ccba62ae70176e24512eda8e4972e608a041c.
- **Text:** `r3a/txt/pdf_2410.04640v2.txt`

> we propose to take the cumulative sum of statistical distances along a trajectory as a measure of the overall temporal consistency in a policy rollout.

> At runtime, we raise a failure warning at the moment that ηt exceeds a failure detection threshold γ, which we calibrate offline using the validation dataset of successful trajectories Dτ.

> we propose to use VLMs to monitor the task progress of the policy by providing them with the robot’s image observations up to the current timestep as a video.

- **Reading: close for P1** (r3a:9), and the nearest in quantity.
  - STAC accumulates the statistical distance (MMD or KL) between successive action-chunk distributions of the policy. That
    is the policy's own accumulated change.
  - It has one conformal threshold, calibrated on the maximum over successful trajectories.
  - The accumulated change is the alarm statistic. It is not the index against which a per-position threshold is read. So
    it is C-R3's quantity in a different role.
  - The separate VLM monitor checks task progress as a signal.

> Differentiating between partial progress and task failure can be ambiguous for a slow moving robot, and thus, we also specify the current elapsed time t and the time limit for the task H.

- **Reading: close for P3** (r3a:10). The speed problem is named: a slow robot looks like a failing one. The remedy is the
  reverse of C-R3's: the monitor is given the clock and the time limit.

## F. Phase-specific detectors in teleoperated surgery (Yasar and Alemzadeh)

### 6. Yasar, Alemzadeh, "Real-Time Context-aware Detection of Unsafe Events in Robot-Assisted Surgery"

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2005.03611, **v2** (18 Jun 2020; v1 7 May 2020). DSN 2020 per the arXiv comment.
- **Integrity:** `raw/pdf_2005.03611v2.pdf` sha256 412f851a6f997bdfe2207b488db8c8c21b862373179ba83a1f008da5316e4ea2.
- **Text:** `r3a/txt/pdf_2005.03611v2.txt`

> Our approach integrates a surgical gesture classifier that infers the operational context from the time-series kinematics data of the robot with a library of erroneous gesture classifiers that given a surgical gesture can detect unsafe events.

> Being context-specific results in more accurate de-tection of erroneous gestures but worse reaction times.

> Table (VIII) shows that there is an improvement of 14.1% and 16.2% of AUC over non-context specific detection, for Suturing and Block Transfer tasks, respectively.

- **Reading: close for P2** (r3a:11), and mixed.

  - The comparator is phase-free, not step-indexed. The operator is a surgeon, not a learned policy.

### 7. Lacoquelle, Pucel, Travé-Massuyès, Reymonet, Enaux, "Warped Time Series Anomaly Detection" (WETSAND)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2404.12134, **v1** (18 Apr 2024; the only version). Vitesco Technologies, ONERA and LAAS-CNRS.
- **Integrity:** `raw/pdf_2404.12134v1.pdf` sha256 c1e3ce6b83fbed0877343c2c73861ecd920dfa7c781b16fe25cd0884c158e911.

- **Reading: states P3** (r3a:12).
  - The robots are Universal Robots arms on a production line, running scripted programs, not learned policies.
  - Cycles of different duration are named as the obstacle to anomaly detection.
  - The answer is dynamic time warping to a Soft-DTW barycenter prototype.

> Given that the 2σ threshold results in F2 = 0.69 and the boxplot threshold results in F2 = 0.92, the threshold is set at the boxplot threshold for the experiments that follow.

> The time series are first last-value-padded to 24576 time steps, which is the maximal number of time steps of the cycles in the training set.

> It yields an F2 score of 0.23, which is better that the previous CAE but is still much lower than the anomaly detection performance with WETSAND.

- **Reading: close for P2** (r3a:13).
  - The time-warped detector (F2 0.92) far exceeds autoencoders run on step-indexed, padded cycles (F2 0.10 and 0.23), on
    real robot cycles.
  - The comparison is confounded: the model class differs. The authors attribute the autoencoders' failure to their
    reconstructing normal and abnormal cycles equally well.
  - It is offline and per cycle, not a runtime monitor, and there is no detection-time measure at an equal false-alarm rate.

## H. The same prediction, shown in process monitoring and functional-data statistics

> However, the identification of the appropriate reference distribution is a non-trivial problem, because processes could exhibit different temporal dynamics.

> FRTM method is compared with two simpler natural competing approaches, namely the monitoring in real-time with no alignment, referred to as NOAL, and the pointwise monitoring approach of the functional quality characteristic, referred to as PW.

- **Reading: close for P2** (r3a:14), close only by domain. This is C-R3's prediction, shown in statistical process

> We attribute this to the fact that these threshold types effectively compare the failure prediction score at timestep t against the scores in the calibration dataset for the same timestep.

## Judgement calls, for the investigator's review

- **The index in Park et al. (2016).** Execution progress is the HMM's hidden-state distribution, which is estimated from
  the observations, so it is an own clock of the behaviour.
  - The progress clusters are built with radial basis functions in time, and the authors call the result a "time-varying
    likelihood threshold".
  - It is read **states** for P1, because the threshold at run time is selected by the estimated progress, not by the step.
  - A reviewer who requires the index to be free of the clock at construction would read it **close**. RAFAIL would then
    still carry P1 (its progress label is distilled from VLM preferences, not the clock).
- **RAFAIL's progress enters the score, not the threshold.** Its conformal threshold is one number per relationship; the
  OOD score is FiLM-conditioned on predicted progress. The caller's P1 names "thresholds or statistics", so it is read
  **states**. Under a threshold-only reading it would be close, and P1 would rest on Park et al. alone.
- **Robot behaviour against learned policy.** Park et al. monitor a hand-designed PR2 behaviour. RAFAIL, FIPER, FoMo-FD,
  VLA-FAIL and Lee and Har monitor learned policies. The P1 grade does not depend on Park.
- **The frontier's answer to P3 is a constant threshold, not a progress index.** FIPER, VLA-FAIL, Lee and Har and FoMo-FD all
  name the mismatch. Three drop the time index altogether; FoMo-FD proposes the progress index as future work. FIPER also
  reports that the per-timestep band still gives the best overall TWA. So the published trade-off is between the step band
  (sensitive, with false alarms under timing variation) and one constant (robust, less sensitive). C-R3's progress-indexed band
  would sit between them. That is the untested comparison.
- **Two cautions against C-R3's "earlier".**
  - Yasar and Alemzadeh: phase-specific detectors were more accurate but later, because the phase had to be inferred
    online.
  - Willibald and Lee: an argument (not measured) that both time and progress indexing assume consistent profiles, in
    favour of a state index.
  - Neither is a step-indexed comparison, so neither is read contradicts.
- **Bhardwaj et al. go the other way on speed.** The detector alarms when the policy's own progress lags the clock, so slow
  successful rollouts are flagged. It is the must-fail case of C-R3 in reverse: the own clock is compared with the wall
  clock, not used instead of it.
- **The neighbouring domains** (FRTM, Wang et al., Rocha de Oliveira and de Juan) are read **close** by the reading policy.
  They are included because they state the mechanism's prediction and show it: registration or maturity indexing beats
  no-alignment monitoring, including earlier signals in FRTM. A reviewer who counts the domain as irrelevant loses no robot
  claim by dropping them.
- **Second-hand and not-reached sources.**
  - The HMM-GP description (r3a:4) is second-hand. Nothing hangs on it.
  - The Park, Kim and Kemp (2019) journal paper is the single most likely place for a robot P2 comparison (progress-based
    against other thresholds, eight detectors). It could not be read.
- **The window.** Park et al. (2016), Park, Hoshi and Kemp (2017) and Yasar and Alemzadeh (2020) are older than 2023. They
  are kept because they state the mechanism in its foundational robot form.
