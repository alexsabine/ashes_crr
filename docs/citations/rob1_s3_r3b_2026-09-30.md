**The mechanism searched (CRR_READING.md, C-R3).**
- A runtime failure detector for a learned robot policy (anomaly or failure prediction, conformal monitoring). Its thresholds
  or statistics are indexed by **task progress, phase or the policy's own accumulated change**, not by the raw step index.
- **The frontier comparator:** conformal thresholds **per step index** (SAFE's functional conformal band; FAIL-Detect's
  time-varying band).
- **CRR's prediction:** earlier detection at an equal false-alarm rate on rollouts whose speed varies.
- **The must-fail case:** rollouts that all run at the same speed, where the two clocks coincide.

**Positions** (the caller's):
- **P1:** progress- or phase-indexed failure detection, or conformal calibration, for robot policies is published.
- **P2:** it is shown to detect earlier or more accurately than step-indexed thresholds.
- **P3:** the step-index mismatch (rollouts of different speed) is named as a problem.

**Reading tags.** These are searcher A's, copied unchanged so that the two searches grade alike (full text in
`Robotics/checks/claims_s3_r3b.py`).
- **states:** the source publishes the position as stated, for a robot (a learned policy or a robot behaviour).
- **close:** a nearby mechanism that differs in one named respect:
  - the index is an execution state, not progress;
  - progress is the signal, not the index;
  - the comparator is fixed or context-free, or the comparison is confounded;
  - the domain is a neighbouring one;
  - the step-index problem is answered by adding the clock.
- **contradicts:** the source shows the mechanism, or its limiting case, fails or is inferior to a step-indexed threshold.
- Clock-side sources, and sources read for context, are listed in sections G and H. They are not claims.

## A. A learned policy whose conformal band is read at the aligned nominal timestep (FIDeL; not found by A)

### Rolland, Mayran de Chamisso, Mouret, "Failure Identification in Imitation Learning via Statistical and Semantic Filtering" (FIDeL)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2604.13788 **v1**, 15 Apr 2026, the only version.
- **Venue:** the arXiv comment reads "8 pages, Appendix coming soon, accepted at ICRA 2026"; the authors' page gives the
  ICRA 2026 bibtex.
- **Integrity:** `raw/pdf_2604.13788v1.pdf` sha256 4a018d7be1d60003aa8bb4e59b34f43b6e5a40589b5e19a860a6481f358259a8.
  The author's copy (`raw/rolland_icra2026_failure.pdf`, sha256
  1112cb01bc882e5f40d89de325d10b7169e48abd695cee3cebb495a17cfd3bc1, created 18 Feb 2026) has the same text in these
  passages.
- **Text:** `r3b/txt/pdf_2604.13788v1.txt`

> We extend Conformal Prediction to handle both temporal and spatial variations, with memory-based temporal alignment that ensures robustness to variable execution speeds.

> The final anomaly score is obtained as the minimum transport cost across nominal timesteps:

> We extend the Conformal Prediction (CP) framework [18], [19] to define a dynamic threshold for our anomaly detector DA, accounting for both temporal structure and patch-level variations.

> At runtime, the current observation is aligned to its closest nominal timestep tmin using OT (Eq. (3)), and patch-level scores are compared to their corresponding bounds

> We additionally evaluated FIDeL on more realistic data by performing inference on trajectories generated with ACT [3], an IL policy.

- **Reading: states P1** (r3b:1). This is the nearest published instance of C-R3's structure that either searcher found.
  - The conformal bound is a band **per nominal timestep** of the demonstrations. That is the form of SAFE's and
    FAIL-Detect's bands.
  - At run time the band is **read at the nominal timestep that the current observation is matched to**, not at the step
    count. The match is the arg-min over nominal timesteps of an optimal-transport cost.
  - The stated purpose is robustness to variable execution speeds.
  - The monitored policy is learned (ACT rollouts on a real soldering task). The BotFails set is teleoperated.
- **Differences from C-R3:**
  - the index is a position in the demonstration found by **appearance matching**, not the policy's accumulated change;
  - nothing forces it to be monotone;
  - the detector reads observations only, and is independent of the policy.

> The closest work to ours, FAIL-Detect [12], performs runtime failure detection in imitation learning with Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNF) [47], but does not distinguish benign anomalies from task-critical failures and assumes that the task is performed with the same temporal consistency during inference as in demonstrations.

> Instead, our method relies on a representation based formulation that captures both temporal progression and spatial structure, enabling alignment across variable execution speeds, localized heatmaps, and more interpretable scores.

- **Reading: states P3** (r3b:2).
  - The frontier's time-varying band (FAIL-Detect, one of C-R3's step-indexed comparators) is said to assume that test
    rollouts keep the demonstrations' timing.
  - The remedy is alignment across speeds, which is C-R3's remedy. It is not the constant threshold that FIPER, VLA-FAIL and
    Lee and Har chose (r3a:17, r3a:19, r3a:20).

> CP-time, a standard conformal prediction approach using only temporal deviations from reference demonstrations.

> Gaussian assumption, a simpler baseline with thresholds derived from a Gaussian fit of calibration scores (mean and variance) at each timestep.

> Comparing CP variants, CP-time&space consistently improves over CP-time.

> Second, the method’s spatial and temporal invariance, though useful for robustness, can reduce sensitivity to fine-grained or order-dependent deviations.

> This is particularly problematic for non-Markovian anomalies, where the correctness of an action depends not only on the current state but also on the history of states or actions that preceded it.

- **Reading: close P2 at most, and a caution** (r3b:3).
  - The thresholding baselines differ in how the band is built (temporal CP, temporal and spatial CP, a Gaussian fit), not
    in its index.
  - None is described as read at the raw step index. So the aligned index is never set against the step index, and no
    detection time is reported.
  - If the Gaussian baseline is read at the raw step, the comparison is confounded with the Gaussian assumption.
  - **The caution:** the authors name the cost of the invariance, without measuring it. Alignment by appearance loses
    order- and history-dependent faults.

## B. The dissertation behind Park et al. (ICRA 2016; Autonomous Robots 2019), which A could not read

### Park, "A Multimodal Execution Monitor for Assistive Robots" (PhD dissertation, Georgia Institute of Technology, May 2018)

- **Version:** the dissertation PDF from the Georgia Tech repository. The title page reads "May 2018" and "Date Approved:
  January 17, 2018"; the PDF was created 8 Mar 2018. The advisor was C. C. Kemp.
- **Integrity:** `raw/park_dissertation_gatech2018.pdf` sha256
  4e7a433e255f0dfbef062df298f4c0d1e263b7b1a76a133205574750d40e582e.
- **Text:** `r3b/txt/park_dissertation_gatech2018.txt`
- **Basis:** section 3.2 cites as its basis the ICRA 2016 paper (A's r3a:1 to r3a:3) and the Autonomous Robots paper, which
  was then "submitted" (refs [42, 142]).

> Narrowing down the range of operations, Serdio et al. introduced a time-varying threshold, allowing a tight decision boundary around low variance area for rolling mills [98]. However, time variations, such as delay or speed variation, easily break down the detection system.

> To address these concerns, Rodriguez et al. set cutoff probability thresholds over discretized time slices [58].

> The use of a time-varying likelihood threshold is similar to our work, but we do not directly use observations. Instead, we vary the threshold with respect to the hidden-state distribution, execution progress, estimated by an HMM.

> Compared to the direct use of time, the representation would have the advantage of handling variability in the timing of a behavior execution, but the true state path is hidden from the observer.

- **Reading: states P3** (r3b:4). This is plainer than the ICRA paper (r3a:2).
  - A time-varying, step-indexed threshold "easily" breaks under delay or speed variation, and the progress index is
    introduced against it.
  - Two step-indexed precursors are named: Serdio et al. (rolling mills) and Rodriguez et al. (robot assembly, thresholds
    over discretised time slices). Neither was read here.

> For the detector, we combine two multimodal anomaly detectors, referred to as HMM-D [42], that use multivariate hidden Markov models (HMMs) and dynamic thresholds to determine anomalies.

> HMM-D is a binary (one-class) detector that learns a model from non-anomalous task executions and detects anomalies when the log-likelihood of a sequence of input signals is lower than a time-varying threshold. HMM-D dynamically changes the threshold depending on the progress of a current task execution.

> We then evaluated our execution monitoring system with Henry Evans, a person with quadriplegia in California, USA.

- **Reading: states P1** (r3b:5), for a robot behaviour.
  - It is the same method as r3a:1, run inside the group's feeding system on a PR2 and evaluated with a care recipient.
  - It adds no new mechanism. It shows that the progress-indexed threshold was used in a system tested with an end user.

> HMM-F: A likelihood-based classifier with a fixed threshold [101].

> The HMM-D and -GP methods resulted in shorter detection delays with higher true positive rates.

> In this evaluation, we tuned the three detectors to have the same, small false positive rate (FPR) given a test set.

> While we do not explicitly test tasks that involved large timing variations, our tasks have timing variations as illustrated in Figure 3.4.

- **Reading: close P2** (r3b:6). This is the nearest robot result on C-R3's own metric.
  - At an equal, small false positive rate, the two progress-indexed thresholds have a shorter detection delay than a
    fixed threshold. The anomalies are simulated steps added to real feeding data.
  - The comparator is **fixed, not step-indexed**.
  - The author states that large timing variations were not tested.
  - Table 3.2 has eight methods (HMM-D, HMM-GP and six baselines); none is step-indexed.
  - Whether the published journal version (AuRo 2019, reached by neither searcher) differs was not checked.

> Cluster membership for HMM-D is based on temporal similarity. We also introduce another clustering-based algorithm, HMM-KNN, that uses clusters based on execution progress similarity.

> HMM-D shows similar but slightly lower performance, but it outperformed HMM-KNN. This result shows the training with the time-based RBFs was beneficial.

- **Reading: close P2, and a caution** (r3b:7).
  - Both detectors read their threshold at the estimated progress at run time.
  - The one whose progress clusters were **built with the clock** (time-based radial basis functions) beat the one built on
    progress similarity alone (AUC, Table 3.2).
  - It is not a step-indexed comparator, so it is not read contradicts.
  - It warns that the purely own-clock construction was not the better one in the one robot study that tried both.

## C. A latent-state threshold on executions of varying speed (Wu, Guan and Rojas; A could not fetch it)

### Wu, Guan, Rojas, "A Latent State-Based Multimodal Execution Monitor with Anomaly Detection and Classification for Robot Introspection"

- **Identifier:** Applied Sciences 9(6):1072, 2019. Published 14 Mar 2019 (received 14 Jan 2019, accepted 2 Mar 2019).
- **Version and reachability:** the open-access PDF from MDPI's resource server
  (`mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/applsci/applsci-09-01072/...`). The article pages on mdpi.com returned HTTP 403, as A found.
- **Integrity:** `raw/wu_applsci2019_mdpires.pdf` sha256 d73969f3e718ea1a3a4f9683a9275ef061ce175737ef6be91b55ad1b1edded8b.
- **Text:** `r3b/txt/wu_applsci2019_mdpires.txt`

> The detector uses a dynamic log-likelihood threshold that varies by latent state for anomaly detection

> with a learned threshold ρ(z) that derives from the most likely estimated hidden state given current observations.

> Figure 7 illustrates hidden-state partitions for 20 nominal executions for Skill 3 that were carried out with varying speeds and goals.

- **Reading: close P1** (r3b:8).
  - The threshold is indexed by the most likely latent state of a nonparametric HMM. That is an execution state within a
    skill, not an ordered progress variable.
  - The nominal executions vary in speed, so the index is meant to be free of the clock.
  - The robot is a scripted Baxter kitting behaviour with DMP skills, not a learned policy.

> We compare the results of our execution varying threshold with a fixed threshold presented in [2].

> From Figure 9, we see that for executions 2 and 4 our varying threshold detector identified anomalies that the fixed threshold method could not.

> This improved sensitivity reduces false negatives and helps us flag anomalies at times closer to the ground truth.

> Our results indicate that while the GD system had the HSD resulting in 16.6% better precision, our system had 61.7% better recall.

- **Reading: close P2** (r3b:9).
  - The state-indexed threshold detects closer to the labelled onset, and misses less, than a fixed threshold.
  - Its precision is lower than a gradient-based detector's.
  - The comparators are fixed and gradient-based, not step-indexed. The timing evidence is two executions in a figure.

## D. The per-step band on duration-normalised movement-primitive time (Pastor et al., ICRA 2011)

### Pastor, Kalakrishnan, Chitta, Theodorou, Schaal, "Skill Learning and Task Outcome Prediction for Manipulation"

- **Version:** the ICRA 2011 conference PDF. Its metadata read "2011 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and
  Automation, May 9-13, 2011, Shanghai", created 11 Feb 2011. The copy is on a CMU faculty page.
- **Integrity:** `raw/pastor_icra2011_cmu.pdf` sha256 5b54fa943f14c4a0fab3535570732b908b4b06addbaeb76d3a4fc6ad25757e76.
- **Text:** `r3b/txt/pastor_icra2011_cmu.txt`

> The function f does not directly depend on time; instead, it depends on a phase variable s, which monotonically changes from 1 towards 0 during a movement and is generated by the canonical system given by:

> The recorded signals were truncated using the start and end time of the DMP and are re-sampled such that each signal only contains 100 samples.

> monitor all sensor signals and compute at each time step for each signal a z-test using the weighted mean and weighted standard deviation from the training phase under the null-hypotheses with confidence c.

  - The policy is reinforcement-learned (PI2) chopstick manipulation on a PR2.

### Kawai, Nagahama (Kawasaki Jukogyo KK), US 2023/0264355 A1, "State monitoring device, state abnormality determination method, and state abnormality determination program"

> a key feature of the DTW method is that it allows for nonlinear expansion and contraction of the time-series data in the direction of the time axis when calculating the degree of similarity.

> the DTW method can obtain the degree of dissimilarity (DTW distance) of two waveforms in a way that does not reflect differences in the time axis direction of the two waveforms, but reflects well differences in the amplitude and other aspects of the waveforms.

## G. Context: the own-progress index in other communities (not claims; each quote verified like the claims)

- **Rotating machinery: order tracking.** Signals are re-indexed from time to shaft angle to remove speed variation. This is
  the textbook form of "the system's own progress as the index" in condition monitoring.
  - Source: Wikipedia, "Order tracking (signal processing)", revision 1374117927 (last edited 9 Sep 2026).
  - `raw/wikipedia_order_tracking.html` sha256 9168c614f3be3b1a01c441042d26223deb67ed304981d915b5e3e7e1663dda22.
  - A tertiary source. The robot-reducer papers that apply it (SAGE, ScienceDirect, IOPscience) were paywalled or served
    script pages.

> In rotordynamics, order tracking, also known as angular resampling, is a family of signal processing tools aimed at transforming a measured signal from time domain to angular (or order) domain.

> Top: Shaft speed is integrated to find shaft phase, determining new sample times with constant angular increments. Bottom: A time-domain signal is resampled into the cycle domain, correcting for speed variations.

- **Robot learning from demonstration: arc length as the alignment clock.** CRR's own-change index (the arc) is used in
  robotics to align demonstrations, not to monitor them.
  - Source: Braglia, Tebaldi, Lazzaretti, Biagiotti, "Arc-Length-Based Warping for Robot Skill Synthesis from Multiple
    Demonstrations", arXiv:2410.13322 **v2**, 28 Feb 2025 (v1 17 Oct 2024). The journal reference is IROS 2025.
  - `raw/pdf_2410.13322v2.pdf` sha256 dc2608db2bf5e747cd8a01d0b3ca3ec61f95f5618882b3d0df6f8dc3e4c4d944.

> specifically designed for robot trajectories, that enables time-independent alignment of the trajectories by providing an arc-length parametrization of the signals.

> This approach eliminates the need for temporal alignment, enhancing the accuracy and robustness of skill representation, especially when recorded movements are subject to intermittent motions or extremely variable speeds

- **"Time-aware" VLA failure detection (NTU) is about timeliness, not the index.** A could not reach this record. B read its
  metadata through the NTU DSpace REST API; the web page served "Human Verification" again.
  - Source: Ma, "Time-aware failure detection for vision-language-action models in robot manipulation", master's thesis by
    research, NTU, 2026, hdl 10356/218808.
  - `raw/ntu_dspace_api_search.json` sha256 53c84f09031b1fec97f7f40f608811ddb5f3fe2a63c2c230081f772e067c58ec.
  - The full text was not reached.
