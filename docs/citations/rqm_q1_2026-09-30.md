# RQM citation checks, family Q1: exemplar-free class-IL with prototypes and class statistics, and drift compensation (2026-09-30; R10)

Family Q1 of the RQM frontier sweep (`Replay_Quality_Memory/DECLARATION.md` §2): exemplar-free class-incremental learning
(EFCIL) that keeps class prototypes or class feature statistics (means, covariances, Gram matrices), prototype augmentation,
feature translation, and compensation of the drift of stored statistics. All sources were fetched on the day (2026-09-30).
Quotes are copied from the text extracted from the fetched file (PDF text by pymupdf); whitespace is normalised and
line-break hyphens are rejoined (a genuine hyphen split at a line end is kept, for example "state-of-⏎the-art" is written
"state-of-the-art"); nothing else is changed. "[...]" marks an omission. Table rows are quoted as the extractor emitted
them (the extractor renders a cross mark in FeCAM's Table 3 as "%"). Mathematics that the extractor garbled is re-typed in
the prose around the quote and marked as re-typed. Fetched files are held outside the repository
(`/tmp/claude-0/rqm_src/q1/`); their sha256 is recorded here. Code links were **not checked**: github.com and
api.github.com returned HTTP 403 through the session proxy on 2026-09-30 for every repository tried (PASS, SDC, FeCAM, ADC,
LDC, DPCR, APR, GATF, EFC, AdaGauss); this is a reachability limit of the session, not evidence that the links 404.

**How the numbers below may be read.** Every class-IL number is the paper's own, on its own protocol (ResNet-18 on
CIFAR-100 unless stated). Numbers from different papers are not comparable across papers: the same method on the same split
is reported at 46.1 (AdaGauss's paper) and 46.57 (CIRCLE's re-run), and APR's re-implementation of LwF reaches 49.19 where
EFC reports 32.80. **Cold Start (CS)** is an equal split of classes from a random initialisation; **Warm Start (WS)** is a large
first task (usually half the classes). A_last / A_step / A_f / A_T is accuracy over all seen classes after the last task
(the declaration's score); A_inc / A_avg is the mean over steps.

**Search coverage.** arXiv search (arxiv.org/search; the export API returned HTTP 429 and was not used) for
"exemplar-free" class-incremental (newest first, 50 results), "semantic drift compensation", "drift compensation
class-incremental", "cold start exemplar-free", "learnable drift compensation", "survey exemplar-free" and "survey
non-exemplar" (title). **No dedicated survey of exemplar-free class-IL was found on arXiv by those searches**; the general
class-IL survey of Zhou et al. (TPAMI) is included. Out of scope here and left to Q3: exemplar-free methods on pre-trained
ViTs (e.g. arXiv:2511.09926, 2602.00144) and the analytic family (ACIL, DS-AL), which appear below only as baselines in other
papers' tables.

Position ids M1–M7 are the declaration's (§2). Reading tags: **states** (the source asserts the position), **close** (asserts
something near it), **bears** (evidence relevant to it without asserting it), **contradicts** (asserts or shows the opposite,
in whole or in part).

## 1. PASS, prototype augmentation and self-supervision (Zhu et al., CVPR 2021)

- **Identifier:** Fei Zhu, Xu-Yao Zhang, Chuang Wang, Fei Yin, Cheng-Lin Liu, "Prototype Augmentation and Self-Supervision
  for Incremental Learning", CVPR 2021, pp. 5871–5880 (pages as cited in the Zhou et al. survey below). **Not on arXiv** (an
  arXiv title search finds only the extension PASS++, §2). Fetched from CVF Open Access,
  https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Zhu_Prototype_Augmentation_and_Self-Supervision_for_Incremental_Learning_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf
  (the open-access camera-ready; CVF gives no version number), fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF `pdf_PASS_cvpr2021.pdf` sha256 e07c5ab34977d83e1f190b0447e4b2d73d9ab5f89bf1904e0e76dfdf442e8ec3.
- **Mechanism:** store one mean per class; replay each old class as its mean plus isotropic Gaussian noise of radius r (a
  scalar from the average feature variance) to train the unified classifier; add rotation self-supervision and feature
  distillation. **Stores:** one d-vector per class (d = 512 for ResNet-18) plus one scalar r. **Backbone:** trained from
  scratch; warm start (half the classes in the first task).

> However, storing data from previous tasks is limited by memory or privacy issues, and generative models are usually unstable and inefficient in training.

> To alleviate distortion of the feature space when learning new task, we compute and memorize one prototype (class mean) for each classes:

> r is a scale to control the uncertainty of the augmented prototypes. In particular, the scale r can be pre-defined, or computed as the average variance of the class representations:

> ResNet-18 [19] is used and trained from scratch in our experiments.

> Except for one setting on CIFAR-100, we mainly train the model on half of classes for the first task, and equal classes in the rest phases.

> In addition, our method outperforms the strong baseline method, iCaRL-NCM [43], by 3.7% on CIFAR-100 (10 phases), and achieves comparable accuracy with state-of-the-art exemplar-based approaches which are based on many saved samples overall.

> Therefore, we emphasize that the feature extractor bias should be considered and more future effort should be put into task-agnostic representation learning for IL, especially for non-exemplar based CIL.

- **Numbers:** PASS's own class-IL accuracies are given in figures (Figs. 5–7), not transcribed here. Under EFC's common
  protocol (§6), PASS on CIFAR-100 is 53.42 (WS, 10 step) and 30.45 (CS, 10 step) A_step.
- **Reading.** M1 **close**: warm start, a backbone trained on half the classes, "comparable accuracy" with exemplar-based
  methods (the paper's claim; the replay numbers are in figures). M2 **bears**: it names "feature extractor bias" and
  "distortion of the feature space" as the problem to be solved, not drift of stored means by that name. M5 **bears**: it
  motivates prototypes by generative models being "unstable and inefficient". M7 **bears** only through the later CS number
  (30.45, §6): PASS's own evidence is warm start.

## 6. EFC, Elastic Feature Consolidation (Magistri et al., ICLR 2024)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2402.03917, current version **v3** (30 May 2024; v1 6 Feb 2024, v2 11 Mar 2024), comment "Accepted at
  Twelfth International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2024)", fetched 2026-09-30. Simone Magistri, Tomaso
  Trinci, Albin Soutif-Cormerais, Joost van de Weijer, Andrew D. Bagdanov.
- **Integrity:** PDF (v3) `pdf_2402.03917v3.pdf` sha256 0d7badd6c5c1d5dbc3c763c2675bd46cbcc59e644a5f5f057aa8c7efddf7a4cf; abstract page sha256
  9c5bc22d7d4be505e297f59e415e7d9b6ea6b18d2579adab2a2f24c51c09d585.
- **Mechanism:** regularise feature drift in the directions that matter to the old classifier through an Empirical Feature
  Matrix (EFM, a second-order approximation of the KL between old-head predictions at perturbed features); replay Gaussian
  prototypes (mean and covariance) with an asymmetric loss; update stored means by SDC-style drift weighted in the EFM
  pseudo-metric. **Stores:** a mean and a covariance per class (or one per task, or a low-rank approximation) and the EFM.
  **Backbone:** trained from scratch; the paper introduces the Cold Start / Warm Start split for EFCIL.

> In this paper, we consider the challenging Cold Start scenario in which insufficient data is available in the first task to learn a high-quality backbone. This is especially challenging for EFCIL since it requires high plasticity, which results in feature drift which is difficult to compensate for in the exemplar-free setting.

> We define Warm Start, differently than Ash & Adams (2020), as continual learning with a large initial task, and Cold Start, consistent with Hu et al. (2021), for continual learning beginning with a random network initialization.

> EFCIL with Cold Starts faces two main challenges: an alternative to feature distillation is required, since the backbone must adapt to new data, and an exemplar-free mechanism is needed to adapt previous-task classifiers to the changing backbone.

> we can interpret it as a pseudo-metric in feature space and use it to understand which perturbations of features most affect the predicted probability

> The second setting, referred to as Cold Start (CS), uniformly distributes all classes among all tasks.

> We use the standard ResNet-18 (He et al., 2015) trained from scratch for all experiments.
