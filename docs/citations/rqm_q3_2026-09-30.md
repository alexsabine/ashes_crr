# RQM citation checks, family Q3: closed-form / analytic class-IL and pretrained-model class-IL (2026-09-30; R10)

Sources for family Q3 of `Replay_Quality_Memory/DECLARATION.md` (§2): closed-form and analytic learning (ACIL, GKEAL,
DS-AL and successors; streaming LDA), and pretrained-model class-IL (SimpleCIL, RanPAC, EASE, prompt-based methods),
including what these methods assume about the backbone. All sources were fetched on the day (2026-09-30). The current
arXiv version is the one marked current on the abstract page as fetched; the PDF of that version was fetched and its
text extracted with pymupdf. Quotes are copied from the extracted text. Whitespace is normalised and line-break
hyphens are rejoined; a genuine hyphen split at a line end is kept ("exemplar-free", "closed-form"). Ligatures (ﬁ, ﬂ)
are written fi, fl. Nothing else is changed. "[...]" marks an omission. Mathematics that the extractor garbled is not
quoted. It is re-typed in the prose around the quote and marked **(re-typed)**. Table rows are quoted as the extractor
flattened them, and the prose says which columns they are.

Fetched files are held outside the repository in `/tmp/claude-0/rqm_src/q3/`. The sha256 of each fetched file is
recorded here. Every blockquote was checked by a script against the extracted text of its own source (the check
ignores whitespace and hyphens, and applies Unicode NFKC). The count is given at the end.

**Reading tags.** Each source ends with a Reading keyed to the positions M1–M7 of the declaration (§2):
- **states**: the source asserts the position;
- **close**: it asserts something near the position;
- **bears**: its evidence is relevant, without asserting the position;
- **contradicts**: it asserts or shows the opposite of the position, or of the expected grade.

A tag records what the source says. It is not the grade; the grades are set by `checks/grade.py`.

The positions, restated from the declaration:
- **M1**: statistics-based exemplar-free methods approach replay-quality class-IL when the features are fixed or
  pretrained.
- **M2**: representation drift is the main bottleneck.
- **M3**: drift can be compensated from current data only.
- **M4**: analytic learning on fixed features equals joint training exactly, with no stored examples.
- **M5**: generative replay (not in Q3).
- **M6**: stored statistics can leak.
- **M7**: small models trained from scratch stay well below replay.

## A. Analytic (closed-form, recursive least squares) class-IL

### Zhuang et al., "ACIL: Analytic Class-Incremental Learning with Absolute Memorization and Privacy Protection"

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2205.14922, current version **v2** (10 Dec 2022; v1 submitted 30 May 2022); NeurIPS 2022.
  Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF (v2) sha256 aa1bae4b2e1f12e92f470df84e391c7d953b0571583eec24932df7469af7b155; abstract page sha256
  09a20031bfac6f9dbaa100178fa16b06dc45659a5de02af5fedd61657726cdcc.
- **Text file:** `/tmp/claude-0/rqm_src/q3/pdf_2205.14922v2.txt`
- **Mechanism:**
  - Train a CNN by back-propagation on a base task holding half the classes.
  - Freeze it, and append a random ReLU "feature expansion" layer.
  - Fit the classifier by ridge regression onto one-hot labels.
  - At each later phase, update the regularised inverse autocorrelation matrix R and the weights recursively.
- **Stores:** R, of size d_fe × d_fe (d_fe = 8k–15k in the paper), and the weight matrix.

**The update (re-typed from eqs. 4, 7, 8, 9, 10).**
- Base fit: W₀ = (X₀ᵀX₀ + γI)⁻¹X₀ᵀY₀.
- Joint solution at phase k−1: W_{k−1} = (Σ_{i<k} XᵢᵀXᵢ + γI)⁻¹ [X₀ᵀY₀ … X_{k−1}ᵀY_{k−1}].
- The stored matrix: R_{k−1} = (Σ_{i<k} XᵢᵀXᵢ + γI)⁻¹.
- Recursion for R: R_k = R_{k−1} − R_{k−1}X_kᵀ(I + X_kR_{k−1}X_kᵀ)⁻¹X_kR_{k−1}.
- Recursion for W: W_k = [W_{k−1} − R_kX_kᵀX_kW_{k−1},  R_kX_kᵀY_k], where the second block is the new-class columns.
- Throughout, X_i = ReLU(f_CNN(input_i)·W_fe), with the CNN and W_fe frozen.

> The absolute memorization is demonstrated in the sense that class-incremental learning using ACIL given present data would give identical results to that from its joint-learning counterpart which consumes both present and historical samples. This equality is theoretically validated.

> is identical to that obtained by (7) at phase k.

(The fragment above ends Theorem 1. Its proof is in the supplementary material, which was not fetched.)

> Even with several appealing features, the ACIL is naturally not as powerful as the BP-based joint learning. The ACIL is facilitated but also constrained by the fact that it freezes the training of CNN weights.

> That is, the ACIL extracts the feature of new task classes using a somewhat obsolete feature extractor. This would lead to certain performance drop.

> Conventionally, the analytic learning cannot compete with BP [26]. However, if the feature extractor (e.g., CNN layers) is pre-trained with BP with the classifier head designed by analytic learning related techniques, the performance can catch up [27].

> The network is first trained (i.e., phase #0) on the base dataset containing half of the full classes from the original dataset.

> The ACIL and LwF do not keep old data while other compared methods adopt the same replay settings (e.g., [2], [9]) by reserving 20 exemplars per old class.

> for 5-phase CIL, the ACIL gives an accuracy of 66.30% on CIFAR-100, which is slightly worse than the results from several combo techniques such as the “POD+AANets” combo (66.31%) by 0.01% and the “POD+AANets+RMM” combo (68.36%) by 2.06%. However, for 25-phase CIL, the ACIL (with 65.95%) outperforms these combo methods

> Although theoretically the ACIL should give identical results regardless of K, the possible mild drop is likely caused by quantization errors since large K indicates more computation rounds hence more quantization operations

> without the FE process (see the first two rows in Table II), the average incremental accuracy experiences a sharp drop (e.g., 66.30%→52.99%).

> As an example, for fix-exemplar setting, the memory used by storing Rk (8k) on CIFAR-100/CUB200-2011/ImageNet is 8k × 8k = 64 million (M) tensor elements, while other methods consume 6.1M/301.1M/3010.6M respectively

> Instead, the Rk is cached to encrypt information for historical samples. However, it is impossible to reverse-engineer the process to obtain the original samples based on the Rk only, avoiding possible breaching of data privacy.

**Reading (ACIL).**
- **M4: states.** The equality claimed is with *joint ridge regression on the frozen features*. It is not equality with
  joint training of the network; the paper says so ("not as powerful as the BP-based joint learning").
- **M4: bears.**
  - The equality is exact in arithmetic. The paper attributes a drift across K to quantisation error.
  - Without the random expansion, accuracy falls from 66.30 % to 52.99 %.
- **M1: states**, in a warm-start protocol. The base task holds half the classes, and replay (20 exemplars per class)
  is beside it: ACIL is 66.30 against 68.36 at K = 5, and 65.95 against 64.12 at K = 25 (CIFAR-100, average
  incremental accuracy).
- **Matched memory: contradicts** the assumption that statistics are the cheap memory. On CIFAR-100 the stored R is
  64 M elements, against 6.1 M for the 20-per-class exemplar buffer, about ten times larger. It is smaller only on
  large-image datasets.
- **M7: bears.** The backbone is frozen after the base task ("somewhat obsolete feature extractor").
- **M6: contradicts** (claim only). The paper asserts without proof that R cannot be reverse-engineered. Q4 grades this
  claim.

### He et al., "Semantic Shift Estimation via Dual-Projection and Classifier Reconstruction for Exemplar-Free Class-Incremental Learning" (DPCR)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2503.05423, current version **v4** (18 May 2025; v1 7 Mar 2025); ICML 2025 (PMLR 267).
  Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF (v4) sha256 52e91bcc36b5dc237045456b1c24c4708003282dbeceac6f2d8cc340baebff37; abstract page sha256
  a264c52eb5a7038268ee6318016e7b3d776dd38f779b132958662232f0888a45.
- **Text file:** `/tmp/claude-0/rqm_src/q3/pdf_2503.05423v4.txt`
- **Mechanism:**
  - The backbone keeps training on each task, from random initialisation (cold start), with LwF-style distillation.
  - The shift from the old to the new backbone is estimated by a least-squares projection fitted on current-task data
    passed through both backbones.
  - Stored per-class statistics are calibrated by that projection.
  - The classifier is rebuilt by ridge regression from the calibrated statistics.
- **Stores:** per class, the uncentred covariance Φ_c (d × d), the prototype μ_c and the count N_c.

**The update (re-typed, eqs. 5, 12–17).**
- Shift projection: P = (X_oldᵀX_old + εI)⁻¹X_oldᵀX_new, with X_old and X_new the current-task data through the old
  and new backbones.
- Classifier: Ŵ = (Σ_c Φ_c + γI)⁻¹ Σ_c N_c μ_cᵀ y_c.
- Calibration of old statistics: Φ̂_c = PᵀΦ_cP and μ̂_c = μ_cP, each refined per class by a row-space projection.

> In this paper, we focus on the cold-start setting (Magistri et al., 2024), where the model is initialized randomly and the all tasks contains the same number of categories

> Specifically, the embeddings of old tasks shift in the embedding space after learning new tasks, and the classifier becomes biased towards new tasks due to training solely with new data, hindering the balance between old and new knowledge.

> As shown in Eq. (13), the classifier can be constructed via covariance and correlation of each task without directly accessing previous data or embeddings.

> However, due to the constrain of EFCIL, the embeddings of previous tasks cannot be extracted by θt then the covariance and correlation can not be constructed directly.

> Since we can access the backbone θt−1 at task t, the semantic shift between the backbone θt and θt−1 can be estimated by a learnable model that captures the difference between embeddings extracted by θt−1 and θt.

> However, a common issue among existing ACL methods is the limited plasticity due to the frozen backbone.

> It is worth mentioned that methods that freeze the backbone (e.g., ACIL, FeCAM, and DS-AL) are ineffective and demonstrate poor performance, whereas simple baselines like LwF and SDC can perform better. This observation highlights the limitations of freezing the backbone. Although freezing backbone can eliminate the semantic shift, it sacrifice the adaptation in new tasks then obtain low performance.

### Jučas & Pan, "Data-Free Reservoir Features for Efficient Long-Horizon Cold-Start Continual Learning" (CIRCLE)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2606.27095, current version **v1** (25 Jun 2026; only version). Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF (v1) sha256 dfac6709cf625e34981bd480626fd4006ec2d45184e76e6d41acbb55dfb314b5; abstract page sha256

- **Mechanism:**
  - A feature extractor that is never trained on data: an ensemble of fixed random bidirectional 2D image reservoirs
    with random projections.
  - SLDA heads on groups of reservoirs, with their softmax outputs averaged.
- **Stores:** SLDA means, counts and shared covariance per head.

> Cold-start exemplar-free class-incremental learning requires learning a growing set of classes without replay, external pretraining, or a large initial task. Existing cold-start methods typically either train the backbone throughout the stream and compensate for semantic drift, or freeze a backbone after the first task, producing features biased toward the initial classes.

> drift-compensation methods require repeated backbone training and increasingly expensive updates as the task horizon grows, while frozen-backbone methods are cheap but weak under cold start.

> However, in cold-start, the data available before freezing is only a small first task, resulting in non-transferable feature extractors biased towards initial classes.

> This highlights a trade-off within cold-start EFCIL: training ϕ avoids representational bias but incurs drift and cost, while freezing ϕ enables efficient, drift-free updates but suffers from first-task bias.

> Given fixed features and the same regularization rule, the sufficient-statistic update is order-invariant and yields the same classifier as LDA trained jointly on all seen samples.

The rows are from Table 1, cold start, CIFAR-100, with Ā and A_T at T = 10, 20, 50, 100 (mean ± std over 5 seeds):

> EFC++ 66.61 ± 0.2 52.68 ± 0.4 55.74 ± 0.1 42.10 ± 0.5 38.93 ± 0.2 26.95 ± 0.4 26.73 ± 0.6 16.40 ± 0.3

> ACIL 55.36 ± 0.5 40.73 ± 0.4

> CIRCLE 55.32 ± 0.3 45.34 ± 0.3 55.90 ± 0.3 45.34 ± 0.3 56.97 ± 0.3 45.35 ± 0.3 57.22 ± 0.3 45.34 ± 0.3

> EFC++ and AdaGauss reach zero first-task accuracy after roughly 30 and 75 tasks, respectively.

> In short-horizon settings, where only a small number of distribution shifts occur, trained-backbone drift-compensation methods such as EFC++ and AdaGauss outperform CIRCLE.

> Our results show that not all data-free features are sufficient. Random CNNs, simple Patch-RNN reservoirs and scattering features do not match the adapted BiRC2D extractor.

**Reading (CIRCLE).**
- **M2: states, with M2's framing corrected.**
  - In cold start the bottleneck is a trade-off: drift if the features learn, first-task bias if they freeze.
  - Drift is "the main limitation" only of the trained-backbone branch.
- **M3: bears (limit).** Drift-compensation methods are best at T = 10. They decay with horizon; EFC++ has A_T 16.40 at
  T = 100, and first-task accuracy reaches zero after about 30 tasks.
- **M4: states**, for data-free fixed features. A_T is flat at 45.34 across T because continual training equals joint
  training.
- **M7: bears.** From scratch and exemplar-free, the best CIFAR-100 final accuracies are about 45–53 % at T = 10. No
  replay row is given in this table, so M7's comparison with replay is not made here.
- **Closest prior art for a "data-free random features + closed-form head" design on the repository's learner.** For
  tabular data a random feature map needs no spatial structure. The authors warn that "not all data-free features are
  sufficient".
