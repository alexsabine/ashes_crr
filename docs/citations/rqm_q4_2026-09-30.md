# RQM citation dossier Q4: privacy of what exemplar-free continual learners store (2026-09-30; R10)

Family Q4 of `Replay_Quality_Memory/DECLARATION.md` §2: membership inference and data reconstruction from class statistics,
prototypes, Gram matrices, gradients, models and generators; differential privacy (DP) for continual learning; the privacy
claims exemplar-free class-incremental (EFCIL) papers make; and regulatory framing where CL papers invoke it. Every source
below was fetched on 2026-09-30. Quotes are copied from the text extracted from the fetched file (PDF text by pymupdf, HTML
by tag stripping); the comparison normalises Unicode (NFKC, which turns ligatures such as "ﬁ" into "fi"), collapses
whitespace and rejoins words hyphenated across a line break; a genuine hyphen split at a line end is written with its hyphen
(for example "model-inversion") and is checked in a hyphen-lenient pass, counted separately. "[...]" marks an omission.
Garbled mathematics is not quoted; where a number from a table is needed, the extracted table row is quoted as it came out.
Fetched files are held outside the repository (`/tmp/claude-0/rqm_src/q4/`); their sha256 is recorded here. The
readings are the investigator's and are tagged against the declaration's positions M1–M7 as **states** (the source says
the position), **close** (says nearly the position), **bears** (relevant evidence without stating it) or **contradicts**.
Nothing here is a ledger row (R8).

## A. Leakage from aggregates, class statistics and prototypes

### S1. Homer et al., "Resolving individuals contributing trace amounts of DNA to highly complex mixtures using high-density SNP genotyping microarrays" (PLoS Genetics 2008)

- **Identifier:** PLoS Genet 4(8): e1000167, doi 10.1371/journal.pgen.1000167, page metadata citation_date "Aug 29, 2008".
  Fetched 2026-09-30 from https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1000167 (HTML).
- **Integrity:** HTML sha256 ed96d5d2a4a54549022a91de1cd10f1c4956aa605afbb02235add679e0f83d17.
- **Text:** `homer2008.txt`
- **Mechanism:** a test statistic comparing one person's genotype with the mixture's per-SNP allele frequencies and a
  reference population's; the stored object is a vector of means (allele frequencies) over many people.

> These findings also suggest that composite statistics across cohorts, such as allele frequency or genotype counts, do not mask identity within genome-wide association studies.

> including mixtures where an individual contributes less than 0.1% of the total genomic DNA.

**Reading.** M6 **states** (for means): a vector of per-feature means over a cohort admits membership inference when the
dimension is high (here SNP arrays). The mechanism transfers to any high-dimensional class mean (a stored prototype is a
per-dimension mean over a class). What leaks is membership, not a reconstruction of the record.

### S2. Pyrgelis, Troncoso and De Cristofaro, "Knock Knock, Who's There? Membership Inference on Aggregate Location Data"

- **Identifier:** arXiv:1708.06145, current version **v2** (29 Nov 2017; v1 21 Aug 2017); NDSS 2018. Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 7063540f53faae3303e9de39c123370eacfc29491729ef69794e8ce7e103fb18; abstract page sha256
  c68f26692467ddce59c6a59f23f84f94fb1cfe820577a1415cdb5f2e03a3385f.
- **Text:** `1708.06145.txt`
- **Mechanism:** a classifier trained on aggregates with and without the target user; the aggregate is a sum (count
  time-series) over a group of m users.

> We find that membership inference is a serious privacy threat, and show how its effectiveness depends on the adversary’s prior knowledge, the characteristics of the underlying location data, as well as the number of users and the timeframe on which aggregation is performed.

> When groups are small, i.e., m = 5 or 10, all classifiers achieve very high AUC scores. For instance, with m = 10, Linear Regression (LR) and Random Forest (RF) achieve a mean AUC score of 0.97 and 0.99, respectively.

> With larger aggregation sizes, m = 500 or 1,000, performance drops closer to the random guess baseline (AUC = 0.5). Nonetheless, even for groups of 1,000 users, Adv can still infer membership of 60% of the target population with an AUC score higher than 0.6.

> Although differentially private mechanisms can indeed reduce the extent of the attacks, they also yield a significant loss in utility.

**Reading.** M6 **states** for aggregates, with the condition that matters for a class-statistics memory: leakage falls
with the number of contributors to the aggregate, but does not vanish. A class mean or covariance over a small class (few
examples, or the first examples of a new class in an online stream) is in the high-leakage regime.

### S3. Wang et al., "Taming Noise-Induced Prototype Degradation for Privacy-Preserving Personalized Federated Fine-Tuning"

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2604.27833, current version **v1** (30 Apr 2026; only version listed). Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 2a46c2e107dcb1f1a2b9e5d77796e560234588a44f14337635fab2a6353b4eb9; abstract page sha256
  0b1519a3fd14728dd671736db8575b64c234fb86723e0adde8545fa7301f9b1a.
- **Text:** `2604.27833.txt`
- **Mechanism:** two attacks on uploaded class prototypes (class-mean features): Feature-Space Hijacking (optimise an input
  so its encoder feature matches the prototype) and a distance-to-prototype membership inference attack; defence by
  per-example clipping plus Gaussian noise (local DP).

> Prototype-based Personalized Federated Learning (ProtoPFL) enables efficient multi-domain adaptation by communicating compact class prototypes, but directly sharing them poses privacy risks. A common defense involves perexample ℓ2 clipping before prototype computation to bound sensitivity, followed by isotropic Gaussian noise to enforce Local Differential Privacy (LDP).

> FSH actively reconstructs raw client data by optimizing dummy inputs to match targeted prototypes.

> MIA is a passive attack where the adversary aims to determine whether a given sample was part of the model’s training set, under the assumption that training samples lie closer to their class prototypes than non-members.

> Top-1 Hit (%): Percentage of reconstructed images whose nearest class prototype matches the target class.

> - NoLDP 0.9999±0.0000 257.7±65.0 100.00±0.00 0.6729±0.0124

> At ϵ = 1, all three mechanisms drive MIA ROC-AUC to chance (∼0.50) and throttle the FSH Top-1 Hit rate to roughly 20%.

**Reading.** M6 **states** for prototypes: without noise, membership inference against stored class means is above chance
(the quoted Office-Caltech row gives MIA ROC-AUC 0.6729 with no LDP; the same paper's Digits and PACS tables give 0.6094
and 0.5642), and a DP mechanism (clip + Gaussian noise) is what brings it to chance. The "reconstruction" is scored at class
level (Top-1 Hit = the reconstruction lands in the right class), so it shows class-representative inversion, not recovery of
an individual training image.

### S4. Xu et al., "Stabilizing and Improving Federated Learning with Non-IID Data and Client Dropout" (ReBaFL)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2303.06314, current version **v2** (15 Mar 2023; v1 11 Mar 2023). Fetched 2026-09-30.

  c08a86483921f1c00da715cac3ff0147f164f0678306cc03ba54b83e00edce3e.
- **Text:** `2303.06314.txt`
- **Mechanism:** inversion of shared class feature prototypes on CIFAR-10 with an up-convolutional decoder, a VAE and a
  gradient-inversion method (GRNN).

> We simulate the scenarios with possible information leakage risks by presenting several data leakage attacks on CIFAR-10 dataset, including up-convolutional neural network (UpCNN) [60] and variational autoencoder (VAE) [61] that try to recover the input images from the feature representations.

> From the reconstructed results in Fig. 11, we can conclude that sharing feature prototypes will neither leak considerable information about the raw data, nor will they reveal any relationship between different image categories.

> Second, the feature prototype is an average of per-image feature vectors and the characteristics of individual images could be concealed.

**Reading.** M6 **contradicts** (partly): an empirical audit in which inverting class-mean prototypes recovered no
semantically meaningful image. It is one attack family on one dataset, with no membership-inference test; it does not
refute S1–S3, which measure membership rather than reconstruction.

### S5. Chennoufi et al., "PROTEAN: Federated Intrusion Detection in Non-IID Environments through Prototype-Based Knowledge Sharing"

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2507.05524, current version **v1** (7 Jul 2025; only version listed). Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 dbec97bc7bac743a0c5f216a20997b03315ff66a53360f597d49fe621efae7c7; abstract page sha256
  9d31214d757d73967cff90076d95f7547ca2528cbce46259724530f29bf0c821.
- **Text:** `2507.05524.txt`
- **Mechanism:** MSE reconstruction attack by a semi-honest server holding the model and per-class prototypes (tabular
  network-flow features); Gaussian noise on prototypes as DP.

> This attack can only reconstruct class representatives for each participant but not the data itself. The leakage is restricted to a high-level summary rather than a complete reconstruction of the data.

> It implies that on one hand, adding DP alleviates, yet does not completely mitigate the privacy risk in the proposed system, appealing for more effective privacy protection strategies.

**Reading.** M6 **close**: what an attacker recovers from a class prototype is the class representative (the
prototype's own content), which the authors still count as a privacy risk that noise alleviates but does not remove.
Bears on the design question: a stored class mean *is* a class-level summary that an attacker can read back.

### S6. Kim et al., "Federated Semi-Supervised Learning with Prototypical Networks"

- **Identifier:** arXiv:2205.13921, current version **v2** (30 May 2022; v1 27 May 2022). Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 81de2ae934c1da46f9d069336aa2330c719b53754865e9e0f193523f0c5fbc4a; abstract page sha256
  99ba38bf40674f51b8ec14af6d2970bb789429bb59b581d76fdfd6a347bf5e2c.
- **Text:** `2205.13921.txt`

> sharing prototypes is lightweight and thus preferred to sharing model weights, it could be vulnerable to the breach of data privacy as neural network activations could be inverted [32, 13, 47] to reconstruct input data, either from a single or a few images [53].

### S7. Dosovitskiy and Brox, "Inverting Visual Representations with Convolutional Networks"

### S8. Yin et al., "Dreaming to Distill: Data-free Knowledge Transfer via DeepInversion"

- **Identifier:** arXiv:1912.08795, current version **v2** (16 Jun 2020; v1 18 Dec 2019); CVPR 2020. Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 3fecf4762ed6a28d6fc9aff035b971c61022b8af7f4bcd3de23c05934ff7b3ce; abstract page sha256
  acc66851d50f533763eba92af19181e72254f7e819241cc574ecb2d18e3358d3.
- **Text:** `1912.08795.txt`
- **Mechanism:** optimise inputs so that per-layer batch means and variances match the running means and variances
  stored in the network's BatchNorm layers.

> These layers store running means and variances of the activations at multiple layers. In essence, they store the history of previously seen data, at multiple levels of representation.

> By assuming that these intermediate activations follow a Gaussian distribution with mean and variance equal to the running statistics, we show that we can obtain “dreamed” images that are realistic and much closer to the distribution of the training

> It might seem as though a set of training images would be required to obtain [...] but the running average statistics stored in the widely-used BatchNorm (BN) layers are more than sufficient.

**Reading.** M6 **states** and M5 **bears**: stored first- and second-order feature statistics are enough to synthesise
realistic samples of the training distribution. For a design that stores class means and covariances, this is the direct
mechanism by which the stored state can be turned back into class-conditional inputs (distribution-level, not a copy of a
record). It is the same machinery EFCIL's data-free methods use for replay (S27).

- **Identifier:** arXiv:1812.00535, current version **v3** (5 Dec 2018; v1 3 Dec 2018); INFOCOM 2019. Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 97e44f401f8705010f1124be755e9558fb39efb935eb3fc7bf2e9ece0704be93; abstract page sha256

- **Text:** `1812.00535.txt`

## B. Leakage from models and gradients

### S10. Fredrikson, Jha and Ristenpart, "Model Inversion Attacks that Exploit Confidence Information and Basic Countermeasures" (CCS 2015)

- **Identifier:** ACM CCS 2015, doi 10.1145/2810103.2813677 (not on arXiv). Fetched 2026-09-30 from
  https://rist.tech.cornell.edu/papers/mi-ccs.pdf (authors' group page; no version number).
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 8be1f2fcf15dee1f3469b06a4f4a083f0c93b2eb37289c8c39dd43d85ad12649.
- **Text:** `fredrikson2015.txt`

> We experimentally show attacks that are able to estimate whether a respondent in a lifestyle survey admitted to cheating on their significant other and, in the other context, show how to recover recognizable images of people’s faces given only their name and access to the ML model.

> an attacker can produce a recognizable image of a person, given only API access to a facial recognition system and the name of the person whose face is recognized by it.

**Reading.** M6 **bears**: model inversion recovers the class representative; when a class is one person (face

stored class mean of a one-person class is a summary of that person.

## D. Differential privacy for continual learning and for the statistics an exemplar-free memory stores

### S20. Farquhar and Gal, "Differentially Private Continual Learning"

- **Identifier:** arXiv:1902.06497, current version **v1** (18 Feb 2019; only version listed); PiMLAI workshop at ICML 2018.
  Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF sha256 014e97fb28f863f078500f4a19a36b8877b54515e6f809787f1a4e475a6fe81a; abstract page sha256
  5641704ecc10c7b00a23984576dcfc0b0e46241765b07fd4729082444edad1de.
- **Text:** `1902.06497.txt`
- **Mechanism:** variational CL whose old-task likelihood is estimated from DP generative models of the discarded data.

> Catastrophic forgetting can be a significant problem for institutions that must delete historic data for privacy reasons. For example, hospitals might not be able to retain patient data permanently.

> We estimate the likelihood of past data given the current model using differentially private generative models of old datasets.

> Without such guarantees, model inversion attacks against GANs are possible (Fredrikson et al., 2015).

> Existing Variational Inference (VI) derivations for continual learning use the posterior at the end of training on one task as the prior for beginning the next (Nguyen et al., 2018). In addition to hurting multi-task performance in certain settings, this means that privacy costs accumulate over the whole of the training process.

**Reading.** M6 **states** (a generator of old data needs DP to carry a privacy claim). The third quote cites Fredrikson
et al. (S10) for attacks "against GANs"; S10 attacks classifiers and decision trees, not GANs, so the attribution in the
source is loose (noted, not corrected). The fourth quote bears on any memory carried forward across tasks: privacy cost
composes over the stream.

**What Q4 says about M1–M7 (for `checks/grade.py`, to be graded there, not here).** M6 is stated by several independent
sources (S1–S3, S8, S17–S24, S46), so an expected REDUNDANT is supported; the qualification the sources force is that the
demonstrated leakage from class means is membership inference and class-representative recovery, not reconstruction of an
individual example (S4, S5, S9 are the counter-evidence at that level), with the exceptions of small classes (S2) and
one-person classes (S10). No source attacks an analytic (Gram-type) memory or a stored mean-plus-covariance EFCIL memory;
the swept EFCIL papers assert privacy (S26, S29, S31, S32, S34, S37) without testing it, and ACIL itself narrows "privacy"
to "cannot re-use past exemplars" (S31). M1 and M4 are bounded by the DP literature: DP class sums are nearly free on a
frozen pretrained backbone with small domain shift (S22), and DP on Gram statistics breaks the exact joint-equality of
closed-form learning, badly at strong privacy (S24, S25). M5 gets privacy costs on top of its accuracy costs (S17–S19,
S27, S28). M2, M3 and M7 are not addressed by any Q4 source.
