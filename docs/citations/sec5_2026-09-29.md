## Schwarz, Luketina, Czarnecki, Grabska-Barwinska, Teh, Pascanu & Hadsell, "Progress & Compress: A scalable framework for continual learning"

- **Used by:** SEC5's candidates A6-MEAN and A6-MEAN-CLIP (the past importance averaged over settled tasks, not summed).

> [...] (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) focuses on overcoming catastrophic forgetting but the accumulation of Fisher regularisers can over-constrain the network parameters leading to impaired learning of new tasks

> The loss for the ith task is then: −log p(Ti|θ) + 1 2∥θ −θ∗ i−1∥2 γF ∗ i−1 (8) where γ < 1 is a hyperparameter associated with removing the approximation term associated with the previous presen- tation of task i.

> Another interesting side effect is that the method can, via the γ down-weighting, explicitly forget older tasks in a graceful and controlled (rather than catas- trophic) manner.

**Reading.**
- **The problem A6 names is known.** Summed Fisher penalties over-constrain later tasks; online EWC answers it with a
  geometric decay γ < 1 of the running Fisher, a hyperparameter.
- **What the CRR reading changes.** `theory/CRR.md` A6: "regeneration returns a reweighted content, never an accumulated
  count", with MaxEnt weights over occasions normalised to one. With no history constraint (P2 at β = 0, P3 at q → 1) the
  weights are uniform: the past importance is the mean over settled tasks. That is a normalised average, not online EWC's
  geometric decay, and it has no hyperparameter; CRR does not fix β or q, so uniform weights are the zero-knob choice, not
  a CRR prediction. Online EWC's γ is P3's q under another name.
- **Not new as an idea.** Down-weighting accumulated Fisher terms is published; the candidate is a tuning-free member of
  that family, chosen by CRR's A6 wording.
