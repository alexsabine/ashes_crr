**The positions (DECLARATION.md).**
- **S1:** the EWC/Laplace penalty strength λ must be tuned per dataset because the (empirical) Fisher's scale is wrong or
  arbitrary, stated in continual learning or Bayesian deep learning.
- **S2:** rescaling a Fisher or Laplace precision by a factor fitted to the loss's observed curvature (a secant, finite
  difference, line-search or Hessian-vector check) is published.
- **S3:** a curvature scale fitted along the optimisation path (quasi-Newton or secant style) is used to set a
  continual-learning penalty or an online Laplace posterior.
- **S4:** setting the penalty or prior precision without tuning, by a principled rule (Bayes weight, marginal likelihood,
  evidence, or calibration), is published for continual learning.
- **S5:** clipping or bounding the penalty curvature by the step size (the stability edge, lr·λ·F < 2) to prevent divergence
  is published for regularisation-based continual learning.
- **S6:** a tuning-free Laplace or EWC weight is reported to match a tuned λ across many datasets.

- **S4** *states* needs a continual-learning method that sets the weight or prior precision without tuning by a principled

  used untuned, is *close*.

  prevent divergence. A step size adapted to the Fisher or λ, or an importance bound not tied to the step size, is *close*.
- **S6** *states* needs a tuning-free Laplace/EWC weight reported not behind a tuned λ across many datasets. One dataset, a λ
  fixed once and reused across datasets, or a tuning-free Bayesian learner that is not a Laplace weight, is *close*.

### 1. Online structured Laplace (Ritter, Botev & Barber, 2018)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:1805.07810, current version **v1** (20 May 2018; the only version listed), comment "13 pages, 6
  figures"; NeurIPS 2018 per the proceedings reference in arXiv:2502.11756 (not verified on the day). Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF (v1) `pdf_1805.07810v1.pdf` sha256 b7dd89ccda2b153030292482b73844e5572d600da16f43eb7a53316959223d32;
  abstract page sha256 6aaec7c71e380a3c7e638aaa891cb7ca4884e9699acfd6da8157f0b5902b6283.
- **Mechanism:** a Kronecker-factored online Laplace posterior with a multiplier λ on each task's curvature, Λ_{t+1} =
  λH_{t+1} + Λ_t.

> As it acts directly on the parameter of a probability distribution, its optimal value can inform us about the quality of our approximation: if it strongly deviates from its natural value of 1, our approximation is a poor one and over- or underestimates the uncertainty about the parameters.

> We conclude from our results that the online Laplace approximation overestimates the uncertainty in the approximate posterior about the parameters for the permuted MNIST task, in particular with a [...] diagonal approximation to the Hessian. Overestimating the uncertainty leads to a need for regularization in the form of reducing the width of the approximate posterior, as the value that optimizes the validation error is λ = 3.

> We use an identical network architecture to the previous section and found stronger regularization of the approximate posterior to be necessary.

> For the natural choice of λ = 1 (leftmost subfigure respectively), the network’s performance decays for the first task for both curvature approximations, yet it is able to learn the most recent task well.

> We decay the initial learning rate for the second task depending on the hyperparameter to prevent the objective from diverging.

> We set k using a coarse grid search for each value of the hyperparameter λ in order to prevent the objective from diverging towards the end of training, in particular with the Kronecker factored curvature approximation.

- **Reading.** S1 **states**: the Bayes value is λ = 1; the tuned value deviates because the curvature approximation mis-scales
  the posterior, and the tuned value differs between benchmarks (claim f1:0). S6 **bears**: the natural λ = 1 forgets
  (f1:44). S5 **close**: the learning rate is tied to λ, by search, to prevent divergence (f1:38).

## Is SEC4's method known?

**Part by part, as far as this family reached.**

1. **The Laplace penalty at the Bayes weight (task size times the Fisher)** is textbook and published as such. Huszár writes
   it down and notes that EWC replaces N_A by λ. GVCL derives λ = 1. Ritter et al. call λ = 1 "its natural value".
2. **The diagnosis SEC answers (the Fisher's scale is wrong, so λ is re-tuned)** is published. See van de Ven 2025 (the
   Fisher estimator moves λ by orders of magnitude), Ritter et al. 2018 (λ's deviation from 1 measures the approximation's
   error) and NCL 2021 (the Bayes weight is unique in theory, yet ad-hoc re-weighting by orders of magnitude is needed,
   perhaps because of the approximation). S1 has three *states* readings. It also has one *contradicts* (EWC-LoRA: one λ
   across datasets, in a pre-trained low-rank regime).
3. **The units calibration of the Fisher by a curvature measured on the loss (S2)** was **not found** as a rescaling of a
   Fisher by a fitted scalar. The nearest objects:
   - **TMLN** (arXiv:2609.35926 v1, 28 Sep 2026) computes, per parameter, the loss drop observed along the path divided by
     the Fisher's predicted drop, the shape of s = c/ρ. It then max-normalises the ratio and uses it as damping.
   - **Layers Matter** (arXiv:2608.15901 v1) re-weights EWC per layer by Hessian-vector-measured top eigenvalues, with c
     still tuned.
   - **CSQN** (arXiv:2503.19939 v1) corrects the Fisher diagonal additively by quasi-Newton pairs sampled at the endpoint.
   - **Martens** records empirical-Fisher preconditioners being rescaled to the right "scale" in optimisation.
4. **A curvature fitted along the path travelled and used as the continual-learning penalty (S3)** is published, if SI is

## Judgement calls, for the investigator's review

- **S3: SI read *states*. This is the call that could move the verdict.**
  - **For states:** SI fits a quadratic along the path travelled: the loss drop over the net displacement, matched in units
    to the loss. It uses that quadratic as the continual-learning penalty. That is S3 as worded ("a curvature scale fitted
    along the optimisation path (quasi-Newton or secant style) is used to set a continual-learning penalty").
  - **For close:** SI does not calibrate a Fisher, and SEC's point is the Fisher's shape with the loss's units. SI's secant is
    on loss values per parameter, while SEC's is one gradient secant per task along the mean-parameter change. SI's strength c
    is still tuned. Benzing shows that SI's estimate in practice is dominated by a bias, not by the path integral.
  - If S3 is read REDUNDANT and S4 is REDUNDANT (three *states* here), the declaration's rule gives **KNOWN**, on F1's
    claims alone. If SI is read *close*, S3 is PARTLY REDUNDANT, and the rule gives PARTLY KNOWN (S4 and S5 REDUNDANT, S2
    PARTLY REDUNDANT). The investigator should decide this explicitly before `grade.py` runs, and should note that the
    declaration's item 2 (rescaling the Fisher by s_j) is S2, where F1 found no *states*.
- **S1: EWC-LoRA read *contradicts*.** With it, S1 grades MIXED rather than the forecast REDUNDANT.
  - It reports one λ (10^7) as best "across all datasets" without dataset-specific tuning, in EWC continual learning. That
    negates "λ must be tuned per dataset" within scope.
  - Its regime is LoRA on a pre-trained ViT, and λ = 10^7 was chosen once by a sweep. Its own third quote agrees with S1's
    mechanism (the Fisher estimator moves the needed λ).
  - A reviewer who reads S1's scope as "training from scratch" would grade it *bears*, and S1 REDUNDANT.
- **S1 states for NCL is hedged** ("could be due to an inadequacy of the approximations"), and NCL gives a second cause.
  Ritter et al. and van de Ven are unhedged.
- **S4 reads the rule literally:** Laplace Redux and VCL are tuning-free and principled in continual learning, so *states*.
  Neither addresses the Fisher's scale: the evidence sets the isotropic prior, and VCL is variational. S4 as worded does not
  require that; SEC's distinctive element sits in S2 and S6.
