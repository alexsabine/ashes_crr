**SEC4's method** (DECLARATION.md, fixed before the sweep): (1) a Laplace/EWC quadratic penalty at w = 1/2 on the
task-size-weighted diagonal empirical Fisher; (2) the units calibration SEC: each task's Fisher multiplied by
s_j = c_j / ρ_j, where c_j is the secant curvature along the path travelled during the task (change of the mean clean
gradient between the first and last 10 % of steps, projected on the change of the mean parameters, over its squared length)
and ρ_j the curvature the Fisher claims along the same direction; (3) a clip of each calibrated importance at κ / (lr · w),
κ = 0.5; (4) the claim that with (1)–(3) no per-dataset tuning of λ is needed.

## A. S2: a Fisher or Laplace precision rescaled by the loss's observed curvature

### 1. K-FAC's re-scaling by the exact Fisher and its reduction-ratio damping (Martens & Grosse, 2015; venue not verified on the day)

- **Identifier:** arXiv:1503.05671, current version **v7** (8 Jun 2020; v1 19 Mar 2015, seven versions), comment "Reduction
  ratio formula corrected. Removed incorrect claim about geodesics in footnote". Fetched 2026-09-30.
- **Integrity:** PDF (v7) `pdf_1503.05671v7.pdf` sha256 da06b21e402d147a2852a3ca7c63be8b0448d5083053b595d754e9bf6439e1fe;
  abstract page sha256 2c5403bb93f7b49619c6ce6a0ca00a863e2aea36e8947270f237c4d2712a369c.

> Given an update proposal ∆produced by multiplying the negative gradient −∇h by our approximate Fisher inverse (subject to the Tikhonov technique described in the previous subsection), the second stage of our proposed damping scheme re-scales ∆according to the quadratic model M as computed with the exact F, to produce a final update δ = α∆.

> Intuitively, this second stage of our damping scheme effectively compensates for the intrinsic inaccuracy of the approximate quadratic model (based on our approximate Fisher) used to generate the initial update proposal ∆, by essentially falling back on a more accurate quadratic model based on the exact Fisher.

> Intuitively, this rule tries to make λ as small as possible (and hence the implicit trust-region as large as possible) while maintaining the property that the quadratic model M(δ) remains a good local approximation to h (in the sense that it accurately predicts the value of h(θ + δ) for the δ which gets chosen at each iteration).

- **Reading.** S2 **close**. Two one-dimensional calibrations of an approximate curvature: the approximate-Fisher step is
  re-scaled by the exact Fisher along the step, and the damping follows the reduction ratio (actual over predicted loss
  change). Both act on an optimiser's step, not on a stored precision; the first check is against the exact Fisher, not the
  loss's secant. (Claim f2:0.)

### 12. Synaptic Intelligence (Zenke, Poole & Ganguli, ICML 2017)

- **Reading.** S3 **states** (judgement call; see the end). SI fits a per-parameter curvature to what the loss did along the
  path actually travelled (loss drop over squared displacement, in the loss's units) and uses it as the continual-learning
  penalty, with c = 1 as its principled strength. It is not a rescaling of a Fisher (S2), and in practice c was tuned.

## Is SEC4's method known?

**Answer from this family: PARTLY. Every ingredient is published separately. The combination (a Fisher/Laplace penalty
multiplied by the ratio of the secant curvature along the task's path to the curvature the Fisher claims, used to make the
continual-learning weight untuned) was not found.** The four parts, against the closest quotes:

1. **The Laplace/EWC penalty at the Bayes weight.** Textbook. The Bayes weight on the empirical Fisher is the sample size
   (Huszár: "Kirkpatrick et al. [2017] introduce a task-specific hyper-parameter λA, which replaces the sample size NA").
   The multiplier's natural value is 1, and a tuned departure from it signals a mis-scaled curvature (Ritter et al.: "if it
   strongly deviates from its natural value of 1, our approximation is a poor one and over- or underestimates the uncertainty
   about the parameters").
2. **The secant calibration (S2, S3).**
   - *The mechanism* is the self-scaling of quasi-Newton methods. The approximation is multiplied by a factor built from
     "bk = s⊤ k Bksk y⊤ k sk", the ratio of the approximation's curvature along the step to the secant curvature ("The
     parameter τk controls the Self-Scaled variant (τk = 1 means no scaling)"). SEC's s_j = c_j/ρ_j is this factor with B
     the task's Fisher. The rescaled Fisher then satisfies the weak secant condition along the task's mean displacement
     (Apollo: "a parameter-wise version of the weak secant condition"). Its secant is from averaged gradients, as in
     SGD-BB ("SGD-BB takes the average of the stochastic gradients in one epoch as an estimation of the full gradient").
   - *A Fisher calibrated by a one-dimensional curvature check* is published in optimisation. K-FAC "re-scales ∆according
     to the quadratic model M as computed with the exact F", and TKFAC keeps "the certain trace relationship between the
     exact and the approximate FIM". Neither rescales against the loss's secant, and neither is a stored precision.
   - *In continual learning, the nearest objects are these:*
     - **RWalk.** It divides the loss change observed along the path by the loss change the Fisher predicts. That is the
       same observed-over-claimed ratio as SEC's, but RWalk adds the ratio to the Fisher as a second importance and
       normalises both "to be in the interval [0, 1]".
     - **CSQN.** It corrects EWC's Fisher with Hessian-vector products ("we use EWC’s Hessian estimate, namely the
       diagonal of the FIM, as the initial Hessian approximation"). The correction is additive, not along the path, and
       λ is still validated.
     - **Layers Matter.** It rescales each layer's EWC penalty by an HVP-measured Hessian eigenvalue. The factor is the
       layer's own curvature relative to the median layer, not a ratio to the Fisher's claim, and the literal schedule
       "underperforms uniform".
     - **SI.** It fits a curvature along the path, in the loss's units ("the total drop in the loss function ... the
       total net motion in parameter space"). Benzing found that SI "relies on its bias, and not on the path integral".
   - *Outside continual learning, a Laplace built from path secants that sets penalty weights untuned* is published for
     additive models. qEFS "relies on structured limited-memory secant approximations to the Hessian", and its weights "can
     be obtained by optimizing the Laplace-approximate Bayesian marginal likelihood".
3. **The stability clip (S5, F3's).** Published in continual learning, in a soft form. Kutalev and Lapina derive the
   explosion "if Ωi ≥ 1 αλ" and bound the regularising step so that it "will not exceed the difference of weights even at
   any large importance of weight". SEC4's hard clip at κ/(lr·w) is a variant.
4. **No per-dataset λ (S4, S6, F3's).** Principled, validation-free weights are published for continual learning:
   - VCL: "does not have free parameters that need to be tuned on a validation set";
   - Lee et al.: the importance hyperparameters are set "without any validation".

   Principled Laplace prior precisions are published outside continual learning (Laplace Redux, Immer et al.). Immer et al.
   name continual learning as future work. The claim that a *calibrated Laplace weight* is not behind a tuned λ across many
   datasets was not found. The nearest statements go the other way: λ = 1 on a K-FAC Laplace "performed poorly" (NCL), and
   "λFisher is unknown and must be tuned regardless" (Squisher).

**The closest source to SEC4's secant calibration.**
- **By mechanism:** the self-scaled quasi-Newton family (source 3). Its factor is the ratio SEC computes, applied to a
  quasi-Newton matrix rather than a Fisher, in an optimiser.
- **In continual learning:** RWalk (source 9). It computes the observed-over-Fisher-predicted loss change along the path,
  per parameter, but uses the ratio additively and normalises away the scale.
- **Rescaling a continual-learning Fisher penalty by a measured loss curvature:** Layers Matter (source 8, by HVP, per
  layer, at the checkpoint).

"Not found" is never "novel". This sweep ran for one day, by keyword. It covered:

- the OpenReview term search;
- web search.

It did not cover:
- the CVF, NeurIPS or PMLR title lists;
- the optimisation journals (SIAM, Math. Programming, ACM TOMS), where a secant-scaled Gauss-Newton or Fisher model may
  well exist (NL2SOL's "sizing" was not reached);

## Judgement calls, for the investigator's review

- **S3: Synaptic Intelligence read states.** This is the call that decides whether S3 is REDUNDANT or PARTLY REDUNDANT.
  - **For states:** SI fits, per parameter, a curvature to what the loss did along the path actually travelled during the
    task (loss drop over squared net displacement, "the same units as the loss"). It uses that curvature as the
    continual-learning penalty, with c = 1 as its principled strength. That is S3 as worded.
  - **For close:** SI's fit is a loss-value fit per coordinate, not a gradient secant along one direction. It builds the
    importance rather than scaling a Fisher. Its strength c was still tuned (0.1). And Benzing shows that as implemented it
    measures gradient noise, not the path integral.
  - A reviewer who requires S3 to mean "a secant scale applied to a Fisher/Laplace penalty" would read it close, and S3
    would then have no states.
  - Either way, SEC's step that is **not** found is the one S2 names: multiplying the Fisher by the ratio of the secant to
    the Fisher's own claim.
- **S2: no states, and three near misses.**
  - **Layers Matter** rescales a continual-learning Fisher penalty by an HVP-measured loss curvature, which fits S2's
    wording in part. It was read close because its factor is the curvature itself (relative to the median layer), not the
    ratio to the Fisher's claim, and c is still swept. A reader who weighs wording over mechanism could read it states.
  - **RWalk** has the ratio (observed over Fisher-predicted loss change along the path) in continual learning, but uses it
    additively and normalised.
  - **The self-scaled quasi-Newton family** has the exact factor but not a Fisher, and not continual learning.
  - The combination of the three (the self-scaling factor, applied to a Fisher, in a continual-learning penalty) was not
    found.
