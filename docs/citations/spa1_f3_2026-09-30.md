## A. S5: bounding the penalty's curvature by the step size

### 1. AR1, Maltoni & Lomonaco (arXiv 2018; Neural Networks 2019): the nearest match to SEC4's stability clip

- **Identifier:** arXiv:1806.08568, current version **v3** (22 Jan 2019; v1 22 Jun 2018, v2 28 Aug 2018), comment "26 pages,
  13 figures; v3: major revision (e.g. added Sec. 4.4), several typos and minor mistakes corrected". The abstract page carries
  no journal reference. A Neural Networks (2019) version is listed on ScienceDirect (found by web search, not fetched).
  Fetched 2026-09-30. Davide Maltoni, Vincenzo Lomonaco.
- **Integrity:** PDF (v3) `pdf_1806.08568v3.pdf` sha256 5236fbcadb047c2664abe26e931b2531ad16444180ae8fcb8c41101a5841518b;
  abstract page sha256 38597ec5dfc43a1903a7a1418afe4b4f47389cb2a410b46213f43c7075acbc40.
- **Mechanism** (agent's reading of §2.2):
  - EWC with a single consolidated Fisher; the penalty's gradient step is written out;
  - the product η·λ·F_k > 1 is named as the overshoot condition;
  - the Fisher is averaged over batches and clipped at maxF;
  - λ is then bounded by 1/(η·maxF), so that η·λ·F̂_k ≤ 1 for every coordinate.

> It is worth noting that in option 2, the Fk values can only increase as new batches are processed, potentially leading to divergence for large λ.

> In the above equation if, for some k, the product η · λ · Fk is greater than 1, the weight correction toward θ∗ k is excessive and we overshoot the desired value.

> where clip sets the matrix values exceeding maxF to the constant maxF .

> Given maxF and η we can easily determine the maximum value for λ as 1/(η · maxF ).

> Fk values are averaged and clipped to 0.001 thus allowing to work with higher λ and better control forgetting.

- **Reading.** S5 **states**. This is a regularisation-based continual learner whose importance is clipped so that the
  penalty's explicit step stays below the overshoot edge (lr·λ·F ≤ 1), to prevent divergence.
  - **SEC4's clip** (κ/(lr·w), κ = 0.5) is the same inequality at half the Euler edge 2. SEC4 fixes lr and w and derives the
    clip.
  - **AR1** fixes maxF (0.001) and derives the largest λ. It also averages rather than sums the Fishers.

  (Claim f3:0.) Neither F1 nor F2 found this source.

### 2. Stabilizing EWC, Kutalev & Lapina (arXiv 2021)

> Further, if Ωi ≥ 1 αλ, then after the optimization step the weight wi will not shift towards w∗ i but will jump over it.

> In the case Ωi ≥ 2 αλ, the weight wi will not only jump over the value of w∗ i , but the distance between wi and w∗ i will increase by a factor of (αλΩi −1).

## Is SEC4's method known?

SEC4's method has four parts (DECLARATION.md). They are read here one at a time, with the closest quotes.

**Part 1: the Laplace penalty at w = 1/2 on the task-size-weighted diagonal empirical Fisher.** This is textbook.
- Huszár names the Bayes strength as the task size, and says EWC replaced it with a tuned λ:

  > In order to have better control over the approximation, Kirkpatrick et al. [2017] introduce a task-specific hyper-parameter λA, which replaces the sample size NA.

- Zhao et al. (ICML 2024) derive the task-size-weighted sum of Hessians as the optimal, tuning-free penalty in continual
  linear regression:

  > Tasks with larger sample size will be allocated with larger weights in the optimal regularization matrix, which is reasonable since they contain more information about w∗.

**Part 2: the units calibration s_j = c_j/ρ_j.** This is the secant curvature along the path travelled, divided by the
Fisher's claimed curvature along the same direction.
- **Not found in this family's sweep** in the form SEC uses: a scalar per task that multiplies the Fisher, fitted as the ratio
  of an observed secant curvature to the Fisher's claim along the path.
- The closest forms, in order:
  1. **RWalk** (2018) measures, along the path, the observed loss change against the Fisher's predicted change. It adds this
     to the Fisher and discards the scale:

     > we define parameter importance as the ratio of the change in the loss to its influence in DKL(pθ(t)∥pθ(t+1)).

  2. **SI** (2017) fits a path secant-type curvature per coordinate and uses it *instead of* the Fisher:

     > The quadratic surrogate loss (green) is chosen to precisely match 3 aspects of the descent dynamics on the original loss function: the total drop in the loss function L(θ(0)) −L(θ(T)), the total net motion in parameter space θ(0) −θ(T), and achieving a minimum at the endpoint θ(T).

  3. **CSQN** (2025) corrects the diagonal Fisher with HVP-based quasi-Newton pairs sampled around the optimum.
  4. **Layers Matter** (2026) reweights EWC per layer by HVP-measured curvature, up to a swept global constant.
- **The gap SEC targets is named** by Ritter et al.: the natural weight 1 fails because the curvature approximation is
  mis-scaled.

  > if it strongly deviates from its natural value of 1, our approximation is a poor one and over- or underestimates the uncertainty about the parameters.

**Part 3: the stability clip at κ/(lr·w), κ = 0.5.** This is **published for regularisation-based continual learning**.
- AR1 (arXiv 2018, v3 2019) names the condition η·λ·F_k > 1, clips F at maxF, and bounds λ by 1/(η·maxF):

  > In the above equation if, for some k, the product η · λ · Fk is greater than 1, the weight correction toward θ∗ k is excessive and we overshoot the desired value.

  > Given maxF and η we can easily determine the maximum value for λ as 1/(η · maxF ).

- Kutalev & Lapina (2021) derive the edge at 2/(αλ) and soft-bound the importance:

  > In the case Ωi ≥ 2 αλ, the weight wi will not only jump over the value of w∗ i , but the distance between wi and w∗ i will increase by a factor of (αλΩi −1).

- SEC4's κ = 0.5 against AR1's bound of 1 (and Kutalev's saturation) is a choice of constant within a published safeguard.

**Part 4: the claim that no per-dataset tuning of λ is needed (not behind a tuned λ).**
- Principled, untuned strengths are published for continual learning:
  - Laplace Redux: marginal likelihood for the prior, Bayes weight 1 on past Hessians;
  - VCL: no free parameters;
  - Zhao et al.: the derived optimum in linear regression.
- None of them reports matching a tuned λ across many datasets.
- The nearest reports:
  - MAS uses λ = 1 untuned, but for MAS;
  - VCL finds λ near 1 on one benchmark.
- Evidence against the Bayes weight: Ritter (λ = 3), GVCL (10–1000), van de Ven (orders of magnitude with the Fisher's
  computation).
- **S6 as stated was not found.**

"Not found" is never "novel". This sweep ran over one day and by keyword, and it covered arXiv search, OpenReview term search
and web search. It did not cover:
- conference title lists (CVF, NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR proceedings pages);
- code;
- non-English literature;
- OpenReview PDFs (403).

The AR1 clip was found only because a web search surfaced it: its abstract does not name the clip. Other safeguards stated
only in methods sections may exist unseen.
