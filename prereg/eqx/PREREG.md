## Hypothesis under test (from theory/CRR.md [H-EQ]; CLAUDE.md §6)
The equanimity rule weights the replayed-past gradient so that settled past
and present exert equal pull: `g = g_present + w·g_past`,
`w = Ω·‖ḡ_present‖/‖ḡ_past‖`, Ω = 1. It "counts as a result only if it beats
experience replay with the two losses summed (ER-sum) and the best fixed w,
on data not used to choose the estimator."

The archive (`archive/cl_ledger_2026-09-14`, recomputed in
`audit/recompute_2026-09-14.txt`) showed on KMNIST r = 0.2 that fixed w = 1
is 1.03 pt behind the rule (per seed +1.00, +0.42, +1.68) — a verdict at
the boundary; the cross-dataset fixed-w sweep was lost. This study repeats
the comparison on three datasets never used in CRR work.

## Gate (R4)

## Instrument parameters (all named; frozen in `runs/eqx/frozen/eqx_score.py`)
MLP d-256-K ReLU, SGD lr 0.05, batch 10, reservoir buffer 500, replay
fraction r = 0.2 (2 replay samples per step), seeds 0–4.
Adaptive estimator: `ratio=ema, smooth=0.98, metric=fisher (diag, running
EMA of (batch-mean gradient)²·batch²), WCAP=50`. **R3 disclosure:** this
estimator was chosen after seeing KMNIST seed 0 on 2026-09-14 (archive
LEDGER §3); it is used here on a different calendar day on datasets it has
never touched, unchanged.
Ω grid {0.5, 0.71, 1, 1.41, 2}. Fixed-w grid {0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 4}. Euclid
metric at Ω = 1 only. ER at r = 0.2 (concatenated-batch mean; equals fixed
w = r up to an lr factor) and r = 1.0 (compute reference).
Constants swept in the sensitivity table: none in this study (single frozen
configuration; the archive already swept lr, batch and smoothing on KMNIST).
A verdict that flips between `fisher` and `euclid` at Ω = 1 is reported as
fragile.

## Baselines (R7)
ER-sum ≡ fixed w = 1 (the two batch means summed) — the standard additive
replay of DER/Mammoth; the fixed-w grid; ER(r). Published methods needing a
framework (ER-ACE, DER++, CLS-ER, MEGA-I) are **not** in this study: no
PyTorch in the environment; so EQ-3 of CLAUDE.md §6 ("against real
baselines") is out of scope here and stays open.

## Hypotheses (scored once by `eqx_score.py score`, per dataset, per seed)
Resolvable step: seed SD on the archive's runs is ≈ 1 pt; thresholds are 1.0 pt.

- **EQX-1 (reduction; the important one).** Δ₁ = acc(EQ Ω=1) − max over
  {fixed w ∈ grid, ER(0.2)} of acc, seed-mean. If Δ₁ < 1.0 on ≥ 2 of 3
  datasets the rule **reduces to a constant**, the ledger row reads
  "Ω = 1 ≡ fixed replay weight (ER-sum family)", and it stops being called
  adaptive. Per-seed Δ₁ and the count of seeds with Δ₁ ≥ 1.0 are reported.
- **EQX-2 (theory value).** Ω = 1 is the best grid point AND both neighbours
  (0.71, 1.41) trail by > 1.0, on ≥ 2 of 3 datasets. FAIL otherwise.
- **EQX-3 (beats ER-sum).** acc(EQ Ω=1) − acc(fixed w=1) ≥ 1.0 seed-mean and
  positive in ≥ 4 of 5 seeds, on ≥ 2 of 3 datasets. FAIL otherwise.
- **EQX-4 (metric).** |acc(fisher) − acc(euclid)| at Ω = 1 < 1.0 → "the
  Fisher metric is immaterial"; reported per dataset.
- **EQX-5 (compute claim).** acc(EQ 0.2) − acc(ER 1.0), report only.

Outcomes named in advance: EQX-1 triggers ∧ EQX-3 fails → Ω = 1 is ER-sum
with a replay weight near 1; the rule is retired as "adaptive". EQX-1 does
not trigger ∧ EQX-3 passes → adaptivity is load-bearing on these streams and
EQ-3 (framework baselines) becomes the next study. Any other combination is
written as it falls; no reinterpretation.
